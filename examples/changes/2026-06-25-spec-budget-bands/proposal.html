Ground the spec-budget defaults and RAG bands in research

Formalise the spec-budget as a contract, move the amber band from an aggressive 70% to the industry-standard 80%, count authored words only, and delete a citation we made up.

Status Proposed Change ID 2026-06-25-spec-budget-bands Spec 000-spectastic (meta-spec) · cross-spec to 011-core-spec Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 2026-06-25 Targets 2 requirements across 2 specs (1 added, 1 modified) Read time

The <spec-budget> gauge ships with a 1,500-word default and an amber band at 70%, but neither is owned by a requirement — and the gauge's own hint cites "Larson: specs over 1,500 words must be split," a claim Larson never made. Meanwhile 14 of 21 specs (67%) already breach the budget, so the 70% nudge fires constantly and is overridden as routine — textbook alert fatigue. This change ADDs REQ-FORMAT-004 to formalise the sizing contract (defaults, bands, and an authored-words count that excludes the auto-built conformance index), MODIFIEs 011-core-spec FR-008 to move the splitting nudge to the 80% amber band and point at the new owner, and removes the fabricated Larson citation. Grounded in the research below; the implementation is deferred to apply.

Ground the spec-budget defaults and RAG bands in research

Proposed

1 · Intent

Two numbers in the sizing discipline are unowned and one is invented. The 1,500-word default lives only in templates/spec.html and assets/spec.js — no requirement governs it — and the gauge's amber hint attributes it to Will Larson: "Larson: specs over 1,500 words must be split." He says no such thing. His actual guidance is about brevity, not a word count: "most people don't read long document[s]. If you write something five or six pages long, readers will start dropping off" (lethain.com), recommending docs be "one to two pages long." A made-up citation in shipping copy is exactly what the project's voice rule forbids.

The 70% amber band is owned — by 011-core-spec FR-008 — but it is too aggressive. RAG thresholds are organisation-defined conventions, not a standard (PRINCE2: RAG "relies on high-level definitions rather than precise figures"); the industry warns at 80%, not 70% (AWS CloudWatch: "Set the threshold to 80%… so that action can be taken before it reaches full capacity"); and warning earlier backfires — alert-fatigue research finds people "override the majority of… warnings, even 'critical' alerts" when volume is high (AHRQ PSNet). The proof is in our own corpus: 67% of specs already exceed the budget, so the 70% nudge is the boy who cried wolf. A credible 80% threshold gets heeded; an ignored 70% one does not.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • ADD REQ-FORMAT-004 to the meta-spec: the sizing-budget contract (defaults, RAG bands, authored-words count).
  • MODIFY 011-core-spec FR-008: move the splitting nudge to the 80% amber band; point at REQ-FORMAT-004 rather than hard-coding the percentage.
  • Remove the fabricated "Larson: specs over 1,500 words must be split" citation (gauge hint + CLAUDE.md lineage).
  • Count authored words only — exclude the auto-built <spec-conformance> index from the budget's Words row.

Out of scope

  • Executing the implementation (editing assets/spec.js, templates, README, CLAUDE.md) — that folds into the target's tasks on /spectastic.apply and is drained by /spectastic.implement.
  • Raising the 1,500-word default itself — the number stays; only its basis (authored words, correct rationale) and its warn band change. Per-spec overrides (e.g. 2,500 for tool specs) remain available.
  • Changing the requirement-count (20) or read-time (12 min) defaults, or the red band (over-budget, >100%).

3 · Approach

Give the sizing budget a single owner. REQ-FORMAT-004 joins the format family (alongside REQ-FORMAT-003, which already governs file size) and states the contract: the default budget, the green/amber/red bands at ≤80 / 80–100 / >100%, and that the Words row measures authored prose — the auto-built conformance index is generated, not written, so counting it (as the gauge does today) inflates every spec by ~430 words and is part of why 67% breach. Read-time keeps counting the whole document (you do read the index); only the budget's Words row changes.

011-core-spec FR-008 then stops hard-coding "70%" and instead fires its splitting nudge when the draft enters the amber band per REQ-FORMAT-004 — one source of truth, so the verb and the gauge can never disagree. This spans two specs (the meta-spec owns the contract; 011 owns the verb behaviour), so it is a deliberate cross-spec change; the adversarial pass (§5) covers the drift that introduces.

4 · Deltas

One <spec-delta> per requirement touched. target= links to the live spec; op= is one of added | modified | removed | renamed.

A spec.html artifact MUST carry a <spec-budget> size gauge. The gauge's Words row MUST count authored prose only — the auto-built <spec-conformance> index is excluded, since it is generated rather than written. The gauge MUST band each metric against its declared budget as green ≤ 80%, amber > 80–100%, red > 100%, taking the worst row as the overall band. The default budget for a feature spec SHOULD be 1,500 words / 20 requirements / 12-minute read; tool-shape specs SHOULD declare a higher budget (e.g. 2,500 words), and system-of-record artifacts (this meta-spec, principles.html) are exempt. The amber band is advisory — a nudge to consider slicing, never a hard gate.

Rationale

The budget is small-batch pressure made visible, but its numbers were unowned and partly invented. 80% amber is the industry-standard "approaching limit" warn point (AWS CloudWatch sets capacity alarms at 80%; queueing theory puts the knee there) and avoids the alert fatigue a 70% band caused — 67% of our specs breached it, so it was overridden as routine (AHRQ on warnings that fire too often). RAG cut points are conventions, not canon (PRINCE2), so we pick the evidenced one. Authored-words stops the ~430-word conformance index from counting against the author. The brevity principle is real and is Larson's ("most people don't read long documents") — but the "1,500 words" figure was never his, so the gauge stops attributing it to him.

What changes: the 011-core-spec FR-008 splitting nudge moves from a hard-coded 70% to the amber band defined by REQ-FORMAT-004 (≥80%), and tracks authored words. (Cross-spec delta.)

The verb MUST track the in-progress draft's authored word count against the spec's declared <spec-budget>. When the draft enters the amber band (≥ 80% of budget, per REQ-FORMAT-004), the verb MUST surface a splitting nudge via ctx.ai.ask<T>() with options to (a) move material to <spec-out-of-scope> with defer-to="TBD-<topic>", (b) scaffold a sibling spec, or (c) acknowledge and proceed.

5 · Risk register

The adversarial pass fired (a must-tier requirement is touched and the change spans two specs). Three findings; each MUST reach accepted, mitigated, or rejected before apply.

Moving amber to 80% and excluding the conformance index both enlarge the green zone — the splitting nudge now fires much later, so specs could bloat past the point where slicing is cheap.

"green ≤ 80%… the Words row MUST count authored prose only" — the combined effect roughly doubles the headroom before amber.

Accepted by design. Size pressure is multi-signal — the smallest-demoable prompt, INVEST-Small, and <spec-out-of-scope> all push back independently of the gauge. The old 70%/whole-doc band was breached by 67% of specs, i.e. ignored; a credible 80%/authored band is one a human will actually heed. We are trading a loud-but-ignored signal for a quieter-but-trusted one.

The authored-words count is harder to compute reliably than a whole-document split, and a miscount (index not yet built, nested tags, the gauge counting itself) would silently mislead.

"the auto-built <spec-conformance> index is excluded" — requires subtracting a generated subtree from the word count.

Mitigable. assets/spec.js builds the conformance index in the same render pass, so the exclusion is a single subtree subtraction; read-time keeps the whole-document count, so the two are intentionally distinct. The apply task list adds a structural assertion that authored-wc < whole-doc-wc whenever an index is present.

FR-008 (011) and REQ-FORMAT-004 (meta-spec) now both name the 80% band; a future edit to one but not the other lets the verb and the gauge silently diverge.

"enters the amber band (≥ 80% of budget, per REQ-FORMAT-004)" — the percentage is duplicated across two specs.

Mitigated two ways. Structurally now: REQ-FORMAT-004 is the single source of truth and FR-008's body says "per REQ-FORMAT-004", so the "≥ 80%" parenthetical is a reader convenience, not a second definition. And enforced: §6 carries a committed task to add a schema rule asserting the assets/spec.js band matches REQ-FORMAT-004 — the same drift-guard shape as the existing verify-view-stale rule. The coupling cannot silently diverge once that rule lands.
