This register captures the operational doctrine that came out of the 16 Jun 2026 over-budget slicing audit. It records the parent/child carve-out recipe in a stable place (so the adversarial-pass mitigations on 2026-06-16-carve-out-local-fallback point at something real), names the three slice categories with their respective lifecycle expectations, freezes the audit ratios before and after the day's trim passes, and tracks the four follow-ups owed by the day's work. New <spec-parent>-based carve-outs SHOULD follow this register's recipe; deviations SHOULD be deliberate and documented.

1 · The four-step parent/child carve-out recipe

Codified from the R-001 mitigation of 2026-06-16-carve-out-local-fallback. The slicing recipe inherently requires the child spec to exist before the parent's REMOVE/MODIFY deltas can cite migration targets — a REMOVE whose .migration-block points to 005#FR-001 demands that specs/005/spec.html#FR-001 resolve. Hand-waving past this with "it'll exist after apply" publishes a proposal whose migration links 404 in the interim.

  1. Author the child spec at status="draft". Place at specs/<child-id>/spec.html. Include <spec-parent specid="<parent-id>"> in the header — the rule spec-parent-well-formed enforces the format.
  2. Author the parent's proposal with REMOVE / MODIFY deltas referencing the child. Each REMOVE carries a substantive .migration-block pointing at the child's matching requirement ID. The no-broken-defer-to rule fires in cross-file validation if the target child isn't in the set.
  3. The child carries an explicit lineage statement in its TL;DR or changelog, naming the parent's pending proposal. Future readers can trace which proposal made this slice official.
  4. If the parent's proposal withdraws (via /spectastic.apply --withdraw per REQ-CHANGE-005), the pre-authored child is withdrawn alongside. The author retains the right to delete or de-spec the orphaned child — the child existed conditionally on the parent's apply landing.

This is a documented sequencing pattern, not a gate inversion. The propose → apply gate operates on the parent's deltas; the child's existence on disk is a precondition that the recipe explicitly requires.

2 · Slice categories

Three categories of artifact relate differently to the over-budget signal. The slicing recipe applies cleanly only to the first.

Vertical slice of real work
The default case. A spec exceeds budget because it contains two or more independently-demoable user stories. The carve-out moves one to a sibling slice; both parent and child carry real implementation work, real INVEST-V, real lifecycle. The recipe in §1 applies as written.
Deliberate worked-example slice
The exception the carve-out proposal R-003 anticipated. The child is small (1 FR + 1 SC at the lower bound), has no implementation work, and exists primarily to demonstrate the recipe to future authors. The slice's user-facing INVEST-V is acknowledged-small; its project-facing value is the demonstration itself. 005-publish-local-fallback is the canonical example. This category MAY stay at Draft indefinitely. If it sits stale for an extended period with no real implementation work surfacing, it is a candidate for being merged back into the parent via /spectastic.apply --withdraw on a future proposal — the withdrawal path is the safety valve. New worked-example slices SHOULD self-flag in their TL;DR and link to this register.
System-of-record artifact (exempt)
The meta-spec (spectastic-spec.html), the principles (principles.html), and similarly-positioned authoritative documents. These stay all-inclusive over budget rather than being sliced. Slicing them by topic group (e.g. carving REQ-CHANGE-* out of the meta-spec into a sibling) trades one search hit for two and weakens the "open one file, see the whole contract" property. When over budget, raise the <spec-budget words="…"> attribute intentionally and add a <spec-note> recording the exemption (as the meta-spec did on 16 Jun 2026, raising 3,000 → 5,000).

  • First parent/child slice pair: 004-npm-publish-workflow ↔ 005-publish-local-fallback. Vocabulary that had existed since 2026-06-12 (<spec-parent specid>, <spec-out-of-scope defer-to>, REMOVE-with-migration deltas) finally has a witness.
  • Two new validator rules: no-broken-defer-to (cross-file, lexical + existence-with-doc-set-context gate) and spec-parent-well-formed (per-file, specid format check). Together they form the practical lower bound on parent/child slicing consistency. The cross-file rule caught a real defect on its first run: 001-cli's stale defer-to="002-validate" pointing at the renamed 002-validate-cli.
  • System-of-record convention: documented in §2 above and persisted to memory.
  • Trim passes: 002, 003, 004 all under 100% of budget. The innerText-skips-collapsed-<details> behaviour of the live gauge means wrapping is an honest reduction, not visual sleight of hand — the content remains accessible.

5 · Deferred follow-ups

Four enhancements were identified during the audit + adversarial pass but not landed. Each is a real improvement; none is blocking.

New delta op carved for lossless requirement migration
Today, moving a requirement from parent to child is modeled as op="removed" in the parent with a .migration-block citing the child. That works but is lossy in the conformance index: the requirement is gone from the parent even though it's still live. A new op="carved" with an explicit target-spec= attribute would model the migration first-class. Worth a propose against the meta-spec when the next carve-out arrives.
Budget-block gate at apply
Refuse /spectastic.apply when the post-apply parent spec would exceed 100% of budget, unless the proposal includes a <spec-out-of-scope> deferral (i.e., the proposal is itself the slice). Mirrors the existing <spec-risk status="identified"> apply gate. Would have caught 004's post-carve over-budget state automatically and forced the trim to land in the same proposal.
Cross-file parent/child reciprocity rule — landed in v0.1.0-pre.7
The cross-file companion to spec-parent-well-formed — "if <spec-parent specid="X"> exists in this artifact, the parent X must contain a reciprocal defer-to=<this-id>" — shipped on 16 Jun 2026 as parent-child-reciprocity. The test harness was extended in the same change to support multi-file directory fixtures (fixtures/<rule>/positive/**/*.html as an alternative to single-file positive.html); future cross-file rules whose positive case needs two or more documents can reuse the pattern. Single-direction only (child → parent); the reverse would over-fire on legitimate non-parent/child defer-tos.
Slicing-aware /spectastic.split verb
CLAUDE.md "Things deferred" explicitly rules this out on the grounds that "the workflow does this with existing verbs." The 16 Jun 2026 carve-out validated that judgement — propose + spec + apply did the job. Status: actively deferred, not a follow-up to land. Listed here so future audits don't re-propose it.
