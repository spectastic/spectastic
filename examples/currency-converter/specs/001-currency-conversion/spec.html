Currency conversion

Enter an amount, pick a pair, read a rate you can tell the age of.

Status Accepted Spec ID 001-currency-conversion Owner example-co/mobile Reviewers example-co/design · example-co/platform Created 28 Jul 2026 Principlesv1.2.0 Smallest demoableOne hard-coded pair, one rate, one staleness label. Read time

The converter screen: an amount, a currency pair, and the resulting figure with the age of the rate it used. For people who convert a handful of times a trip and care more about knowing a number is four hours old than about it arriving instantly. The outcome that matters is that a stale or missing rate is never mistaken for a current one.

1 · Context

The app currently shows a single home rate fetched at launch and never refreshed, with no indication of when it was fetched. Support tickets that mention a "wrong" rate are almost never wrong rates; they are correct rates from several hours earlier, presented as though they were current. That is the defect P-1 exists to prevent, and the current screen cannot honour it because it has nowhere to put the age.

The users are travellers converting a handful of times a day, often on poor connections, and occasionally with no connection at all. They are not traders. Latency matters far less to them than knowing what they are looking at.

2 · User scenarios & testing

Each story MUST be independently testable: if you implemented only US1, you would still have a viable MVP.

US1 · Convert an amount P1

As a traveller, I want to enter an amount and see it in another currency so that I can tell whether a price is reasonable.

Acceptance: Entering an amount with a valid pair renders the converted figure and the rate's age. The figure updates when either currency changes. With no amount entered, the screen shows the empty state rather than a zero.

US2 · Judge whether the rate is current P1

As a traveller, I want to see how old the rate is so that I can decide whether to trust it before I hand over money.

Acceptance: A rate older than the freshness window renders the staleness treatment, which carries text as well as colour. A rate inside the window carries no badge. The age is derived from when the rate was observed upstream, not from when the app received it.

US3 · Keep working when the network does not P2

As a traveller on a bad connection, I want to still see the last known rate so that the screen is useful rather than an error.

Acceptance: With no connectivity the screen shows the last cached rate with its age and an offline indication; it does not show an error dialog and does not clear the figure. With nothing cached it shows the cold-start state.

Edge cases

  • A pair the service does not quote is indistinguishable to the user from a currency code that does not exist, though the contract distinguishes them.
  • A rate-limited response carries a retry time, so the state can say when rather than whether.
  • An expired API key is unreachable by user action and unfixable by the user; the state still has to render something.

3 · Requirements

Conformance keywords (MUST, SHOULD, MAY) follow RFC 2119.

Functional

The screen MUST render a converted figure for a valid amount and pair, and MUST render the age of the rate it used alongside it.

Rationale

The figure without its age is the defect this spec exists to remove; the two are one piece of information, not two.

A rate whose observation time is older than the freshness window MUST render the staleness treatment, and that treatment MUST be legible without colour.

Every documented error response of the rates operation MUST map to a designed screen state, and the mapping MUST be recorded where a reviewer can read it. Where several responses share one state, the collapse MUST be recorded rather than implied.

Rationale

P-4 binds forward from principles v1.2.0 and does not reach this spec, which predates it. The requirement is carried voluntarily because the state list was going to be written anyway and an unrecorded collapse is indistinguishable from an omission.

States that no response implies — no amount entered, in flight, offline, nothing cached — MUST be designed explicitly. The contract-derived list is a floor.

With no connectivity the screen SHOULD retain the last known figure and its age rather than clearing it.

The screen MAY offer a manual refresh. It MAY not be the only way to obtain a current rate.

Non-functional

The converted figure MUST render within 100 ms of a keystroke using the cached rate, independent of any network call in flight.

The layout MUST hold at the largest Dynamic Type accessibility size without clipping or truncating the figure, and every interactive element MUST meet the 44 pt tap-target floor.

No analytics event MUST be emitted until the consent decision is answered, and no payload MUST contain an amount or any personal data.

Out of scope (deferred to other slices)

  • A chart of a pair over time. This slice shows one rate at one moment.
  • A downloadable rate pack for travel with no connectivity at all, as distinct from intermittent connectivity.
  • Converting one amount into several currencies at once.

4 · Data model

Two entities, both owned by this feature. The wire shape and the persisted shape are the design's business.

Entities

Rate
The exchange ratio between an ordered pair of currencies at a stated moment. Strictly positive; never zero and never negative. Carries the moment it was observed upstream, which is not the moment it was received and not the moment it was displayed. A Rate with no observation time is not a Rate.
Conversion
An amount, a pair, and the Rate applied to it. Exact to the quote currency's minor unit. A Conversion always names the Rate it used, so a figure on screen can always answer how old it is.

The freshness window is a property of the screen, not of a Rate — the same Rate is fresh to a traveller and ancient to a trader. It belongs in the design, and it is the one number that decides whether FR-002 fires.

5 · Success criteria

Technology-agnostic, measurable outcomes. These are how we know it worked.

In usability testing, ≥ 90% of participants shown a deliberately stale rate correctly identify it as not current within 5 seconds, without being prompted to look for the age.

All ten screen states are reachable in the test suite, and the count of states with no test is 0.

Support tickets attributing a wrong figure to a stale rate fall to ≤ 1 per month, from a baseline of 14.

6 · Assumptions

The rates service keeps its promise that asOf is the upstream observation time rather than the time of serving. Nothing in the app can verify this, and every staleness claim rests on it.

Travellers convert tens of times a day, not thousands, so a cache measured in minutes is adequate and the rate-limit state is rare rather than routine.

7 · Open questions

None outstanding. The freshness window, the many-to-one state collapse and the declined tvOS variant were each anchored during the interview and are recorded in the design. Analytics consent is tracked as a project-level decision rather than an open question on this spec — the tracking plan is written and explicitly unwired, per NFR-003.
