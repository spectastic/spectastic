Add P-6 — Draft is loose, ratified is locked

Applied

1 · Intent

Across the artifact-generating kernel verbs — principles, spec, plan, tasks, and prospectively any future verb that emits a single-file HTML artifact — the same defect surfaced four times in succession. The verbs treat the destination's existence as the gate for refuse / overwrite / re-enter behaviour, ignoring the destination's <spec-status>. Two failure shapes emerged: too-rigid (008's refuse blocks the natural Draft authoring loop; 009 matches) and too-loose (011 silently overwrites Draft specs; 012 silently sharpens Accepted plans, bypassing the change-management surface).

Both shapes share a root cause: the project has never named the asymmetry between Draft and past-Draft states as a first-class principle. Each verb's authors made local judgement calls that drifted in different directions. The cleanest fix is to elevate the asymmetry to P-6 and let it cascade: each verb-specific proposal becomes a one-line FR that cites P-6 as the justification rather than re-deriving it. Future verbs inherit the discipline by reference.

3 · Approach

Single ADD delta. P-6 follows the shape of P-1..P-5: short heading + 2–3 sentence body explaining the constraint + the consequence. The principle defines two terms (Draft, past-Draft) by reference to REQ-LIFECYCLE-002's named status values, names what authoring tools must accept against each, and points at the change-management surface (/spectastic.propose → /spectastic.apply) as the canonical path for past-Draft mutations.

The principle is phrased as a positive assertion of authoring tool behaviour, not a prohibition. It tells future verb implementations what to do ("accept in-place edits against Draft artifacts without ceremony"; "route past-Draft mutations through the change-management surface") rather than what to forbid. Matches the voice of P-1 through P-5.

4 · Deltas

What's added: a new project principle codifying the Draft-vs-past-Draft asymmetry. Lands in principles.html's Core principles section, after P-5.

P-6 · Draft is loose, ratified is locked

An artifact in <spec-status value="draft"> carries no consumer commitments — it's an in-progress authoring artifact. Authoring tools (kernel verbs, slash commands, CLI subcommands) MUST accept in-place edits against Draft artifacts without invoking the change-management surface (/spectastic.propose → /spectastic.apply). Minimal authoring discipline still applies — preserve stable IDs across edits (per P-3) and append a one-line <spec-changelog> entry per edit. "Without ceremony" scopes to the change-management cycle, not to the changelog/ID-preservation hygiene.

An artifact in a terminal-or-onward state (Accepted, Superseded, Deprecated; per REQ-LIFECYCLE-002) carries consumer commitments — readers have anchored their understanding against its content. Mutations to these states MUST route through the change-management surface so the lifecycle audit trail records the intent + the deltas. Authoring tools MUST NOT silently mutate terminal-state artifacts.

Reversible-to-Draft states (In Review; Blocked when the originating state was Draft) sit between. They carry interim reader expectations (sign-off in flight; resolution pending) but REQ-LIFECYCLE-004's FSM explicitly sanctions returning them to Draft via the named In Review → Draft (author-initiated revision) and Blocked → originating-state transitions. The lawful path for revising a reversible-state artifact is the FSM transition first, then the Draft-state in-place edit — not the heavier proposal / apply cycle. P-6 defers to REQ-LIFECYCLE-004 for these states; it does not duplicate or override the FSM.

The asymmetry is the discipline. Draft (and reversible-to-Draft via the FSM) is loose; Accepted-and-onward is locked behind a deliberate gesture.

5 · Risk register

Adversarial pass auto-fires per the propose-time heuristic (adding a project principle is foundationally must-tier in spirit even though principles don't carry a priority= attribute; the spec amends will all cite P-6, so a wrong P-6 would cascade widely). Findings embedded below; status starts at identified and transitions on author commitment.

"Past-Draft" lumps reversible states (In Review, Blocked) with terminal ones

Concern. P-6's body asserts that "any past-Draft state (Accepted / In Review / Superseded / Deprecated / Blocked, per REQ-LIFECYCLE-002) carries consumer commitments." But REQ-LIFECYCLE-004 explicitly allows In Review → Draft (author-initiated revision) and Blocked → originating-state (which can be Draft). Treating those reversible states as locked-behind-change-management contradicts the FSM, and the asymmetry P-6 names is really Draft-or-reversible-to-Draft vs Accepted-and-onward — not Draft vs everything else.

30-day frame. Within 30 days a sibling verb proposal cites P-6 to refuse in-place edits against an In-Review spec the author intends to bounce back to Draft; the fix is a P-6 errata, which is harder than amending an FR because principles are non-negotiable per principles.html's header.

Author draft response. Accepted as written; P-6's body amended in place. The principle now distinguishes three categories explicitly: (1) Draft — loose, in-place edits without invoking change-management; (2) terminal-or-onward (Accepted / Superseded / Deprecated) — locked, mutations route through propose/apply; (3) reversible-to-Draft (In Review; Blocked when originating from Draft) — sits between, and P-6 explicitly defers to REQ-LIFECYCLE-004's named FSM transitions rather than duplicating or overriding them. Recommend transitioning to mitigated. Status left at identified for author commitment.

P-6's "MUST route through change-management" weakens REQ-LIFECYCLE-004's In Review → Draft path

Concern. REQ-LIFECYCLE-004 already names the lawful path for revising an In-Review artifact: flip it back to Draft via the author-initiated In Review → Draft transition, then edit. P-6's second paragraph says "Mutations to past-Draft artifacts MUST route through the change-management surface (/spectastic.propose → /spectastic.apply)" with no carve-out for the FSM's existing reversal transitions. The principle silently overrides REQ-LIFECYCLE-004's escape valve and forces the heavier propose/apply cycle in a case the meta-spec specifically designed a lighter gesture for.

30-day frame. An author in an In-Review revision loop is told by tooling — citing P-6 — to author a change proposal instead of using the In Review → Draft flip the meta-spec sanctions; the contradiction surfaces as a defect against either P-6 or REQ-LIFECYCLE-004, and resolving it requires amending one to defer to the other.

Author draft response. Same underlying mitigation as R-1 — P-6's body now has a dedicated paragraph on reversible-to-Draft states that explicitly defers to REQ-LIFECYCLE-004's named transitions ("The lawful path for revising a reversible-state artifact is the FSM transition first, then the Draft-state in-place edit — not the heavier proposal / apply cycle. P-6 defers to REQ-LIFECYCLE-004 for these states; it does not duplicate or override the FSM"). The contradiction is closed by construction. Recommend transitioning to mitigated. Status left at identified for author commitment.

"Without ceremony" licenses skipping the changelog-entry discipline the originating fixes require

Concern. P-6 paragraph 1 mandates that authoring tools "MUST accept in-place edits against Draft artifacts without ceremony." But 008/T-001's Fix explicitly says Draft in-place edits should "preserve ID, append a one-line <spec-changelog> entry" — that is minimal ceremony, and it's load-bearing for the audit trail. The §3 Approach claims P-6 tells verbs "what to do" not "what to forbid", but "without ceremony" reads naturally as a license for a future verb implementor to skip the changelog append entirely, because the principle is non-negotiable and the sibling FR is not.

30-day frame. A future verb's sibling proposal cites P-6 to justify omitting changelog entries on Draft re-runs ("the principle says no ceremony"); Draft-state history becomes unreconstructable across the affected verbs, and the fix is either a P-6 wording amendment or a per-verb FR that explicitly contradicts P-6's plain reading.

Author draft response. Accepted as written; P-6's paragraph 1 amended to scope "without ceremony" explicitly: "without invoking the change-management surface ... Minimal authoring discipline still applies — preserve stable IDs across edits (per P-3) and append a one-line <spec-changelog> entry per edit. 'Without ceremony' scopes to the change-management cycle, not to the changelog/ID-preservation hygiene." The license-to-skip reading is closed; future verb authors citing P-6 see the changelog requirement directly inside the principle's body. Recommend transitioning to mitigated. Status left at identified for author commitment.
