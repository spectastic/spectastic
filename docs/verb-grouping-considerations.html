Verb grouping

Eight lifecycle verbs became thirty-one top-level commands. The fix is not fewer features; it is grouping them under the theme they belong to.

Status Draft Type Direction note — to be specced Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 19 Sep 2026 Companion discoverable-defaults Sources codebase audit of spectastic --help, 19 Sep 2026 Read time

The CLI's --help lists 31 top-level commands. Eight are the lifecycle; the rest arrived one spec at a time, each honestly small, and in three different naming styles — bare (adrs, verdict, materialise), colon-suffixed (units:add, visual:import, verify:exec, tests:for), and grouped with subcommands (corpus import, contract coordinate). The direction recorded here, decided while naming 122's decisions verb: group under the noun the verb acts on, the way corpus already does, and stop adding colon suffixes. This note exists so the regroup can be specced later without re-deriving the inventory.

1 · The surface as it stands

From spectastic --help on 19 Sep 2026, the top-level commands, by naming style:

Three styles for one idea is the defect. units is the sharpest case: units path is a subcommand and units:add is a colon verb, on the same noun, in the same release.

2 · The direction

Group under the noun. A theme is the thing the verbs act on — decisions, units, visuals, verification, contracts, the corpus — and its verbs are subcommands: spectastic decisions for, spectastic decisions export. This is what corpus already does, and corpus is the group with the most verbs and the least confusion about where a new one goes.

Colon suffixes are a stop-gap, not a style. Each was the least-effort way to add one verb beside an existing one. None should be added from here; each existing one is a candidate to fold into its group when that group is next touched.

The lifecycle eight stay bare. They are the product's spine and the slash commands; grouping them would cost the thing OpenSpec got right and this project deliberately kept.

Old names keep working as hidden aliases for at least one release: the agent definitions, the generated skills, and downstream muscle memory call the old names, and a rename that breaks an Accepted spec's tests is a propose, not a tidy-up.

4 · What the spec must decide

  • Whether a group can also be a verb (verify as both), or whether groups are pure namespaces like corpus.
  • The alias policy: hidden from --help, for how many releases, and whether the alias prints a one-line nudge.
  • How the skill/agent definitions and the generated Codex skills track a rename — the drift gate (031/111) sees the markdown, not the CLI's names.
  • Whether --help should render groups as sections (the themed listing the user asked for) rather than a flat list.
