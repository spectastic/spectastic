1 · Method

The agent-workflows brief documents the rubric family and names the default: RICE (Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort) as the team-backlog lens, with WSJF (Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size) for portfolio sequencing where time-criticality and enablement dominate. Its guidance: "pick one as your main tool and use the other for a gut check." Picking the next initiative is portfolio sequencing, so each candidate is scored with RICE and cross-checked with WSJF.

Scores are directional 1–10, not false precision — per the expense brief's own humility, the conversation a rubric forces matters more than the number. RICE Confidence is a slice's grounding status (§10.2).

3 · The agent-workflows breakout — foundation → headliners → others

The brief's §4 names six workflows and signals their shape (line: "W-1 and W-2 … need genuinely new orchestration; W-3, W-4, W-5 scale existing verbs … the cheapest path to value is W-5 and W-3; the highest-value is W-1"). Tiered:

Foundation — W-1 · Hands-off lifecycle pipeline

It builds the orchestration substrate — decider · checkpoint · bounded-loop · validate-between-steps — that W-2..W-5 all reuse. Its raw RICE is lowest (biggest effort, autonomy risk the field warns about) but its enablement is highest: nothing else gets a hands-off mode without it. It is also the #1 headliner (submit a spec, walk away).

Headliners — W-1 + W-2

The two the brief names. W-2 · value-ranked slicer is the more bounded headliner: it composes the existing spec + propose + apply and adds only the ranking judge + the coverage critic — so it carries higher Confidence and lower Effort than W-1. It is the method this very report runs.

Others — W-3, W-5, W-4

W-3 · adversarial panel and W-5 · triage fan-out are cheap, high-confidence scalings of existing verbs (they top the raw-RICE column). W-4 · conformance sweep is an advisory audit layered atop validate — medium value, findings land as triage cards, never edits.

Done — W-6 · Explore graduation

Shipped this cycle as 023-explore-graduation + 024-explore-restore. It leaves the backlog — a reminder to re-score a backlog against what actually shipped.

4 · The insight & recommendation

Raw RICE ranks the cheap speedups (W-5, W-3) highest — its built-in bias toward low effort. The WSJF / foundation-first lens corrects for that: a workflow's enablement (does it unblock others?) and a candidate's time-criticality (is the cost of delay real now?) are what elevate the strategic spine. So the two rubrics together, not either alone, give the order.

R-001 · Pick up git-strategy next; grab W-3 + W-5 as cheap wins; sequence the spine W-1 → W-2

Status
Recommend
Context
git-strategy tops both rubrics and is an enabler (its branches/PRs/validate-gates are exactly the guardrails W-1's autonomy needs). The decomposition surfaces two cheap tactical wins (W-3, W-5) that out-RICE expense.
Decision
(1) git-strategy remains the #1 strategic foundation — start with its schema-rules slice. (2) Opportunistically take W-3 + W-5 (tiny, high-confidence, build on existing verbs). (3) Pursue the agent-workflows spine W-1 → W-2 after git-strategy lays the branch/PR + validate-gate guardrails W-1's hands-off autonomy must ship behind. (4) expense after the foundations — high impact, but its own domain confidence (estimation is unreliable) and large effort place it behind the enablers.
Consequences
+ Foundation-first: each step de-risks the next (git-strategy → safe W-1 autonomy). + The cheap wins ship value while the foundation lands. − Defers the flashiest bet (W-1 hands-off) behind less-visible plumbing — the right call when autonomy without guardrails is the failure mode the field names.
