Research · Onboarding & sign-off

The Training-Wheels Protocol

A fading scaffold that lets junior engineers and product folks meaningfully sign off on specs — surfacing domain, framework, and implementation knowledge at the moment of decision, then removing itself as competence grows.

Status Draft · research, not yet a spec Kind Research & design exploration (dogfooded in the design system) Owner Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Last updated13 Jun 2026 Feeds A future /spectastic.spec for the protocol itself Read time

In a spec-driven world the spec is the controlled artifact and code is its derivative — so sign-off becomes the load-bearing quality gate, and it lands on the two personas least equipped for it: the junior engineer (can read code, lacks tacit review judgment) and the product person (owns the domain, bounces off framework terminology). The shared failure mode is rubber-stamping, which AI makes worse. The fix is not "explain more" — it is a training-wheels protocol: block the known wrong paths, surface knowledge just-in-time in two-level layers, and fade the scaffold as competence is demonstrated — because guidance that helps a novice actively harms an expert. Spectastic already encodes most of the bones (INVEST gate, smallest-demoable, risk register, defer-to visible-failure, the two-phase interview). The protocol is a competence-graded layer over them, plus an in-document knowledge graph that degrades gracefully where richer graphs aren't available.

1 · The problem, stated precisely

Spec-driven development elevates the spec to the primary artifact and treats code as its expression in a particular language and framework. GitHub's Spec Kit methodology states it directly: "The specification becomes the primary artifact. Code becomes its expression… Maintaining software means evolving specifications." spec-driven.md. That single move turns sign-off from a formality into the gate the whole quality story hangs on — and it lands that gate squarely on the two personas least equipped for it today.

The two personas

  • The junior engineer — can read code, but hasn't built enough systems to have the tacit judgment review demands. Reviewing what you didn't write is harder than writing it: it requires understanding without the scaffolding of having built it yourself.
  • The product person — owns the domain and the "why," but bounces off framework terminology, architectural trade-offs, and the downstream implications of an implementation choice.

The shared failure mode is rubber-stamping, and AI accelerates it. The learning that normally happens in review — juniors absorbing seniors' reasoning, seniors articulating tacit knowledge — is exactly what gets hollowed out when an assistant produces a confident artifact and a thin-context reviewer waves it through. A protocol that merely adds explanation can make this worse: it manufactures confident sign-off on things the signer doesn't actually understand. The talent-pipeline worry is real and widely voiced: if entry-level reps disappear, the future senior pipeline breaks — and there is no proven model yet for how juniors learn in a spec-first world. That uncertainty is why this protocol ships with its own measurement (§9).

So the design goal is sharper than "surface more knowledge." It is: build justified confidence, and make unjustified confidence structurally hard.

2 · The spine: wheels that come off

The metaphor is load-bearing and has a real research lineage. Four linked findings give the protocol its shape, and each is a citable, well-established effect:

  1. Training-wheels interfaces work by blocking wrong paths, not adding prose. The original HCI study built a novice word-processor UI that made error-prone states unreachable, and measured substantially faster learning and better comprehension; the unscaffolded control group spent nearly a quarter of its time recovering from errors the training interface simply blocked. Carroll & Carrithers, "Training Wheels in a User Interface," CACM 27:8 (1984). ACM · NN/g summary.
  2. Layer knowledge — but no more than two deep. Progressive disclosure defers advanced options to a secondary surface; the usability ceiling is firm: designs that go beyond two disclosure levels lose users between the layers. Nielsen, Progressive Disclosure (1995). NN/g. The 2-level limit is a specific, citable guideline, not a vibe.
  3. The wheels must come off. The expertise reversal effect: guidance essential for novices becomes redundant and actively degrades expert performance. A permanent training-wheels mode is a bug. Kalyuga, Expertise Reversal Effect and Its Implications for Learner-Tailored Instruction, Educational Psychology Review (2007). Springer.
  4. Fade on a competence trigger, via a validated sequence. The guidance-fading effect: worked example → completion problem (some steps given) → independent problem. Faded sequences beat static full guidance on post-tests; scaffolds drop "once the learner can complete the task," not on a clock. Renkl & Atkinson (2000/2004). Springer. Novices also learn principles far better by studying worked examples than by unguided problem-solving (the worked-example effect, Sweller & Cooper).

Which yields the definition the rest of this document builds on:

The Training-Wheels Protocol is a fading scaffold. It surfaces domain, framework, and implementation knowledge at the moment of decision, blocks the known wrong paths, and progressively removes itself as the signer demonstrates competence — because guidance that helps a novice harms an expert.

The good news from auditing the codebase: the bones already exist. Spectastic encodes best practice as structure in the artifact — the INVEST gate, the smallest-demoable prompt, the ISO-31000 risk register, defer-to that renders a loud missing defer-to in salmon, and the two-phase AskUserQuestion interview. The protocol is mostly a competence-graded layer over these, not a new subsystem.

3 · Three knowledge surfaces × two modes

The ask names three things to surface — domain, framework/technical, and implementation behaviour/choices — and asks about implicit vs. explicit. Each cell below maps to a mechanism spectastic already has or can add cheaply.

SurfaceExplicit (author opts in)Implicit (surfaces itself)
Domain Living, context-scoped <spec-glossary>; the interview requires a one-line definition for any domain noun in a must requirement. <dfn> auto-cross-linking already links the first occurrence of each defined term to its definition — no author effort.
Framework / technical A plain-language summary layer (a <spec-tldr> per section); a readability gauge beside <spec-budget>. Progressive disclosure via <details> + <spec-audience-map>: essentials on top, the mechanism one click down — and only one.
Implementation choices Every <spec-decision> carries a <spec-matrix data-winner> so the trade-off is shown, scored, and the chosen option highlighted. At the novice band, pull the nearest archived examples/ ADR as a worked example beside the blank one (the worked-example effect).

Domain knowledge is the discipline of one rigorous, shared vocabulary spanning the domain expert's words, the whiteboard, and the code — because software does not cope with ambiguity. The same word legitimately means different things in different bounded contexts, so the glossary must be context-scoped and living, not a one-time deliverable. Ubiquitous language, bounded context, context map: Fowler · Ubiquitous Language · Fowler · Bounded Context.

Accessibility caveat (multi-source, high confidence). Do not use <abbr title> or hover-only tooltips as the definition surface — <abbr> is not focusable, fails on touch, and screen-reader behaviour is inconsistent. Per WCAG 2.1, a definition must be reachable on hover and keyboard focus, stay visible, and be dismissible. Spectastic's existing <dfn>-anchored-to-glossary pattern (a real link, not a title attribute) is already the accessible choice — keep it. bitsofco.de · Higley · Tooltips in WCAG 2.1 · NN/g · Tooltip Guidelines.

4 · The protocol: a competence ladder

Render the fading as three explicit proficiency bands, stored per author (and per bounded context) and surfaced as a status pill the way <spec-status> already works. This is the guidance-fading sequence made operational.

BandScaffold activeFades when…
Wheels novice Worked example shown beside every blank block; every domain term must be defined; the interview asks every non-obvious question; wrong paths are blocked (can't promote to accepted with an open question or a failing INVEST row); plain-language summary required. The author has signed off on N specs that generated no downstream corrective triage cards.
Completion developing Worked example collapsed into <details> (on demand); interview asks only the non-obvious questions (the existing decision-exhaustion discipline); nudges instead of blocks ("Budget is amber — split?"). Competence signal sustained across contexts.
Independent proficient Scaffolds off by default; coaching available only on explicit request. Leaving them on would degrade expert work (expertise reversal) — turning them off is a requirement, not a courtesy. — (resets locally on entering a new domain).

Two rules the research forces:

  1. Fade on competence, not time. The trigger is demonstrated outcome — e.g. specs the author signed off on that didn't later generate implementation- or spec-layer triage cards. Spectastic's triage layer-classification already produces exactly that signal. Don't graduate on a 90-day timer.
  2. Reset locally on new context. Proficiency is per-bounded-context. A senior entering an unfamiliar domain is a novice in that domain — re-engage the domain wheels even at the Independent band.

Per-persona on-ramps

Extend <spec-audience-map> into two reading lenses, toggled like the existing dark-mode control (progressive disclosure applied to the reader, within the 2-level ceiling):

  • Product lens — collapses framework/implementation <details>; foregrounds plain-language summaries, glossary links, the smallest-demoable line, and the outcome of a decision matrix (what got easier/harder) rather than the mechanism.
  • Engineer lens — foregrounds requirements, conformance index, ADRs, the architecture SVG.

5 · Making sign-off mean something

This is the crux. Five mechanisms, ranked by how directly they fight rubber-stamping:

  1. Constrain the review to checklist-verifiable properties; escalate the rest. A thin-context reviewer can honestly verify "every requirement is testable and unambiguous," "success criteria are measurable," "no open clarifications remain" — and escalate domain-specific concerns to experts. Spectastic's INVEST gate, conformance index, and "no [NEEDS CLARIFICATION]" check already are this checklist; make them the explicit sign-off surface. Google's reviewer doctrine: approve on verifiable code-health properties and "escalate domain-specific concerns to subject matter experts." eng-practices.
  2. Mechanise ambiguity detection so the human doesn't have to spot it unaided. Spectastic already has the seam: the adversarial risk pass in /spectastic.propose lands findings as <spec-risk status="identified"> that block apply until resolved. Extend that pass from propose-time to sign-off-time — a junior can't approve while an identified ambiguity is open. This is the error-blocking principle applied to review. Kiro ships neuro-symbolic requirements analysis to flag criteria that "accept multiple plausible interpretations." kiro.dev (vendor, medium confidence).
  3. Require written reasoning to approve. The strongest anti-rubber-stamp mechanism in the literature: the signer writes a brief human-authored rationale, so they remain the author of their reasoning even when AI produced the artifact. For spectastic: a <spec-signoff> block with a required "what I verified / what I escalated" field. "Prompt & Code Review," arXiv 2602.00496. The Linux-kernel norm is the same: a Reviewed-by tag "shouldn't be a rubber stamp."
  4. Reframe the verdict from "No, because" to "Yes, if." Reviewers state what it would take to approve, which unblocks less-senior authors and converts a gate into actionable feedback. The <spec-risk> response block is the natural home. Squarespace's RFC reframe. The Power of "Yes, if".
  5. Train the reviewer, not just the spec (shadow review). The junior writes the review; a senior reviews the review. Make review a mentoring surface — call out what was done right, not only what was wrong. A <spec-signoff role="shadow"> renders visibly provisional and pairs to a senior confirmation. Shadow review & review-as-mentorship: Pragmatic Engineer · Google eng-practices.

Two failure modes to design against. (1) Status-correlated rubber-stamping — at one well-known org, RFC reception correlated with author rank: senior authors got a few cosmetic notes then a rubber stamp. A protocol that auto-trusts senior authors recreates this. (2) The spec-sign-off ≠ correct-code gap — approving the spec doesn't guarantee the (non-deterministically generated) code matches it, so accountability must stay with the human who ships, who watches it go live and is ready to roll back. Pragmatic Engineer · RFCs · Intercom Eng.

6 · Knowledge graphs, in four tiers

Spectastic is single-file, dependency-free HTML — so the protocol must degrade gracefully: fully functional with no graph, richer where one exists. Four tiers, smallest footprint first.

TierWhat it addsFootprint
0 · In-document graph Emit the spec's requirements, decisions, and glossary terms as JSON-LD in a <script type="application/ld+json"> block using schema.org types (TechArticle, SoftwareSourceCode, APIReference). Every spec becomes a node in a content knowledge graph. Zero dependencies — true to single-file. The recommended default; always available.
1 · Linked ontology <dfn data-kg-uri> links a local definition to a node in an enterprise ontology (glossary ↔ ontology ↔ KG feedback loop). "Where available" = the attribute is present. Optional attribute; local glossary still works when absent.
2 · GraphRAG explainer Answers "what are the implications of this choice?" — multi-hop reasoning over connected entities, with source-grounded provenance the reviewer can audit. Powers the on-demand explainer. External service; behind the --coach path only.
3 · Code knowledge graph Resolves a referenced symbol against a code graph (typed facts: definitions, references, call/inherit edges) to show what a framework actually does — the structural half of the question. External index; "where available."

The synthesis for "what does this framework do / what are the implications of this implementation choice": code knowledge graphs answer the structural half (symbols, references, call edges); GraphRAG answers the narrative/implication half with provenance; JSON-LD/schema.org is how a spec publishes its own knowledge into a graph. Code KGs: Meta Glean, CodeQL, Sourcegraph code graph. Multi-hop + provenance: Microsoft GraphRAG. In-document: schema.org / JSON-LD.

Name collision to avoid: "Glean" is two distinct things — Meta's open-source code indexer (Tier 3, the code graph) and Glean the enterprise-search company whose KG is pitched at onboarding. Both are relevant; keep them separate.

GraphRAG's headline "outperforms baseline RAG" result is LLM-graded, not a hard benchmark — treat as directional. KG-reduces-hallucination claims lean on vendor framing; directionally supported, promotional in tone. Recommendation: ship Tier 0 by default, design the attributes for Tiers 1–3, and treat 1–3 strictly as "where available" enrichments.

7 · What I'd actually build (proposed decisions)

Each recommendation is framed as an ADR-shaped <spec-decision> with status proposed — ready to be promoted (or contested) if this research becomes a spec. None requires leaving the single-file HTML model.

D-001 · Proficiency bands as the fading engine

Status
Proposed
Context
The research is unambiguous that scaffolding must fade on competence or it harms experts (expertise reversal). A protocol needs a single variable that gates every other scaffold.
Decision
Introduce a per-author, per-bounded-context proficiency= setting (wheels | completion | independent), surfaced as a status pill, driving worked-example pairing, interview depth, and block-vs-nudge behaviour. Fade on the existing triage-card competence signal; reset locally on new domains.
Consequences
+ One lever controls the whole scaffold. + Reuses the triage signal already produced. − Requires a place to persist per-author state, which a single file doesn't have natively (project-level config, or a header attribute the author sets honestly).

D-002 · A <spec-signoff> element that resists rubber-stamping

Status
Proposed
Context
Sign-off is the load-bearing gate, and a bare "approve" button is the rubber-stamp affordance. The strongest countermeasures are written reasoning and a blocking ambiguity check.
Decision
Add <spec-signoff> with a required human-written "verified / escalated" field, a "Yes, if" field, a named accountable human, and a role="shadow" variant. Extend the existing risk-pass gate so promotion to accepted is blocked while any <spec-risk status="identified"> remains.
Consequences
+ Sign-off becomes an authored act, not a click. + Reuses the risk-status gate verbatim. − Adds friction to approval (intended); − shadow-review pairing needs a convention for who confirms.

D-003 · Product / Engineer reading lenses

Status
Proposed
Context
The product persona bounces off framework depth; the engineer wants it. One document must serve both within the 2-level disclosure ceiling.
Decision
Extend <spec-audience-map> with a lens toggle (reuse the dark-mode-toggle pattern) that collapses/expands framework and implementation <details> and re-weights what shows by default. Plus an on-demand /spectastic.explain (or --coach flag) that pulls from glossary → ontology → GraphRAG where available.
Consequences
+ One artifact, two audiences, no fork. + Explainer is on-demand (just-in-time), avoiding always-on expertise-reversal harm. − A real toggle is JS; must degrade to "everything expanded" with JS off (per the progressive-enhancement principle).

D-004 · Tier-0 JSON-LD emitter as the default knowledge graph

Status
Proposed
Context
"Enable knowledge graphs where available" must not betray the no-dependency principle. The smallest graph is one embedded in the file itself.
Decision
Emit each spec's requirements, decisions, and glossary terms as schema.org JSON-LD in a <script type="application/ld+json"> block. Design Tiers 1–3 as optional enrichments behind attributes, never hard dependencies.
Consequences
+ Every spec is machine-readable with zero new dependencies. + Forward-compatible with enterprise graphs. − JSON-LD duplicates content already in the DOM; needs a generator step so authors don't hand-maintain it.

9 · Open questions

  1. Scope of proficiency. Per-author, per-project, or per-bounded-context? (Likely all three, with context dominating — but where is the state stored in a single-file world?)
  2. Block or flag? Does the protocol block a novice's sign-off (truer to the error-blocking research) or only flag it for senior confirmation (gentler, lower-friction)?
  3. Which KG tier ships in v1? Recommendation is Tier 0 only, with attributes designed for 1–3 — but is even Tier 0's generator step worth it before a spec exists that needs it?
  4. Measuring the bet. The deepest unknown is honest: there is no proven model for how juniors learn in a spec-first world. What's the metric — downstream corrective triage cards per author band over time? And who reads it?
  5. Does this earn new verbs/elements? Spectastic guards its 8-verb surface and per-element rent. Are <spec-signoff> and a /spectastic.explain justified, or can the lenses and sign-off ride existing elements (e.g. a <spec-triage>-shaped sign-off card)?

10 · Glossary

training-wheels protocol
A fading scaffold: surfaces knowledge and blocks wrong paths for novices, then removes itself as competence is demonstrated.
scaffolding
Temporary support calibrated to a learner's zone of proximal development; defined by being removed once the learner can perform unaided.
fading
The deliberate, gradual removal of scaffolding as competence grows — triggered by demonstrated outcome, not a fixed schedule.
expertise reversal
The effect whereby guidance that helps novices degrades expert performance — the reason the wheels must come off.
progressive disclosure
Showing essentials by default and deferring advanced detail to a secondary layer; usable to at most two levels deep.
ubiquitous language
One rigorous, shared vocabulary spanning domain expert, document, and code — scoped to a bounded context.
rubber-stamping
Approving without spending real attention; the failure mode the sign-off mechanisms are designed to resist.
GraphRAG
Retrieval-augmented generation grounded in a knowledge graph; strong at multi-hop "implication" questions with auditable provenance.
JSON-LD
Machine-readable structured data embedded in a page via <script type="application/ld+json">, using a shared vocabulary (schema.org) — the Tier-0, zero-dependency knowledge graph.
