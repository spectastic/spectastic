Considerations · Corpus acquisition

The source might be a person, or a document no one has fetched

A corpus is only as good as what gets into it — and the two richest sources, an expert's head and the authoritative original, are the two the proposal had no door for.

Status Draft Type Acquisition & trust survey Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 24 Jul 2026 Sources 6 primary, verified 24 Jul 2026 Companion Knowledge bases for spec authoring Read time

The knowledge-base proposal has one way in: a human converts a PDF. Two acquisition paths were never considered. First, the source is a person — a subject-matter expert whose knowledge should be interviewed out and persisted, not left to walk out the door. Second, the authoritative document exists but no one has fetched it — the tool could find the original from a respected origin (an SEC filing, a standards paper), ingest it, and then cite the committed artifact. Both reuse machinery spectastic already has (the interview discipline; the spike; the deep-research harness), and both produce the same thing: a citable corpus document with provenance and a content hash, differing only in where it came from and what guard makes it trustworthy. The move that makes the second path safe is the answer to "confidently cited without hallucination": the model never cites from memory — it cites a committed, hashed artifact, or it acquires one first. A fabricated citation becomes structurally impossible, because you cannot cite a source ID that does not resolve to a real file. Autonomous sourcing is worth building — guarded: bounded to an authority allowlist, human-confirmed before commit, fully provenanced, and fenced.

1 · The gap — a corpus that only ingests documents

Everything the companion doc proposed assumed the knowledge arrives as a file: convert the PDF, stamp its provenance, commit it, cite it. That covers published standards and manuals. It misses the two sources a real project leans on hardest.

The first is tacit: the settlement-ops lead who knows which exception paths actually fire, the staff engineer who remembers why the retry window is fifteen minutes. That knowledge is in a person, not a document — and knowledge capture exists precisely because it walks out the door if no one elicits it.Structured expert interviews, open + probing questions, think-aloud/protocol analysis, and multi-SME cross-check are the established elicitation toolkit; the non-negotiable last step is validation — the expert signs off on the captured text [3], [4].

The second is latent: the authoritative document that exists — the actual SEC release, the BIS guidance — but that no one has fetched into the corpus yet. When the model half-knows a domain, today it can only mark the claim assumed. But the real source is a URL away. The question this doc answers is whether the tool should go get it, and how to do so without trading model-hallucination for fetch-the-wrong-thing.

2 · One contract, three doors

The key simplification: every acquisition path produces the same artifact — a corpus document with the provenance frontmatter and content hash the companion doc already defined, addressable by a stable source ID. The paths differ only in where it came from (origin) and what guard makes it citable (status).

DoororiginCitable once…
converted (existing)the source PDF + the convertera human spot-checks the conversion
interview (new)interview: <SME role>, <date>the SME signs off on the captured text
sourced (new)the fetched URL + retrieval date + hash + the allowlisted authoritya human confirms the ingestion

Because the output is identical, everything downstream — the edition pin, the staleness gate, the provenance gate, the citation form the harness injects — works unchanged across all three. Acquisition is a front door onto one contract, not three parallel systems.

3 · The interview path — a person as a primary source

Spectastic already has the mechanism: its interview discipline — the two-phase "interview me" pattern, open-ended in chat and decision-committing via bounded questions, run to decision exhaustion. That is exactly what knowledge elicitation prescribes. The acquisition path is that discipline pointed at a person and persisted: run the interview, write the answers into a corpus document whose origin names the expert and the date.

The one guard that makes it trustworthy is SME sign-off. An LLM transcribing an expert can distort, compress, or subtly invert what was said — so the captured document is not citable until the expert confirms it reads true. This is the interview analogue of the "spot-check the conversion" discipline: status: sme-interview means "awaiting sign-off, not yet grounding material"; a decision may reference it, but not as verified, until the expert has signed. Prior art exists for treating elicited expert knowledge as a retrieval source in its own right.A retrieval-augmented architecture for preserving expert knowledge in a regulated (energy) domain — expert knowledge as a first-class, citable corpus [5].

No new verb. Spectastic guards its eight-verb surface, and this is the standalone "interview me" pattern with one added step — persist the result as a corpus document. The interview machinery is reused wholesale; only the output destination and the sign-off status are new.

4 · The autonomous-sourcing path — find, fetch, ingest, cite

This is the ambitious one, and the one the user's finance scenario describes: the tool finds the SEC release, ingests it, and then a decision cites it. Two things already in place make it tractable rather than reckless.

It is a persisted spike. The plan lifecycle already has the spike — a time-boxed investigation that resolves a fact and records its finding. Autonomous sourcing is a spike whose finding is not an ephemeral note but a committed corpus artifact: the same investigation, with a durable, citable output. And the fetch machinery already exists — the deep-research harness (fan-out search, fetch, adversarially verify, synthesize with citations) is the mechanism; sourcing is a narrower, bounded use of it.

The trust anchor is an authority allowlist. The danger is not fetching — it is the model deciding a random blog is authoritative. So sourcing draws only from a curated, per-domain set of vetted origins — sec.gov, bis.org, iso.org, nist.gov — never the open web. This is not speculative: shipping deep- research tools already restrict browsing to trusted sites, and the ones that cite fetched sources with full metadata (URL, retrieval timestamp) are the reliable ones, while those synthesising from training data are where citation hallucination lives.OpenAI deep research reportedly lets you restrict searches to trusted sites; research agents that cite specific live-fetched sources with source-URL + extraction-timestamp metadata build verifiable audit trails, unlike chatbots citing from training data [1], [2].

The flow, with the human checkpoint where the user placed it: the model fetches from the allowlist and drafts a corpus document — content, plus provenance (origin-url, retrieved, source-sha256, and the authority it came from) — but the draft is status: needs-human-confirm and not citable until a human approves the ingestion. The model does the finding and the ingesting; the human gates the commit. And because a fetched document is a strictly worse injection surface than an in-repo one, its text is fenced on the way in (P-11, the existing sanitiser) — the model reads it as data, never as instructions.

5 · The guarantee — cited without hallucination

Never cite from memory.

The whole point is a single rule: a citation resolves to a committed, hashed corpus document, or it does not exist. The model does not cite what it recalls about T+1 settlement; it cites KB-001@2024-05-28, a real file with a hash. When the corpus has no document for a claim, the model does not invent one — it acquires one (interview or allowlist-bounded fetch), which produces a real artifact, and then cites that.

What makes this a guarantee rather than a hope is the provenance gate the companion doc already proposed: a validate rule that a cited source ID must resolve to a corpus document. Under that rule a fabricated citation is structurally impossible — you cannot cite KB-004 unless KB-004 is a real, provenanced file on disk. The model's freedom to hallucinate a source is removed not by asking it nicely but by making an unresolvable citation a build finding. This is the deterministic half of the honesty the companion doc draws: presence and resolvability are guaranteeable.

What is not guaranteed is that the fetched or elicited source actually supports the claim — the model can cite a real document for a point it does not make. Validating that a citation is faithful to its source is an open research problem (retrieval-augmented citation validation).Faithful-citation validation — checking the source actually supports the claim, above a resolve-only gate [6]. So the guarantee is precise and bounded: no fabricated sources; not guaranteed-correct reading of real ones. That is still a large step up from citing from memory.

6 · Should we do it? — yes, guarded

Both paths are worth building; autonomous sourcing especially, because it changes what "grounded" can mean — a domain fact becomes citable on demand, not only when a human happened to pre-load the document. That is the difference between a corpus that must be assembled before it is useful and one that fills itself as specs need it. The value is high, so the guards are the price, not an afterthought:

  1. Allowlist-bounded. Sourcing reads only from a curated, per-domain set of authoritative origins. No allowlist entry, no fetch. The allowlist is the single most important artifact — it is where "authoritative" is defined.
  2. Human-confirmed before commit. The model fetches and drafts; a human approves the ingestion into the corpus. The draft is not citable until then. This is the elevated form of the conversion spot-check.
  3. Fully provenanced. Origin URL, retrieval date, content hash, and the authority it came from — the audit trail the reliable research tools already keep.
  4. Fenced. Fetched text enters through the existing sanitiser; it is data to quote, never instruction (P-11).
  5. Interview, symmetric. The SME path takes the same shape — the model elicits and drafts; the expert (not just any human) signs off before the document is citable.

Sequencing: this rides on the corpus convention and the provenance gate the companion doc names first — there is nothing to confirm-into and nothing to enforce resolvability without them. Acquisition is a second slice, not the first.

7 · Ceilings and open risks

  • The allowlist is only as good as its curation. A wrong or stale "authoritative" origin launders a bad source into a confident citation. Curating it is human editorial work no mechanism removes.
  • Right source, wrong passage. The model may fetch the correct document and still cite the wrong part of it — the faithfulness ceiling of §5, undecidable to gate deterministically.
  • Sign-off attests provenance, not truth. An expert can be confidently wrong or out of date; SME sign-off records who said it and when, not that it is correct. Interview knowledge inherits the same staleness the edition pin was built for.
  • Auto-fetched material carries licensing exposure — worse than the human-converted case, because the tool chose the source. The corpus's redistribution question (companion doc) applies with the human one step further removed.
  • A new injection surface. External web content is the worst case the fence is built for; strong control, not a solved problem.
  • Autonomy erodes the human's felt responsibility. The more the tool fetches and drafts, the more a rubber-stamp "confirm" replaces real review — the confirm gate must stay a genuine checkpoint, not a habituated click.
