Considerations · Learning design

Grounded, correct, and still a lecture

explain never says anything false — it cites only what it opened. But being right is not the same as teaching. The art of teaching lives in the moves a lecture skips: the analogy, the worked example, the two cases held side by side, the picture beside the words.

Status Draft Type Design brief — feeds a future /spectastic.spec Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 25 Jul 2026 Sources 6 primary + the product's own examples page, verified 25 Jul 2026 Read time

explain and explain --course are grounded but lecture-shaped — a correct monologue. A course objective's entire teaching payload is a single flat read paragraph, which the evidence ranks among the lowest-utility ways to learn. Yet spectastic already owns the pedagogical skeleton: its wheels·completion·independent bands are guidance-fading, its course quiz is retrieval practice, its teach-back is self-explanation — and it already claims Explorable Explanations in its design lineage. It has simply never spent that skeleton on the representational moves — analogy, worked example, contrasting cases, illustration, active reading — that turn an explanation into a lesson. Five decisions cash it out; the cheap one (a prompt-only coach upgrade) needs no code, the richer one (a structured teaching payload for courses) is a feature spec, and the boldest one grants the extended verbs a sanctioned interactivity tier the shipping artifacts deliberately don't get.

1 · The gap

The explain verb does one thing exceptionally well: it refuses to invent. It resolves every target against real source, cites only the IDs and paths it opened this turn, and fades its depth across three proficiency bands (wheels · completion · independent). It is honest. But read the shape of what it produces and a second truth surfaces — it is a lecture. A correct paragraph is delivered; the reader receives it; nothing is asked of them until, in course mode, a quiz arrives at the end.

The course makes the gap concrete. Each objective's teaching content is a single field — read: string — a flat paragraph rendered into a "Read" tab (course.ts, renderObjective). No analogy, no worked example, no two cases set against each other, no diagram beside the prose. Just well-grounded exposition. And exposition-you-read is, as §2 shows, one of the weakest study techniques there is.

"I read the objective. I could pass the quiz. I still couldn't have explained it to someone else — I'd been told the answer, not taught the shape."

The sharpest evidence that this is a solved problem — one spectastic hasn't solved here — is spectastic's own front door. spectastic.io/examples.html teaches at exactly the bar this brief wants: a live token-bucket-vs-sliding-window simulator, a BFS-vs-Dijkstra pathfinder you drive, a FIFO-vs-LIFO calculator, each walking a concept end to end. The marketing page teaches better than the teaching verb. And the lineage is already claimed: the design notes credit Bret Victor's Explorable Explanations as the philosophy behind "the document is the artifact; readers can probe it." The explain command has never cashed that lineage out.

What already exists is good and must not be thrown away. The bands are a real pedagogical structure; the quiz-gated mastery (answer correctly → objective ticks) is retrieval practice done right; the ungraded teach-back is self-explanation. The gap is narrow and specific: the command has the scaffolding of good teaching and none of its representational craft.

2 · What learning science knows

Five findings, each from a well-cited primary source, shape the design. Read together they say one thing: the moves spectastic is missing are the moves that carry the most evidence.

Passive loses to active — and "read the paragraph" is passive

The definitive meta-analysis is blunt. Across 225 studies, active learning raised examination performance by 0.47 standard deviations and cut failure rates such that lecturing students were 1.95× more likely to fail (Freeman et al., 2014, PNAS). Dunlosky's landmark review of ten study techniques sharpens the point for our exact case: it rates practice testing and distributed practice high utility, but rereading, summarising, and highlighting low utility (Dunlosky et al., 2013). A flat read paragraph is, mechanically, rereading — the technique the evidence rates worst.

Worked examples, then fade the scaffolding

For a novice, a fully worked example beats unguided problem-solving, because it spends limited working memory on the solution's structure instead of on flailing. The craft is in the fade: present a complete worked solution, then omit the last step, then the last two, until the learner is solving unaided — the "guidance-fading" and "completion-problem" effects (Sweller & colleagues on guidance fading). spectastic's bands already name this continuum — the middle band is literally called completion — but they fade verbosity, not worked-example steps. The literature says fade the steps. (Honest caveat: fading done badly adds load; some studies find it can hurt, so the fade must be deliberate, not a verbosity dial.)

Teach by comparison — hold two cases side by side

This is the heart of the request, and it has the most surprising number attached. When learners compare two analogous cases rather than studying them separately, they abstract the deep, transferable structure instead of the surface story — in one negotiation study, comparers were nearly 3× more likely to transfer the strategy to a new problem (Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson — analogical encoding). A single example invites recall by surface similarity; two aligned examples force the structural alignment that is understanding. This is precisely what the site's token-bucket-vs-sliding-window and BFS-vs-Dijkstra pairings do.

Words with pictures beat words alone

Across 200-plus experiments, Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning establishes that people learn better from words and relevant graphics together than from words alone, governed by principles the design can follow cheaply: signaling (cue the key part), spatial contiguity (put the label next to the thing), coherence (cut the decorative) (Mayer, CTML review, 2023). A course that renders only prose leaves the entire pictorial channel unused.

Let the reader act on the idea

Bret Victor's Explorable Explanations (the term coined by Brusilovsky in 1994; Distill its modern exemplar) argues for active reading: an interactive model of a concept, paired with guidance that "deliberately guides the attention of the audience toward particular phenomena." The distinction matters — not a widget for its own sake, but a guided probe. This is the bar the extended verbs can reach that the shipping artifacts, bound to print-static discipline, deliberately cannot.

3 · The thesis — five decisions

spectastic already owns the pedagogical skeleton. The thesis is not "add pedagogy" — it is spend the skeleton you have on the representational moves you're missing. Four moves, plus the one structural change that unblocks them.

D-1 · The bands become faded worked examples, not verbosity dials

Status
Recommended
Context
The bands wheels · completion · independent today fade how much prose the coach emits (explain.md step 5: "only the depth changes"). The guidance-fading literature says fade the worked-solution steps — and the middle band already carries the exact name of the completion-problem effect.
Decision
Reframe the bands as a worked-example fade. wheels = a fully worked example (every step shown, reasoning included). completion = a completion problem (the worked example with the last step or two left for the learner). independent = the problem/refresher alone. The grounding guarantee is unchanged at every band; what fades is scaffolding, deliberately, step-wise.
Consequences
+ Reuses the vocabulary that already ships — zero new surface for the chat coach, which is prompt-only (018 D-001). + Names a structure the bands only gestured at. − Course-depth-by-band is 019's already-deferred TBD; landing it in the course is P2 work, not free.

D-2 · Analogy and contrasting cases become first-class teaching moves

Status
Recommended
Context
The single strongest lever in §2: comparing two aligned cases roughly tripled transfer (Gentner et al.). The coach reaches for a flat definition where an analogy or a two-case contrast would teach the deep structure.
Decision
Make "reach for an analogy or a contrasting pair before a bare definition" an explicit authoring move. In the chat coach: a described analogy, or a two-column contrast, in prose — prompt guidance only. In the course: a typed side-by-side — reuse <spec-tradeoff> / <spec-matrix> where a scored comparison fits; add a net-new <spec-analogy> / <spec-contrast> where they don't.
Consequences
+ Directly answers the request; grounded in the highest-transfer finding in the review. + Contrast is naturally gradable (a quiz can ask which case a property belongs to). − A bad analogy mis-teaches; the coach must ground both sides in real source, and a course analogy still cites its refs (existence-verified by the kernel).

D-3 · A picture beside the words — via the illustration pattern that already ships

Status
Recommended
Context
Words-plus-graphics beats words-alone (Mayer), and spectastic already renders inline-SVG sparkline diagrams in every design.html via <spec-arch> — self-contained, no external asset, legible with no JS.
Decision
Let a course objective carry an optional <spec-arch> illustration beside its reading, obeying Mayer's cheap principles: signal the key part, put labels next to what they name (spatial contiguity), cut the decorative (coherence). No new rendering technology — the pattern, CSP-safe and print-static, is proven.
Consequences
+ Opens the unused pictorial channel with zero new infrastructure. + Themes automatically off the design tokens. − Authoring a good diagram is real effort; it stays optional, never mandatory boilerplate (coherence cuts both ways).

D-4 · The extended verbs earn a sanctioned interactivity tier the shipping artifacts don't get

Status
Recommended
Context
Explorable Explanations (Victor) want guided interaction — the reader predicts, toggles, steps through. spectastic's strict disciplines exist to protect durable, distributed artifacts: P-4 (no-JS-fallback) and the spec-045 CSP keep a shipped spec.html portable and safe. A course.html is a different animal — opt-in, ephemeral, git-ignored, opened locally, never shipped. It already carries one sanctioned inline gate script (019 FR-006).
Decision
Define an extended-verb interactivity tier: for the ephemeral learning artifacts of the extended verbs (the course today), relax the no-JS-fallback requirement and widen the CSP enough for a bounded, tool-generated explorable-widget vocabulary — predict-then-reveal, step-through a walkthrough, toggle a comparison, nudge a parameter. The tier is explicitly not extended to any shipping lifecycle artifact, which keeps the full discipline.
Consequences
+ Unlocks genuine active reading where it's safe to — the ephemeral, local, opt-in surface. + Draws a clean, defensible line (durable-and-shipped vs. ephemeral-and-local) instead of a blanket relaxation. − It relaxes a principle for a class of artifacts; §6 asks whether that needs a scoped exception or a principles amendment. − Even with JS allowed, P-11 still binds: generated widget code only, and course content stays fenced/escaped (the 045/046 injection lineage doesn't lapse just because the fallback rule does).

D-5 · The structural unlock — a typed teaching payload replaces the flat read string

Status
Recommended
Context
Every move above dies at the same bottleneck: a course objective's teaching content is read: string — one paragraph, no structure to carry an analogy, a worked example, a contrast, or an illustration. 019 v1 deliberately said "no new custom elements" to stay small.
Decision
Widen CourseObjective so read is an optional structured teaching payload — reading prose plus optional analogy, workedExample, contrast, and illustration members — rendered by assembleCourse into the typed elements of D-2/D-3. The kernel's existing verify pass extends to the new members (an illustration is data, not script; a contrast still cites refs).
Consequences
+ One structural change unblocks D-1 through D-4 in the course. + Keeps the grounding guarantee — every member is verified against real source, same as today. − v1's "no new custom elements" is spent, deliberately; a course gains a small render/CSS surface (justified by the teaching gain, and the moves stay optional so a minimal course is unchanged).

4 · Two surfaces, two costs

The moves land on two very different surfaces, and conflating them is how this gets over-scoped. The bare coach is chat; the course is an artifact.

MoveBare coach (chat)Course (artifact)
Faded worked examples (D-1)Prompt guidance — reframe the bands. No code.Vary objective depth by band — 019's deferred TBD.
Analogy / contrast (D-2)Described analogy / two-column prose. No code.Typed <spec-analogy>/<spec-contrast> + reuse <spec-tradeoff>.
Illustration (D-3)ASCII / described sketch only.<spec-arch> inline SVG.
Explorable widgets (D-4)n/a — chat has no artifact.The sanctioned interactivity tier.
Structured payload (D-5)n/a — the coach emits no object.Widen CourseObjective + assembleCourse + verify.

The asymmetry is the plan. The bare coach is prompt-only — it has no kernel by design (018 D-001), so D-1 and D-2 land as authoring guidance in explain.md with no TypeScript at all. That is the cheap, high-leverage P1. The course is where the structure, the assembler, and the verify pass live — D-5 unblocks the rest, and it is a real feature spec: P2. D-4's tier is the boldest and can trail as its own slice once the payload exists.

5 · What's reused, and what's genuinely new

Most of this is already in the box. Honesty about the small net-new surface:

The through-line: spectastic keeps its 8-verb core surface untouched, adds no new verb, and spends its existing component and kernel patterns. The only category-new idea is D-4's tier — and even that leans on an existing precedent (the course already runs one sanctioned script) and an existing threat model (045/046).

6 · Open questions

  1. Is D-4 a scoped exception or a principle amendment? Relaxing P-4's no-JS-fallback for a whole class of artifacts is a constitution-level nuance. It could be a scoped carve-out ("extended-verb ephemeral artifacts are exempt from P-4") recorded where P-4 lives, or a first-class amendment. The verb-scoped instinct says carve-out; the honest cost is that P-4 stops being unconditional.
  2. What is the injection surface of a relaxed-CSP course? A course is generated from spec and corpus content, which is untrusted data (the 045/046 lineage). Allowing JS must not mean allowing author-supplied JS — only tool-generated widget code, with content still fenced/escaped. The tier's security model is its own design problem, not a footnote.
  3. Does richer scaffolding fight the coach's "one-shot, no ceremony" ethos? The bare coach is prized for answering fast and stopping. A worked-example-with-analogy is more to read. The band is the release valve — independent stays terse — but the default (wheels) getting heavier is a real tension to weigh, not assume away.
  4. Interleaving and spacing stay deferred. Dunlosky rates distributed and interleaved practice high, but a course is a single sitting; genuine spacing needs cross-session state (still 019's deferred TBD). This brief deliberately does not open it.
  5. How is a course analogy kept honest? An analogy that mis-maps teaches a wrong structure. The verify pass checks ref existence, not analogical fit — is "does this analogy hold?" a blind-check the kernel can pose, or an irreducibly human review?

7 · Out of scope (for this /doc)

  • Building the P1 coach upgrade — the band reframe and the reach-for-analogy/worked-example/contrast guidance in explain.md. Prompt-only; a future /spectastic.spec (or a direct edit, since the coach has no kernel).
  • Building the P2 course payload — the structured read, the <spec-analogy>/<spec-contrast> elements, the assembleCourse render, and the verify extension.
  • Defining the extended-verb interactivity tier — the relaxed no-JS-fallback rule and the widened course CSP with its security model.
  • The explorable-widget vocabulary itself (predict-then-reveal, step-through, toggle-a-comparison), built on the tier.
  • Hands-on Build (apply/create) objectives, band-personalised course depth, interleaving, and the graded teach-back — all already deferred by 019, and orthogonal to the understand-level moves above.
