Worked example · Corpus grounding

Grounding, its absence, and its expiry — in one screen

Two decisions, both plausible English sentences. One points at a document a reviewer can open; the other rests on what the model happened to know. The design system already tells them apart.

Status Draft Type Worked example Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 24 Jul 2026 Companion Knowledge bases for spec authoring Read time

The knowledge-base proposal argues in the abstract; this is the same idea built and openable. A finance-settlement corpus holds two cited sources; a demo spec computes settlement dates; and its plan carries four decisions that show, side by side, what grounding looks like when it is present (verified, green), absent (assumed, loud amber), and expired (an edition pin catches a citation that silently went stale). Nothing here is a new component — it is what the existing <spec-decision grounding> machinery already renders the moment a citation has somewhere to point. Finance is the vehicle because its failures are measurable: a benchmark reports GPT-4-Turbo incorrectly answering or refusing 81% of curated SEC-filing questions, and grounding against curated documents is reported to cut hallucination 70–90%.

1 · The setup — a corpus and a spec that cites it

The corpus is a single pack built to the proposed convention — an Agent Skills folder, so it is portable across skills-compatible agents rather than tied to the model that authored it:

  • knowledge/finance-settlement/SKILL.md — the discovery layer: a name and description an agent reads first.
  • knowledge/finance-settlement/references/ — the documents, pulled in only when the work needs them:
    • KB-001 — the US equities settlement cycle (current edition T+1, @2024-05-28).
    • KB-002 — FX settlement risk and payment-versus-payment.

Each document carries provenance frontmatter — origin, edition, license, converter, content hash — and KB-002 deliberately carries a restrictive license, so the corpus surfaces a redistribution question rather than hiding it. A demo spec (001-trade-settlement) then computes settlement dates and exposure, and every domain requirement in it cites a corpus ID.

The two corpus documents are hand-written illustrative excerpts, marked status: illustrative-excerpt in their frontmatter. They paraphrase well-known public facts (T+1's May 2024 compliance date; the Herstatt window) to show the shape — they are not authoritative rule text, and the frontmatter says so, which is exactly the kind of check the provenance contract exists to make possible.

2 · The contrast — present vs absent grounding

The plan carries four decisions. Two are grounded against the corpus, one deliberately is not, and one shows a citation that expired. The first two are the whole argument:

DecisionGroundingRenders as
D-001 — settlement date from a business-day calendarverified, cites KB-001@2024-05-28a quiet green grounded
D-002 — retry window for failed instructionsassumed — no corpus document covers ita loud amber ASSUMED
D-003 — report exposure on principal, with a PvP flagverified, cites KB-002a quiet green grounded

D-001 and D-002 sit next to each other. Both read as competent English. One points at a committed document — the governing settlement rule — and renders calm; the other rests on model recall and renders as a visible failure. Today, without a corpus, every domain decision spectastic writes can only be the second kind. That is the entire proposition, and it is not a mock-up: it is the existing grounding machinery, given something real to point at.

3 · The scope line — what the corpus does not ground

The prototype is careful about what it doesn't cite, because that line is as important as the citations.

  • D-002 is left assumed on purpose, with a named resolution path: ingest the market-practice document as KB-003 and re-ground, or mark it explicitly as a local product choice. The tool's job is to make the gap loud, not to paper over it.
  • One requirement — a latency target — is deliberately not corpus-grounded, and says so in its own grounding note. A performance number is a local product decision, not a domain fact. The corpus grounds domain claims; it must not become a ritual where every requirement manufactures a citation.

This is the discipline the companion doc's widened scope depends on: grounding is a claim about domain facts, and a corpus that is asked to justify everything justifies nothing.

4 · The expiry — a citation caught going stale

The fourth decision demonstrates the versioning discipline literally, using a real supersession: US equities moved from T+2 to T+1 on 28 May 2024.

D-004 (from the prototype) · a citation that went stale

Context
A decision authored in 2023 cited KB-001@2017-09-05 — the T+2 cycle that was current then. When T+1 replaced it, its current edition became @2024-05-28, and the 2023 decision's grounding silently stopped being true.
What the pin does
Because the citation names an edition, not just an ID, it still resolves — the T+2 edition is kept under references/superseded/, never deleted — and a staleness gate flags it as citing a superseded edition. Without the pin, this decision would still read verified against a rule that no longer holds.

That is the answer to "how would the original reference have been more clearly versioned": the citation carries the edition it was made against, the old edition is retained rather than overwritten, and the difference between them is machine-detectable. Grounding does not just get to be true — it gets to be caught when it stops being true.

The rendered rule this demonstrates — persistent ID + edition pin + archived copy + supersede-by-append + a staleness gate — is §6 of the companion doc, made openable.

5 · What it proves, and what it doesn't

Shown: the corpus shape (a pack with progressive disclosure); provenance frontmatter, including a deliberately restrictive license; requirements and decisions citing IDs at editions; the verified / assumed contrast rendered by the existing design system; a real superseded-edition example; and the scope line between domain facts and local choices. Both artifacts pass spectastic validate with no findings, and every citation resolves to a file on disk — the check a future provenance gate would automate.

Not shown: the validate rules themselves (the provenance and staleness gates are proposed, not built); real PDF conversion (the documents were hand-written excerpts, not run through Docling); retrieval at scale; and the init scaffolding a real project would get. This is a shape to react to, not a shipped feature.

The scope line the prototype draws — spectastic owns the contract (layout, provenance, the edition-pinned citation, the gates, a dogfooded reference corpus, init templates) and never the content or the converter — is argued in full in the companion doc's recommendation.
