Considerations · Visual specs

A screenshot has no tokens

An image carries the result of a design decision and not the decision. You can measure that a gap is twenty-four pixels; you cannot ask a picture whether it meant to be.

Status Draft Type Architecture & landscape survey Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 11 Aug 2026 Companion annotation-surface · data-model-contract · non-service-observability Sources 21 external, retrieved 11 Aug 2026 · codebase audit Read time

The lifecycle has no notion of a screen. Across 93 specs, 32 considerations docs and 8 templates there is no view, page, flow, mockup, component-inventory or design-annotation concept anywhere; the word screen occurs twice in the templates and both times it means a screenful of prose. Zero <img> elements exist across specs/, docs/, templates/ and examples/, two images are tracked in the whole repository and both are marketing, and .gitignore ignores root PNGs outright. So this is greenfield — but the project has answered the question once already without noticing. When it needed a design reference for its own VS Code canvas, it committed a self-contained HTML mockup rather than a screenshot and instructed the reader to "measure spacing, connector curvature, and node layout against the brief rather than guessing." That is both halves of the answer, in the project's own hand, never generalised. The landscape agrees from the other direction: the closest peer format covers tokens and prose and explicitly covers no motion, no accessibility, no platform variants and no screens; the design tools have converged on five mutually incompatible read paths, one of which costs a seat and rations free users to six calls a month; and the field's own 2026 conclusion is that feeding an agent numeric deltas beats feeding it screenshots, because spatial pixel comparison is the thing models are worst at. Recommendation: reuse the chassis this repository already built for contracts — declare in the design, keep the real thing in a sidecar, render a drift-guarded view — make the design tool a source and never the authority, check fidelity by measurement against declared tokens rather than by comparing pixels, and gate the whole vocabulary on a project-shape axis that does not exist yet. On versioning, resist the single number: a token set, a screen spec and a captured render change at three different rates, this estate already has a mechanism for each — and there is a fourth clock, the platform, that nobody owns and that restyled every Apple app in one release. 59 of the 71 architecture sketches already in the estate hardcode their colours, which is what the one pictorial channel the tool already has looks like when nobody owns it.

1 · The ask — five design sources, one build

The requirement is not "support Figma". It is that a designer working in Figma and someone who prototyped an app in Sketch, Claude Design or Google Stitch should get the same workflow and the same design-accurate build. That constraint does most of the design work on its own, because it rules out the obvious architecture — reading the design tool — before anything else is decided.

Two things survive every column. Every one of them produces a rendered artifact, and most can be made to produce a token file. Nothing else is common: the annotation model, the component model, the variant model and the API are different in all five, and two of them have no persistent design document at all — the design is the code they emitted.

"First class" has to mean something checkable or it is a mood. Five clauses, and the fifth is the one that matters:

  1. An agent can read it without vision — otherwise the two readerships are not equal, they are a text reader and a picture reader who agree by luck.
  2. A human can read it without a design-tool licence.
  3. It survives the design tool being swapped, including for a tool that did not exist when it was written.
  4. Something can fail when the build diverges from it. A document nothing can contradict is a mood board.
  5. It records which decision produced a value, not only the value.

Clause five is where the screenshot dies, and it is worth being precise about why, because the failure is not resolution and it is not the model's eyesight. A picture of a card with 24 px of internal padding is a faithful record of 24 px. It cannot tell you whether that came from space.400, from a designer nudging a handle, or from a rounding error in an export — and those three have different fixes with different owners. Improving the image does not help. The information was never in it.

This is the same shape as the finding in orchestrator-placement: improving retrieval made routing-by-resemblance more confident rather than more correct, because the missing information was never in the corpus. Here, sharpening the render makes the measurement more precise and no more meaningful. When better input does not help, the input is the wrong kind.

2 · The audit — greenfield, and one answer already given

There is no visual vocabulary of any kind. The spec template's nine sections are Context, Scenarios, Requirements, Data model, Success criteria, Assumptions, Open questions, Conformance and Change log. The design template's twelve are Principles check, Technical context, Data model & contracts, Grounding, Approach, Alternatives, Decisions, Project structure, Risks, Complexity, Questions and Change log. Neither has a section for interface, presentation or appearance. A grep for spec-screen, spec-mockup, spec-token or spec-viewport across specs/, templates/, packages/ and assets/ returns nothing. The intuitive constraint is not the real one. The natural assumption is that this repository's security posture forbids images. It does not. The artifact CSP permits img-src 'self' in all eight templates and in every generated view, so a same-origin file reference has always been allowed. What is blocked, at error severity, is a data: URI in src= — the no-executable-content rule holds src in its URI_ATTRS set — and remote hosts. An inline base64 screenshot is a hard validate failure today; a relative path to a file next to the artifact is not. So the obstacle to images has never been the CSP. It is that no directory convention exists, and that the one place images did land is explicitly ignored.

And the project has already answered this question. The single design-handoff-shaped document in the repository is docs/vscode-lifecycle-brief/references/README.md. It opens by inviting exactly what you would expect — "Drop inspiration images, Figma exports, and screenshots here" — and no image was ever committed. What landed beside it is 03-lifecycle-canvas.html, a 1.8 MB self-contained interactive mockup, described in the README's own words as "the artifact, not a screenshot", with the instruction to "use it to measure spacing, connector curvature, and node layout against the brief rather than guessing" and a pointer to "where the live design system lives (for measuring, not guessing)".

That is both halves of this survey's recommendation, written in July 2026, applied once, and never turned into a convention. The artifact rather than the image; measurement rather than the eye. The rest of this document is largely an argument that the instinct was right and should be generalised.

The one pictorial channel that does exist is unowned. <spec-arch> wraps an inline SVG architecture sketch and the design command makes it effectively mandatory for any feature with more than one moving part — 71 of 83 designs carry one. Of those 71, 59 hardcode hex fills and strokes inside the SVG and 7 use theme variables. The stylesheet themes the frame (var(--c-surface), var(--c-border-soft)) and nothing themes the contents, so in dark mode the card is dark and the diagram inside it is a light-mode island. templates/design.html ships it that way, with five literal hex values in the scaffold's own <style> block, which is precisely how it propagated to 59 files. This is not a finding about visual specs so much as a preview of them: the moment a picture enters this system without an owner, it stops following the design system it is embedded in.

One distinction is worth drawing before that finding is generalised. A diagram must follow the reader's theme — an illustration that stays light inside a dark page is the defect just described. A render must not, because it is evidence of a particular mode, and restyling it to match the reader would destroy the thing it is evidence of. The two cases look identical in a repository of text artifacts and want opposite treatment, which is exactly the kind of rule that goes unwritten until something breaks.

The corpus is the wrong home, by its own contract. It is tempting — and it was an explicit hypothesis going in — to feed a component library into the knowledge corpus and let the existing prompt-injection seam carry it. Two things rule it out. The corpus is markdown-only at four independent layers: the loader skips anything not ending .md, the document body is typed string, the prompt block returns string, and the provider takes string. Adding an image is a change at every one of them, not a configuration. More decisively, the corpus's own grounding directive scopes citations to design-bearing domain facts and carves out, by name, "a UX choice". A design system is the canonical UX choice. The corpus would have to contradict its own instruction to hold one.

3 · The peer record — where the field actually is

The closest peer solved the identity layer and stopped. DESIGN.md, an Apache-2.0 format from Google Labs, is a single version-controllable file combining machine-readable tokens in YAML front matter with human-readable rationale in the markdown body — colours, typography, radii, spacing, and per-component token maps, followed by eight required prose sections from Overview to Do's and Don'ts. It is a good format and it is deliberately bounded. It covers no motion, no accessibility beyond contrast checking, no platform variants, no responsive breakpoints and no screens. It answers "what does this brand look like", which is the question a coding agent asks once per project. It does not attempt the question asked once per feature: what is this particular view, in each of its states, and how do I know when I have built it.

The token standard is stable exactly where it is least needed and draft where it is most. The DTCG Format Module reached its first stable version, 2025.10, in October 2025, with a recommended media type of application/design-tokens+json and the file extensions .tokens and .tokens.json. It defines the primitive and composite types — colour with an explicit colour space, dimension, duration, cubic-bézier, shadow, typography, transition — and aliasing by curly-brace reference. Reference implementations exist in Style Dictionary, Tokens Studio and Terrazzo, and Penpot, Figma, Sketch and Framer all support or are implementing it. But theming and modes are not in the Format Module at all. They are in the Resolver Module, which introduces sets, modifiers and named contexts with a resolutionOrder — and which carries the sentence "Do not attempt to implement this version of the specification." The mechanism that would express light versus dark, compact versus regular, or iPhone versus tvOS is the part that is explicitly not ready.

Worth recording precisely, because two summaries of the 2025.10 release state it the other way round: the Resolver document requires a version at its root and requires it to be 2025.10. The Format module requires no version field at the document root at all. A token file and a resolver file are different documents with different rules, and conflating them would put a required field on the wrong one.

The design tools have converged on MCP and diverged on everything else. Figma's server is the richest — design context, sparse metadata, screenshots, variable definitions, code-connect mappings, and a motion tool returning keyframe tracks, easing and CSS @keyframes. Penpot ships its own MCP in its main repository. Stitch ships one that hands designs to a coding agent. The convergence is real and it is not a portability answer, because the tools' models did not converge — only the transport did. And Figma's is priced: a Dev seat at $12/month or a Full seat at $16, with Starter plans and View or Collab seats limited to six tool calls per month. A design that treats a live Figma read as the source of truth excludes free users, Sketch users, and both AI-native tools outright.

The annotation vocabulary already exists and is not machine-readable anywhere. Zeplin categorises pinned annotations as Requirement, Behavior, Animation, API and Tracking, with custom types on top. Figma's Dev Mode annotations sit under Development, Interaction, Accessibility and Content, are settable through the plugin and REST APIs, and can be generated in bulk. The CVS Health accessibility annotation kits — the de facto standard for communicating landmarks, headings, focus order and reading order — are CC-BY-4.0 Figma component libraries with no schema, no JSON and no code output whatsoever. The industry has a shared vocabulary for design intent, and it exists exclusively as coloured stamps inside design files. That is the gap, and it is a strange one: everyone agrees what the categories are and nobody has written them down in a form a program can read.

And the field has already retreated from pixel comparison. The 2026 write-ups converge on the same finding from different directions: an agent asked to eyeball two screenshots is being asked to do the thing it is worst at, and the fix is to hand it structured numeric deltas — "heading font size is 28px, spec says 24px" — extracted from getComputedStyle() and getBoundingClientRect(), at which point it converges in a few iterations. The complementary finding is that vision review is genuinely good at what it is good at — colour drift, padding deviation, wrong weight, grid misalignment, duplicated elements — and "structurally unable to judge interaction states, motion, or anything below the captured frame." Meanwhile Playwright's own answer to brittle assertions is the ARIA snapshot: a YAML rendering of the accessibility tree with roles, names and states, asserted with toMatchAriaSnapshot(). On the native side the same instrument exists under a different name — swift-snapshot-testing supports recursive view descriptions and text dumps alongside images, and XCUITest exposes the accessibility hierarchy.

The portable measuring instrument, in other words, is the semantic tree, not the DOM. That matters more than it first appears, because it is the only measurement surface that exists on both a web page and a tvOS view.

4 · The exemplar, held to the bar

The SCR-HOME handoff artifact (v3.0.0) is the strongest input to this survey and the working hypothesis for most of it: one self-contained file per page, seven annotation layers over a live embed of the real design, a handoff.json sidecar declared normative where the prose disagrees, stable anchors on every section, and a revision log that diffs frozen frames. Held to the five clauses in §1, most of it survives. Some of it should not.

ElementVerdictWhy
Sidecar normative, prose advisoryKeep — it is already the house ruleIdentical to the settled position on contracts: the file is the contract; the artifact is the argument about it. Adopting it here makes the visual spec the second instance of a decided pattern rather than a new one.
Seven layers (structure, behaviour, motion, content, accessibility, data, visual)Keep the axis, not the numberIt is a superset of Zeplin's five and Figma's four and it is better than both. But seven fixed layers on every screen is the mistake the observables trace already made once — see §11.

The one structural objection is the unit. The exemplar is organised per page — one file per screen, which is exactly right for a marketing site with four routes. It generalises badly in two directions at once. Downward, a design system's components are shared across screens, so a per-page artifact either repeats each component's states in every file it appears in or references them and stops being self-contained. Upward, a product shipping the same feature on iPhone, iPad, macOS and tvOS does not have four pages; it has one feature with four presentations, and per-page files would fragment a single decision across four documents with no mechanism keeping them honest. The layer taxonomy survives that; the file boundary does not. §8 is the alternative.

Two smaller notes worth keeping. The exemplar's data layer — the page's API contracts and its tracking plan, specified next to the element they belong to and gated on an unanswered consent question — is the most under-represented idea in the whole field, and it is the layer Zeplin gets right and design files omit. And its deviations block, four lines in the PR rather than silence, is the correct shape for the disagreement between a spec and a build. §7 takes it further by typing them.

The result is not close, and the reason is that option D is not a proposal so much as a recognition. Specs 069, 070 and 072 already built exactly this chassis for API contracts: a design section that declares the interface, a sidecar file at a conventional path that is the contract, and an embedded view that is materialised rather than transcluded and guarded against drift the way verify-view-stale guards the verify view. A design system and a screen inventory have the same three-part shape as an OpenAPI document: a declaration, a file, and an argument about it. Option A loses on expressiveness and on drift — a state matrix in prose is a list nothing can check — and it also loses on altitude, since the companion survey already settled that spec.html holds the conceptual model and the design holds the physical shape. A screen's structure is physical. Option C is the right shape for the fidelity report and the wrong shape for the intent: something has to be authored, or there is nothing to diverge from. Option B scores well on everything except the constraint that actually binds.

D-001 · The visual spec is a design declaration, a sidecar, and a generated view — not a new artifact class

Status
Proposed
Context
Specs 069, 070 and 072 built a three-part pattern for contracts: design.html §3 declares, specs/<id>/contracts/ holds the file, and a materialised, drift-guarded view renders it inside the artifact. The templates' deny-by-default CSP forbids runtime transclusion, so any embed is a copy and therefore a drift problem rather than a rendering one — a question that pattern has already answered. Nothing about a design system, a screen inventory or a state matrix differs structurally from a contract in this respect.
Decision
Land visual specs on the same chassis. The design artifact gains a section declaring the project's design sources, its token set, and the screens this feature touches. The sidecar splits by cardinality: the token set and the axis grid are project-scoped, because every feature shares one design system; the screen inventory and its annotations are feature-scoped, beside the spec that owns them. A component is the third kind and the only one that migrates — born feature-scoped, promoted to project scope when a second feature reaches for it. Promotion is a human act carried by a proposal, never automatic, and it is a change of clock rather than a file move: the component leaves the feature's change log and joins the token set's version. Neither directory is references/, which belongs to the knowledge corpus, nor contracts/, which belongs to 070. Any rendering inside an artifact is materialised at generate time and drift-checked, never transcluded. No ninth verb.
Consequences
+ Almost no new architecture: the declaration mechanism, the sidecar convention, the materialise-and-drift-check pattern and the validate hook for "a declared path must resolve" all exist and have tests. + The verb surface stays at eight, and a designer's change to a screen inherits propose, the adversarial risk pass and the apply gate for free. − design.html is already twelve sections and this makes it thirteen; the artifact is doing more work than its name suggests and a future split is foreseeable. − It means a visual spec is never self-contained the way the exemplar is: the artifact points at files, and a document that has left the repository has lost half of itself. That is the same trade 070 made, made again. − The two scopes are the part of the chassis that does not transfer from contracts, and getting them wrong is expensive: N designs each naming one shared token set is N declarations of one thing with no rule for disagreement. declaredInterfaceState already carries a fix for the sibling bug — "declarations ACCUMULATE rather than shadow" — and a union, which is right for N contracts, is meaningless for one design system. − Because a local component is deliberately invisible to other features, nothing can detect the second use; promotion depends on a person noticing they have built the same thing twice. That is consistent with the rule of three, which says to tolerate the second duplication rather than abstract on it, and it is still a workflow resting on human attention.

6 · The design tool is a source, never the authority — D-002

Portability is not achieved by supporting five tools. It is achieved by depending on none of them. If the read path is a live query, then the spec is only as available as someone's seat, as stable as someone's API, and as portable as the tool it was written against — and two of the five sources in §1 have no persistent design document to query at all. The alternative is already settled elsewhere in this codebase: 073 FR-003 establishes declaration over inference in both directions, on the reasoning that filesystem inference cannot distinguish a producer from a consumer while a declaration can, and declaredInterfaceState already reads designs as standing project posture. The same argument transfers without modification: a tool export cannot tell you which values were decided and which merely are; a declaration can.

So an adapter's job is not to be queried. It is to land two artifacts — a DTCG token file and a set of renders — into the sidecar, once, as a commit. After that the design tool can be cancelled, changed or go out of business and the spec still validates, still measures and still builds. An adapter becomes a convenience for getting good input rather than a dependency for reading it, which is also what makes the Figma seat price an acceptable cost for the people who already pay it and a non-issue for everyone else.

D-002 · A declared token file outranks anything a design tool can be asked

Status
Proposed
Context
Five design sources with five incompatible models and one shared output — a rendered artifact, plus a token file where the tool supports one. Figma's read path is metered and seat-gated. Corrected 18 Aug: the claim that Claude Design has no persistent design document to read was false — it exposes project, file-listing and file-reading calls, and the editor ships a client for them. The decision is unaffected, because it rested on portability rather than on absence, but the stated reason was wrong and an implementer reading it would have restated it. 073 FR-003 already settled declaration over inference on directly analogous reasoning, and made inference something that always runs and can never be switched off rather than something a declaration replaces.
Decision
The authority is a DTCG token file plus declared renders, committed to the sidecar. Design-tool adapters are import-time producers of those two things and are never consulted at validate, measure or build time. Inference from a render remains available as evidence and can be reported, but never outranks a declared value, and a value that was inferred rather than declared is marked as such rather than silently promoted.
Consequences
+ Portability is structural rather than a matter of writing five adapters well, and a source with no adapter is degraded to manual authoring rather than excluded. + The token file is a real standard with real implementations, so the same file feeds Style Dictionary and the build, not just spectastic. − A committed token file goes stale the moment the design moves, and nothing detects that; the drift check can only prove the artifact matches the file, never that the file matches the designer's intent. − It asks designers whose tool has no token export to produce one, which is real work being pushed onto the person least equipped to see why.

7 · Fidelity is measured, not compared — D-003

A percentage of differing pixels is not actionable. It does not say what is wrong, it does not say where, it cannot distinguish anti-aliasing from a broken layout, and it certainly cannot say which of two very different defects it found. The field's 2026 conclusion and this repository's own discipline point the same way. CLAUDE.md already carries the rule — "presence is not containment… assert containment and on-screen bounds, not just that the element exists" — paid for by nodes and a hover card that passed every "it renders" check and were visibly cropped. Every existing visual assertion in tests/ is a computed-style or bounding-box arithmetic check. The project independently arrived at the right method and has never pointed it at a design.

What makes measurement more than a nicer diff is the second half: a measured value can be snapped to a declared token. If a card measures 24 px of padding and the design declares space.400 = 24px, the observation is conformant. If it measures 24 px and the binding says space.300 = 16px, there are a small number of hypotheses, and they are different tickets with different owners:

  • The implementation is bound to the wrong token, or to no token at all.
  • The design is off-system — the mockup used a value the token set does not contain.
  • The platform restyled underneath both and nothing is wrong with either. §9 adds this one, and it is the reason the list is not closed at two.

A pixel diff cannot even express the distinction. A typed deviation must, and — this is the part that matters — it must refuse to choose silently, in the same way the placement survey's verdict shape refuses to route to a plausible-but-wrong owner because "no confident answer" was not on the menu. A deviation class of token, structure, state, content or noise is directly actionable; a percentage is a prompt to go and look.

D-003 · A deviation is typed, token-snapped, and names its hypotheses without picking one

Status
Proposed
Context
The repository has no pixel-diff machinery and a stated discipline of computed-style and geometry assertions, arrived at through a real defect that "it renders" checks missed. The field's independent finding is that structured numeric deltas converge where screenshot comparison does not, and that vision review is strong on static measurable properties and structurally blind to states, motion and anything outside the captured frame. Meanwhile the token set is the only thing that makes a measurement interpretable — 24px is a fact, space.400 is a decision.
Decision
Fidelity checking measures both sides and compares numbers, never images. Measurement reads the semantic tree and computed geometry, not pixels. Every deviation carries a class, a measured value, a specified value, and the nearest declared token; where a measured value does not correspond to any declared token the finding names every hypothesis that fits and selects none of them — the implementation is bound to the wrong token, the design is off-system, or, per §9 D-008, the platform restyled underneath both. Vision is admissible as an additional reviewer for the relational properties measurement is bad at, and is never the gate. Measurement is scoped to the surface the project owns: a component consumed from an external library is measured against that library's contract, not against this project's token set.
Consequences
+ Findings are actionable without a human opening two images side by side, and they terminate — a token-snapped delta has a correct value to converge on. + It reuses the existing assertion style and the existing Playwright setup, needing no baseline images, no threshold tuning and no new flakiness class. − Measurement only sees what it queries, so a defect nobody thought to measure is invisible, where a pixel diff would at least have flagged it. That is a real loss and the honest answer is that vision review covers it partially and imperfectly. − Snapping requires the token set to be complete and correct; against a thin or wrong token file every measurement reports a deviation and the signal drowns. − Three hypotheses is more honest than two and less decisive: the more ways a finding can decline to attribute itself, the more of the triage stays with a human. − Owned-surface scoping is not optional and it is not free. On a project built from an external library, most of the rendered geometry belongs to somebody else, so an unscoped pass would report noise in proportion to how much of the interface the team did not write — which is most of it on the stacks most people use.
 A tempting shortcut to name and refuse: asserting the rendered artifact against a stored reference render, with the render as the baseline. That is pixel diffing with extra steps, and it inherits every failure mode — font rendering, anti-aliasing, animation frames, and a baseline that has to be re-approved on every legitimate change. The reference render's job is to be evidence a human can look at, not the thing a machine compares against.

8 · Variants are an axis grid, and platform is one axis — D-004

The multi-platform case is not a special case. An app shipping on iPhone, iPad, macOS and tvOS looks like a hard problem until you notice that light-versus-dark, compact-versus-regular and default-versus-empty-versus-error-versus-loading are the same problem at different sizes. All of them are contexts that select a different resolution of the same declared design. Treating platform as an axis alongside mode, viewport and state means one feature has one visual spec with a grid over it, rather than four documents that agree by hand.

The DTCG Resolver's sets-and-modifiers model is the right shape for the first three, and it is a draft that says not to implement it. The resolution is to borrow the shape — named axes, named contexts, an explicit resolution order — without depending on the draft file format, and to keep the mapping thin enough that adopting the resolver later is a serialisation change rather than a redesign.

The state axis is the one that can be partly derived, and it is where the existing estate has a genuine advantage. A project that declares an OpenAPI or AsyncAPI contract has already enumerated its error responses; each one is a screen state that must exist. A collection endpoint implies an empty state. A field with a maximum length implies an overflow state. Deriving those and requiring the author to either design or explicitly dismiss each one turns the most commonly skipped part of UI work — the states nobody drew — into a checkable list, using contract data the lifecycle already holds. That is a better argument for visual specs in this tool specifically than anything about tokens.

Platform, however, is not a token axis alone, and it would be dishonest to model it as one. A tvOS view has a focus engine, no cursor and no touch; focus resolves geometrically and every interactive element needs a visible focused state that is unmistakable from an unfocused one. A macOS window has a pointer and a menu bar. These are different interaction models, not different values, so the platform axis has to be able to select structure and behaviour, not merely colours and spacing — which is exactly why the annotation layers in §10 have to be per-context rather than per-screen.

D-004 · One spec, an axis grid over it — borrowing the resolver's shape, not its file format

Status
Proposed
Context
A product may ship the same feature on four Apple platforms plus web, in two modes, at two size classes, in six states. Per-screen or per-platform artifacts fragment one decision across many documents with nothing keeping them consistent. The DTCG Resolver Module models precisely this with sets, modifiers, named contexts and a resolution order — and states that this version must not be implemented. Platform additionally selects interaction model, not only token values.
Decision
Model variants as named axes with named contexts and an explicit resolution order, adopting the resolver's conceptual shape while keeping the serialisation local until the module stabilises. Platform is an axis peer to mode and viewport, and is permitted to select structure and behaviour rather than values alone. State is an axis whose contexts may be derived from a declared interface contract and must then be either designed or explicitly dismissed, never silently absent.
Consequences
+ Ziggy-shaped products get one authored spec rather than four, and the grid makes an unaddressed combination visible instead of merely missing. + Deriving states from an existing contract turns the most-skipped part of UI work into a checkable list using data the lifecycle already holds. − The grid is combinatorial and most cells are uninteresting; without a strong default that only material differences are recorded, this manufactures busywork at exactly the rate the observables trace did. − It is a bet on a draft. If the resolver stabilises differently, the mapping is rework — and this decision is marked spike rather than verified for that reason.

9 · Versioning — three clocks, and a fourth nobody owns

The variant grid is space; versioning is time. It is also the question that decides whether any of the rest works, because fidelity measured against a declared token set means nothing the moment the declaration and the build are at different points in time. The instinct is to put a version number on the visual spec. That is the mistake. Four things change here, at four different rates, driven by four different people, and only one of them wants a number.

What changesAt whose paceMechanism this estate already hasHow it compares
The token set — the design system many features depend onThe design-system ownerprinciples.html: semver, forward-only binding, a compare-and-swap stale guardfrom === live, and it throws
A feature's visual spec — its screens, states and annotationsThe feature<spec-changelog> + typed <spec-delta> + changes/archive/Nothing — append-only history

The platform — iOS 26, a browser, a WebViewSomebody else entirelyNothing.—

The estate has already solved the hardest of these and applied it once. principles.html is the only artifact in the whole system carrying a version number — <b>Version</b> <span>1.9.0</span> — under a policy written into the document itself: "Semver. v1.0.0 is the founding ratification", with MAJOR for a principle removed or redefined, MINOR for one added, PATCH for wording. It is machine-guarded, too: apply.ts refuses a proposal whose declared from version does not equal the live one, so two amendments cannot silently race.

But the clause that matters most is the one nobody notices. Principles bind forward only — "binds new and amended specs from v1.4.0; the structured-waiver clause from v1.7.0", and it "does not retroactively re-open specs 001–029." That is the unanswered question of design-system versioning, answered. When the token set moves, what happens to the forty screens already built against it? The industry's answer is deprecation windows and a migration project; this repository's answer is that a version binds what comes after it, and what shipped under v1.4.0 remains conformant to v1.4.0. principles.html is to specs what a token set is to screens, and the mechanism transfers whole.

The counterpart is just as clear: no spec, design, tasks or verify artifact carries a version at all. The change log is the entire history — specs/000-spectastic/spec.html has thirty-nine entries and no version anywhere. So both halves of the answer already coexist in this codebase: a number for the thing many things depend on, an append-only log for the things that depend on it. Nothing new has to be invented; the two mechanisms have to be told apart.

And there is a property here worth protecting on purpose. Six distinct versioning mechanisms exist across this estate — corpus editions, contract baselines, materialised-view drift, compatibility declarations, the status lifecycle, principles semver — and not one of them ever orders anything. A corpus edition is compared with string equality and has no format contract at all. A contract baseline is an exact byte comparison. Principles is from === live, which is a compare-and-swap and not a check that the bump was legal. "Newer" is decided by arrival or by location, never by comparison. That reads like an omission and is closer to a discipline: equality is decidable without agreeing a format, and the moment something claims to know that one version is later than another it has taken on a format contract it must then enforce forever. A visual spec should inherit the property, not fix it.

D-007 · Three clocks, three existing mechanisms, and never one number over the whole thing

Status
Proposed
Context
A token set, a feature's screens, and a captured render change at different rates and are owned by different people. The estate already versions the first shape (principles.html: semver, forward-only binding, a stale guard that throws) and the second (append-only change log, typed deltas, archive), and already has the third as the contract .baseline/ — captured at propose time, compared baseline versus current rather than incoming versus current, with absence recorded as a distinct state. External practice agrees from the design side: documentation versions are held separate from code versions, and token sets are expected to evolve independently of the component library.
Decision
Version the token set like the principles document — semver, an explicit bump policy, and forward-only binding so a bump does not retroactively invalidate every screen already shipped. Version a feature's visual spec like a spec: no number, a change log, typed deltas, an archived proposal. Do not version captured evidence at all — a render is a dated, ratified baseline that is superseded rather than incremented, and re-ratification is a human act. Compare by equality everywhere, and choose strictness per consequence: exact bytes where a false negative would clobber someone's work, normalised where a difference only means stale.
Consequences
+ Nothing new is invented — three mechanisms already exist, are tested, and have their edge cases recorded. + Forward-only binding gives an honest answer to the question that stops most design-system version bumps, without a migration project attached to every one. − Three mechanisms means three things to explain, and a reader who wants "what version is this screen" gets an answer with three parts. − Forward-only binding means the estate accumulates screens conformant to older token sets, and nothing reports how far behind any of them is; that visibility is a separate problem this decision does not solve.

The fourth clock has no owner and is the one that will bite first. Liquid Glass is the largest visual redesign Apple has shipped since iOS 7, landing across iOS, iPadOS, macOS and tvOS 26 — and it was still moving during the beta, which increased the opacity of toolbars and buttons in response to legibility complaints. A visual spec authored against iOS 18 is invalidated by that without anyone touching the app, the design file or the token set. The web version of the same problem is slower and no different in kind.

This has a direct consequence for §7. A measured deviation was framed there as two hypotheses — the implementation is bound to the wrong token, or the design is off-system. On a platform that restyles underneath both, there is a third: nothing is wrong with either, and the ground moved. A check that cannot express that will report a defect against whichever of the two it happens to prefer, which is the silent wrong-owner failure §7 exists to prevent. The fix is cheap and it is a declaration: a visual spec names the platform version it was designed and last verified against, and the measurement pass reports the platform it actually ran on.

D-008 · Every clock the project does not own declares a baseline, and "the dependency moved" is a third defect hypothesis

Status
Proposed
Context
Nothing in the lifecycle records which operating system, browser, runtime or component library a design was drawn against. Liquid Glass demonstrates that a platform can restyle every app on it in one release and keep adjusting through a beta cycle — and a major version of an external component library is the same event at a different altitude. D-003 names two defect hypotheses for a measured deviation, and neither is true when the thing that changed is a dependency nobody on the project controls.
Decision
A visual spec declares a baseline for every clock it does not own — the platform, per context in the §8 grid, and any external component library it consumes or vendors — recording both what it was designed against and what it was last verified against. The measurement pass records what it actually ran on. Where the two disagree, the deviation is classed as a dependency delta and is not attributed to the implementation or to the design. This amends D-003 from two hypotheses to three; the refusal to choose silently between them is unchanged and now covers one more case.
Consequences
+ Removes a whole class of confidently-wrong findings, which is the failure mode a fidelity check can least afford. + Makes "this design is stale because the OS moved" a reportable fact rather than something a developer discovers by eye eighteen months later. − It is another declaration to keep current, and a stale baseline is exactly as misleading as a stale token file — the mechanism cannot tell a considered baseline from a forgotten one. − It does not say what to do about a dependency delta. Deciding whether to chase a restyle or a library major is a product judgment and the tool has no business having a view. − Generalising past the platform widens what has to be declared from one baseline to one per dependency, which is the kind of list that rots quietly.

Which leaves the question every versioning scheme has to answer: what is breaking? It is tempting to answer mechanically, and the landscape says plainly that nobody can yet. For an API contract the answer is mature: oasdiff classifies 509 distinct changes across an OpenAPI document, and buf does the equivalent for protobuf. For design tokens the entire state of the art is a Figma plugin that diffs Variables against a tokens.json. There is no rule library, no CI tool, and — more to the point — no agreed taxonomy of what breaking even means for a token. A darker accent is invisible to a layout and fatal to a contrast ratio. A four-pixel spacing change is nothing until it is the thing that made a label truncate. Practitioners describe the disagreement directly: a designer calls a change minor because the component still looks familiar while engineering experiences it as breaking because spacing shifted and layouts moved.

The estate already has the honest shape for this, and it is not a heuristic. Spec 077 records a producer's compatibility stance as a closed enum, validates its shape at error severity, checks that coupled attributes are coherent — and verifies nothing. The words "producer claims" are baked into the stylesheet, and the spec states outright that nothing enforces the stance at any tier. A design-change class should be the same artifact: a claim, rendered as a claim, with the same refusal to overstate.

D-009 · Whether a design change is breaking is a producer's claim, never a verified property

Status
Proposed
Context
Breaking-change detection is a solved problem for API contracts — 509 classified rules for OpenAPI, an equivalent for protobuf — and an unsolved one for design. The whole available tooling for token diffing is one design-tool plugin, and no taxonomy exists for what a breaking token change is. The failure is not tooling effort: the impact of a visual change depends on layouts the token set cannot see. Spec 077 already models exactly this situation for event-schema compatibility.
Decision
A token-set release declares a change class as a producer's claim, on 077's pattern: a closed enum, error-severity shape validation, copy that says claims rather than is, and an explicit statement that nothing enforces it. Deprecation uses the standard's own channel — a DTCG $deprecated value naming the replacement — so a rename ships the new token plus a deprecated alias rather than a removal. Any mechanical diff that is built reports what changed, never whether it breaks.
Consequences
+ Refuses an overclaim the field has not earned, and reuses a pattern already built, validated and rendered here. + $deprecated is a real standardised channel, so the deprecation half is interoperable rather than local. − A claim can be wrong, and a wrong claim is worse than no claim because it looks like diligence. The 077 precedent carries that same weakness and accepted it. − It leaves the most useful thing unbuilt: an author still has to decide the class by judgment, on exactly the evidence that made the automated version impossible.
 One asymmetry to hold on to when reading the peer material: a design tool cannot tell you which version of a component a screen was drawn against. Figma versions at the file level through branching and version history, component-level versioning has been a standing feature request rather than a feature, and a library cannot be pinned to a previous version. That is a second, independent reason the declared file has to be the authority — the tool is not able to answer the question even when you are paying it.

10 · Annotations should not become a tenth taxonomy — D-005

Four schemes already describe the same thing. Zeplin's five types, Figma Dev Mode's four categories, the CVS accessibility kits' stamp types, and the exemplar's seven layers all carve up design intent, and they mostly agree. Inventing a fifth carve-up because none is a perfect fit would produce a vocabulary nobody outside this tool can read — which is the failure mode the tokens decision spends its whole argument avoiding.

Five of the eight rows resolve to something that already exists and is already machine-readable, and three of those five resolve to the accessibility tree. That is not a coincidence. WAI-ARIA is an ontology of roles, states and properties designed to make interface semantics machine-readable — it is the only widely implemented type system for "what is this element and what is it currently doing", it is what Playwright's ARIA snapshots serialise, and it is what XCUITest exposes on the other side of the platform divide. Using it as the type system means the annotation layer is the same artifact as the measurement surface from §7, which is worth more than the taxonomy tidiness: a declared role becomes an assertion for free.

The two rows with nothing behind them — tracking and content — are the honest gaps, and tracking is the more interesting one. An analytics plan specified at design time, next to the element that emits the event, and gated on an unanswered consent question, is a genuinely good idea that no SDD tool models and that the exemplar gets right. It is also plainly a slice of its own.

D-005 · Reuse the accessibility tree as the annotation type system

Status
Proposed
Context
Four annotation taxonomies exist and broadly agree; none is machine-readable — the CVS accessibility kits, the industry standard for exactly this, are CC-BY-4.0 Figma components with no schema or code output. Mapping the union onto existing vocabulary shows five of eight layers already have a home and three of those five are ARIA roles and states, which are already serialised by Playwright's ARIA snapshots and already exposed by XCUITest.
Decision
Type annotations with ARIA roles, states and properties rather than a new vocabulary; express the layer taxonomy as a grouping over that type system, mapped explicitly to Zeplin's and Figma Dev Mode's names so an imported annotation lands somewhere. Requirement-class annotations reference an existing <spec-requirement> by id and never restate it. Tracking and content are recorded as genuine gaps and specced separately rather than improvised into the first slice.
Consequences
+ A declared annotation is directly assertable, on web and on Apple platforms, with no translation layer — the annotation and the test become the same statement. + Imported annotations from Figma or Zeplin have a defined landing place instead of arriving as untyped prose. − ARIA is a web ontology; the mapping to native traits is close but not exact, and pretending otherwise would encode a lie about tvOS focus in particular. − It cannot express the design-intent annotations that have no accessibility analogue — visual emphasis, brand rationale, "this must survive greyscale" — which stay as prose and stay unchecked.

11 · None of this may seed unconditionally — D-006

This project has already run this experiment and lost. The observables trace shipped a well-built feature into an estate it did not fit: 37 of 61 verify views carry an observables section, every row in all 37 is a gap, 77 gap rows in total, and no <spec-slo> element has ever been authored outside fixtures. A 100% gap rate across 37 artifacts is not a backlog; it is a question being asked of a population that cannot answer it. The diagnosis in that survey is directly reusable: the profile system has six axes — verification, enforcement, toggles, grounding, frameworks, contracts — and not one of them says what the project is, so mechanisms silently default to a shape.

Visual specs are the same trap with a bigger blast radius. A CLI, a library, a batch job and a parser have no screens. Seeding screen vocabulary, a variant grid and a design-source declaration into every project would put an empty visual section into the majority of them, and the majority is exactly where the estate is: this repository itself is a CLI and a library, and its one UI surface is the tool rendering itself. There is a further precedent — a standard-tier accessibility principle was deliberately not seeded on the grounds that it "reads as noise for non-UI standard/CLI/library projects", and re-deferred rather than dropped.

The gate that is needed already has a name and a planted slug. TBD-project-shape-axis was planted by the non-service-observability survey as the seventh profile axis. This survey does not re-plant it — it is the same slug for the same reason, and re-planting would double-count a pending item. It does, however, add a second independent motivation for it, and that is worth recording: an axis wanted by one feature is a nice-to-have; an axis two independent surveys arrive at from opposite directions is a missing primitive.

D-006 · The visual vocabulary is shape-gated, and the gate does not exist yet

Status
Proposed
Context
The observables trace is the worked failure: 37 sections, 77 gap rows, a 100% gap rate, and zero authored SLOs, because nothing anywhere asks whether a project is the kind of thing that could have one. The six profile axes all ladder rigor and none states project shape. A prior accessibility principle was deliberately withheld from standard tier on the same reasoning. TBD-project-shape-axis is already planted.
Decision
Every element of the visual vocabulary — the design section, the sidecar, the variant grid, the fidelity check — is gated on declared project shape and is absent, not empty, where the shape does not warrant it. Absent means the section is not scaffolded and no gap is reported; an empty section that reports a gap is the failure being avoided. The shape axis is the mechanism this decision wants, and this survey cites the existing slug rather than planting a duplicate. It is a prerequisite for the gate being declared rather than inferred, not for the gate existing: an interface-style heuristic can hold the line meanwhile, failing safe on the antecedent the way the contract-first category already does, and the axis then demotes that heuristic to a fallback rather than replacing it.
Consequences
+ Non-UI projects are untouched, so the feature cannot manufacture 77 more gap rows across the estate. + It supplies the second independent motivation for an axis that has been wanted and unbuilt, which is what usually moves a primitive from wanted to built. − It makes slice one depend on work owned by another survey, and the honest reading is that this survey cannot start without it. − Shape is declared, so a project can declare wrongly, and a mis-declared shape fails silently in the direction of absence — the least visible failure mode there is.
 A question this survey raises and does not close, inherited from the same source: whether shape belongs to the project or to the unit. A repository holding a service and its client library has two shapes; a repository holding an app and its design system has two as well. TBD-shape-per-unit is already planted for exactly this, and the visual case makes it more pressing rather than differently.

12 · Recommendation — three slices, in dependency order

  1. Give the design somewhere to declare its surface. A design.html section declaring the design sources, the token file and the screens a feature touches; the sidecar directory convention; the element vocabulary with its loud gaps; the variant grid; and the two declarations versioning needs — the token set's version and the platform baseline. Gated on project shape, which means the shape axis lands first. This buys an authored artifact that a human and an agent can both read, and nothing else can be built until something exists to diverge from.
  2. Make fidelity measurable. The measurement pass over the semantic tree and computed geometry, typed deviations that snap to a declared token, the refusal to attribute a deviation to one of its three hypotheses, and the ratified baseline the run compares against. Proven on the web surface, where the instrument already exists and the existing assertion style already does most of this. This buys the design-accurate build — it is the only slice that can fail a divergence.
  3. Cross the tool boundary. Source adapters that land a token file and renders; widening the AI port to carry an image so vision review can be an additional reviewer; the native measurement port over swift-snapshot-testing and the XCUITest accessibility hierarchy. This buys the designer's half of the workflow, and it buys Ziggy.
Do not start with the adapters. They are the most visible slice, the most demoable, and the one most exposed to other companies' roadmaps and pricing — and they write into a target that does not exist yet, so building them first means inventing the target implicitly, in an importer, in whatever shape the first tool happened to have. The second temptation to refuse is pixel diffing. It is by far the cheapest thing on this list to build, it produces an impressive-looking artifact within a day, and §7 is an argument that it does not work. Cheap and demoable are exactly the properties that get a wrong mechanism adopted before anyone measures whether it converges.

13 · Caveats on the sources and the audit

Corrected 18 Aug 2026, after a real import. Two claims in this survey about what a design tool exposes were asserted and are false. Claude Design does have a readable design document, and it also supports pushing a design system into it as a first-class, incremental, plan-then-write workflow — a direction of travel this survey never considered. Neither correction changes a decision: D-002 rested on portability, not on absence. But an implementer reading the original would have restated a checkable falsehood, which is the cost.

A third claim, that the sources emitting an artifact emit a stylesheet, was also asserted for all five and verified for none. A real export carries its values as inline attributes instead. The refusal it supports still holds — the values are text either way — but it now says what was actually observed.
  • §12 planted no slug for the third leg of the option it chose, and slice one therefore built two legs out of three. D-001 is titled a design declaration, a sidecar, and a generated view, and option D won at 23 precisely because 069 → 070 → 072 had already built all three. The suggested-next-artifacts list below then names the declaration, the sidecar, the vocabulary, the grid, the components and the versioning — and never the view. Specs 093–098 drained the list faithfully, so the omission propagated: no materialiser, no drift rule, nothing that assembles the sidecars into something a person reads. The bullet below about the exemplar proving artifacts can be authored and saying nothing about whether they can be generated turns out to have been describing a hole in this section, not a limitation of the exemplar.
  • The exemplar built that leg as a separate file, which is not what an embedded view is. examples/currency-converter/specs/001-currency-conversion/visual.html was authored as a standalone artifact and its README argued the case for one. But 072 puts the contract view inside design.html — materialiseContractViews injects a nested <spec-contract-view> into each declaration as the design verb writes, and a validate scan guards the copy. D-001's own words are "an embedded view" and "any rendering inside an artifact is materialised at generate time". So the survey was right and the exemplar was wrong, and a reader who took the exemplar as the reference would have built a ninth artifact to avoid a ninth verb. The design doc is the design doc; the view belongs in it.
  • The layer axis §10 voted to keep was not carried into the vocabulary. §4's verdict on the exemplar's seven layers is "keep the axis, not the number", and D-005 says to express the taxonomy as a grouping over the accessibility type system. What shipped in 095 carries no layer attribute at all, and its category map covers four of the eight rows in §10's own table — motion, data, requirement and accessibility resolve as unknown, though §10 found a home for every one of them. Annotations also attach to a state rather than to a named element, so a measurable assertion about a particular control — the minimum tap target on a button — cannot be written down, which is exactly the token-snapped observation §7's measurement pass was going to need.
  • TBD-visual-tracking-plan is reopened. It was planted as a question this survey declines to close, and that framing survived the slice-one drain. §4 nonetheless calls the tracking layer "the most under-represented idea in the whole field" and the layer Zeplin gets right, and both worked examples specify it well. Declining to close a question is a reason not to answer it hastily, not a reason to refuse the evidence when it accumulates on one side. The slug becomes work.
  • This survey's own first reading was wrong, and it would have justified a much larger intervention. The initial assumption was that the artifact CSP forbids images and that a visual spec would therefore need a CSP exemption of the kind courses hold. It does not: img-src 'self' has always permitted a same-origin file reference, and what is blocked is a data: URI and a remote host. The real obstacle is a missing directory convention and a .gitignore line, which is a far smaller thing. A reviewer running their own check should find the same, and the correction is recorded here rather than quietly fixed.
  • Almost nothing was prototyped. No adapter was written, no measurement pass was run, and no token file was produced from any of the five sources — so the claim that measurement converges where comparison does not remains the field's, corroborated by this repository's assertion style and not demonstrated here. What was built is a worked exemplar, examples/currency-converter/, which exercises the vocabulary and the variant grid by hand. It proves the artifacts can be authored and says nothing about whether they can be generated or checked.
  • The estate contains no real UI project. Every finding about screens is reasoned rather than dogfooded — there is no visual spec to audit because there is no visual capability, and the only UI surface in the repository is the tool rendering itself. This is the reverse of the position the observables survey was in, where 77 real gap rows made the argument. The exemplar narrows the gap and does not close it: it is a fictional project authored by the same person as the survey, so it can demonstrate that the vocabulary is expressible and cannot demonstrate that anyone else would reach for it.
  • Ziggy was not inspected. The multi-platform reasoning comes from the general Apple platform record — the focus engine, size classes, adaptive semantic colours — and not from that codebase. If its actual variant structure differs from the axis model in §8, §8 is what should move.
  • The variant model bets on a draft that says not to implement it. D-004 is marked spike for that reason. If the Resolver Module stabilises with a different model of contexts and resolution order, the mapping is rework.
  • The design tools were read, not exercised. Figma's tool list, its seat pricing and its rate limits come from its published documentation as of this date; Penpot's and Stitch's MCP servers were not run. Pricing and rate limits are the most perishable claims in this document.
  • Vision-model capability claims are secondhand and stated at a level of generality that will age badly. The specific claim that vision is blind to interaction states and motion is structural and should hold; the claim that it is reliable on colour drift and spacing is empirical and model-dependent.
  • The platform-clock argument rests on one redesign. Liquid Glass is a vivid example and a single data point; the claim that platform restyles are frequent enough to warrant a declared baseline is an extrapolation from it, not a measured rate. If Apple's cadence is once a decade, D-008 buys less than it appears to — though the cost of the declaration is low enough that the argument survives being wrong about the frequency.
  • The deprecation-window figures are secondhand. The three-to-eighteen-month range is quoted from a practitioner survey rather than confirmed against those organisations' current policies, and it is used here only to show that a window is normal practice, never to recommend a specific length.
  • The exemplar was evaluated as a document, not as a workflow. Its seven layers were held against the five clauses in §1, but nobody has authored a second handoff with it, which is where a format's real cost shows up.
