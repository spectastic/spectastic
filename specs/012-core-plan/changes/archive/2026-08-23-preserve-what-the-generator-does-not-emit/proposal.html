The declarations survive re-entry

Re-entry preserves ADRs because one requirement says so. Every other thing an author put in the design has no such sentence, and is deleted on the next run.

Status Applied Change ID 2026-08-23-preserve-what-the-generator-does-not-emit Spec 012-core-plan Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 23 Aug 2026 Targets 1 requirement Read time

FR-011 already requires re-entry to preserve every <spec-decision>, and that rule was implemented today after 012/T-002 found it had shipped unenforced for two months — scoped to ADRs, because FR-011's text is. The <spec-visual> and <spec-contract> declarations are still deleted on the next run, measured at 0 occurrences after one ordinary spectastic design <id>. That matters because two generators produce this artifact: the canonical templates/design.html scaffolds both declarations, and the kernel's own renderer emits neither — so a design authored through the documented procedure loses them the first time the kernel verb touches it. One ADD names the two elements and requires they survive.

The declarations survive re-entry

Applied

1 · Intent

The kernel renders a complete design.html from the model's fixed JSON schema and writes it over whatever was there, so content the schema has no field for cannot survive. Measured on a Draft design carrying three things a person had put in it, then one ordinary run:

BEFORE: spec-visual=1 spec-contract=1 hand-authored=1
AFTER:  spec-visual=0 spec-contract=0 hand-authored=0

This is a widening, not a new idea. FR-011 already states the principle for one element: re-entry “MUST preserve every existing <spec-decision> block… MUST NOT renumber or silently rewrite an existing ADR.” A first draft of this change claimed no preservation requirement existed at all; the adversarial pass produced FR-011 and the draft was discarded rather than reworded. What FR-011 lacks is not correctness but reach: it names one element, so an implementation satisfying it exactly — which is what landed today — preserves ADRs and keeps deleting everything else.

The content being lost is not incidental. <spec-visual> and <spec-contract> are the answers the design command's own interview exists to collect — not restatable from anything else in the repository, and treated as authoritative by every other consumer of the design. An author answers the interview, re-runs the verb for an unrelated reason, and their answers are gone with no record they were given.

Two generators, and only one scaffolds them. FR-014 makes templates/design.html the canonical procedure, and it carries both elements; the kernel's renderer emits a five-section subset with neither. So the loss is not hypothetical for a document nobody has written yet — it is the documented authoring path meeting the kernel verb. Stated carefully because a neighbouring claim does not hold and an earlier draft made it: this is not why design --visuals has never landed a file. That flag refuses because no design in the estate carries a declaration at all, which shares a cause with this loss rather than being caused by it.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • Extending the preservation obligation from <spec-decision> to the two declaration elements, named outright, so a design authored through the canonical template survives the kernel verb.

Out of scope

  • Editing FR-011. Its ADR clauses say more than the general rule does — stable ids, no renumbering, ENHANCE-in-place — and folding them into a broader sentence would lose that detail ; the two sit together, general and specific.
  • Teaching the kernel to author the interview's sections. Preserving an answer is a different obligation from producing one , and only the first stops the loss.
  • Whether the sibling generating verbs lose content the same way. They render their artifacts through the same shape and plausibly do, but that is unmeasured, and this proposal has already discarded one draft built on a claim that was reasoned rather than run — worth measuring rather than assuming.

3 · Approach

One ADD, naming the two elements outright rather than describing them by a predicate. Both tempting phrasings fail. “Content the verb did not author” is undecidable: design.html records no provenance, so a section the kernel emitted last week that a person has since rewritten is byte-indistinguishable from one it emitted this morning. “What the generator does not emit” is worse than undecidable — it is unstable, because two generators disagree about what they emit, and it would stop covering these elements the moment the kernel learns to emit them, which is the deferred work most likely to happen next. A must-tier rule that quietly narrows as the code improves is one nobody will notice failing.

The requirement therefore covers exactly the case that was measured and is silent on the rest. Hand-written prose outside these elements is still lost, and so is a hand edit inside a generated section. That is a real remaining gap, stated here rather than implied by a broader sentence this change cannot honour.

The two materialisation steps designCommand already runs strip and replace a region they did not render — the embedded views, by design (072, 099). Under a naive reading of “preserve what you did not emit” that is a violation; under this wording it is not, because a generated view is emitted by the generator on every run. Worth stating because the first draft's phrasing would have made required behaviour non-conformant.

4 · Deltas

One <spec-delta> per requirement touched. target= links to the live spec. op= is one of added | modified | removed | renamed; a missing or invalid op renders visibly broken.

Re-entry MUST preserve the interface and visual-surface declarations (<spec-contract>, <spec-visual>) carried by the existing design, and MUST NOT discard one silently.

Rationale

Extends FR-011, which states this for <spec-decision> alone and is therefore satisfied by an implementation that preserves ADRs and deletes everything else — exactly what landed when FR-011 was finally implemented. Measured on a Draft design carrying both declarations: 0 occurrences after one ordinary re-run, no warning, successful exit. Two generators produce this artifact and only one of them scaffolds these elements: FR-014's templates/design.html carries both, while the kernel's own renderer emits a five-section subset with neither — so a design authored through the canonical procedure loses its declarations the first time the kernel verb touches it. The two elements are named outright rather than described by a predicate: “what the generator does not emit” is undecidable while two generators disagree about what they emit, and would silently stop covering these elements the day the kernel learns to emit them, which is the deferred work most likely to happen next. Scoped to the declarations because they are what the loss actually costs — they are answers to interview questions, not restatable from anything else in the repository, and every other consumer of the design treats a declaration as authoritative.

4b · What this change removes

Each entry names one thing this change takes away — a clause dropped from a requirement it retypes, an obligation a sibling loses, a behaviour or affordance no requirement records. An empty register is the answer “this removes nothing”, so the common case needs no editing.

Where a delta removes something itself, its own reason and migration stay authoritative — this register is for what no delta can express.

  • The generator's freedom to own the whole document. Today the kernel renders every byte and may lay the artifact out however it likes on each run. After this it must carry unrecognised elements across, so a future change to the generated shape has to reckon with what is already in the file. That is the point, and it is still a constraint the verb did not have.
  • The reading that a design's content is entirely machine-generated. A reader could previously assume any design.html was whatever the last run produced. Afterwards it is a mixture, with nothing in the artifact marking which parts survived from an earlier one — the same absence of provenance that makes the stricter wording undecidable in the first place.

5 · Risk register

Populated by the adversarial risk pass when the proposal triggers the heuristic (touches a must-tier requirement, contains an op="removed" delta, or spans ≥2 topic prefixes). One <spec-risk> per finding. target= is required (missing renders broken). status= defaults to identified; apply refuses until each transitions to accepted, mitigated, or rejected.

The predicate is not decidable, because “the generator” is not one thing. Two generators produce design.html: the kernel's renderer emits a fixed five-section template containing neither declaration, and templates/design.html — the canonical procedure this same spec mandates in FR-014 — scaffolds both. On the template path the two elements FR-016 exists to save are generator-emitted, so an implementation that keeps deleting them is conformant. Worse, the predicate is pinned to a mutable implementation detail and shrinks in the dangerous direction: the day the kernel learns to emit those sections — this proposal's own deferred TBD-design-kernel-decision-sections — the declarations fall outside FR-016 and start vanishing again, with the must-tier rule still green and nothing to fail.

Original delta: “preserve elements of the existing design that the generator does not itself emit”. Against packages/core/src/commands/design.ts (kernel template) and templates/design.html:107,167, reachable per live FR-014.

Accepted; the delta was rewritten before it shipped. Verified directly: the template does scaffold <spec-contract> at line 107 and <spec-visual> at line 167, and no design.html in the estate carries either as a real element. The requirement now names the two elements outright, which is decidable and, more importantly, stable — the finding's sharpest point was not that the predicate was ambiguous today but that it would silently narrow tomorrow, and a rule that stops covering its own subject as the code improves is worse than one that never covered it. The two-generator fact was unstated in the draft and is now the load-bearing paragraph of §1.

FR-016's second must — “the verb MUST report what it could not keep” — has no channel, and no delta opens one. FR-012 pins the return contract to DesignResult = { html, decisionsCount, principlesCheck } and forbids the kernel from writing; FR-001 requires purity, so it cannot print either. The report would have to happen in the CLI, which discharges a must-tier guarantee at the edge rather than in the kernel — the exact P-8 gap 012/T-002 diagnosed, relocated one layer out instead of closed. The precedent is not reassuring: estimabilityBlockers was already added to DesignResult in code without FR-012 or §4 ever being modified.

Live FR-012: “MUST return a structured DesignResult ({ html, decisionsCount, principlesCheck }) … MUST NOT call ctx.fs.writeFile itself”.

Accepted, and the clause was removed rather than given a channel. The finding is right that a reporting obligation needs a field FR-012 does not have, and right that adding one by hand is how estimabilityBlockers drifted. But the clause was also not earning its place: with the requirement narrowed to two named elements there is no “could not keep” case — either the element is carried across or the implementation is non-conformant. A reporting channel for content that genuinely cannot survive belongs with the broader preservation question, which is deferred, not here.

Scope is too narrow to deliver its own headline win, and §6 task 4 is not achievable as written. Out-of-scope item 2 defers authoring the declarations, and the earlier draft that had --visuals write its own was correctly killed — so on the kernel path there is no way for a design to acquire a visual surface at all. Preservation saves a declaration that nothing in this scope can create: across the whole estate no design.html carries a real <spec-visual>, and specs/*/visual/ does not exist anywhere. Task 4's fixture can only be a hand-planted declaration, which proves preservation but not “what this unblocks end to end”. And “the three steps report completed” is unreachable in a CLI test regardless: with no render port the report is render: not-attempted.

§2 out-of-scope item 2, against §6 task 4 and packages/core/src/visual/one-step.ts.

Accepted on both halves. Verified: no design in the estate carries a real declaration, and the template path is the only thing that produces one. Task 4 is rewritten to claim what it can prove — a design carrying a declaration survives the kernel verb — and the end-to-end wording is gone, along with the “three steps completed” claim a browser-free test cannot make. The scope stays narrow deliberately: the authoring leg was killed on its own merits an hour ago because the flag cannot supply a legal tokens=, so widening to include it would re-open a question already answered. What this change is worth is now stated honestly — it protects the documented authoring path from the kernel verb, and does not on its own make --visuals work.

Corpus contradiction. KB-0001@2026-07-25 states that a must-tier decision may not rest on an assumed fact without an explicit accepted risk. FR-016 is must-tier and its rationale asserts as fact that the declarations' “loss is why --visuals has never landed a file”. That causation is unverified and, checked against source, wrong: --visuals refuses because no declaration is found, and no design has ever carried one to lose — the loss and the refusal share a cause rather than one causing the other. §5's register was empty and §7 declared “None outstanding”. This is the same failure the live spec already paid for: §6 records the parse5 walker as an assumption, must-tier FR-011 rested on it unrecorded, and 012/T-002 found no such parse exists.

KB-0001@2026-07-25 §“The grounding discipline”, against the original FR-016 rationale and the empty risk log.

Accepted; the claim was false and is removed rather than softened. Checked against source: the refusal comes from hasVisualSurface finding nothing, and nothing has ever been there to delete, so the two are siblings rather than cause and effect. §1 now says so explicitly — naming the neighbouring claim that does not hold, since an earlier draft made it and a reader deserves to know which way the causation runs. The citation is verified: KB-0001 resolves to spectastic/spectastic · spectastic-concepts · 001-foundations at edition 2026-07-25 and carries that rule.
