Extend the profile principle catalog — versioning, supply-chain, accessibility

Applied

1 · Intent

The engineering-principles survey's §6 catalog is the set of shape-of-system principles worth seeding into a downstream project's constitution. The lean-four pass took the agent-facing core and, after its adversarial review, deferred the maturity markers — semantic versioning, supply-chain hygiene, accessibility — to keep the standard-tier skeleton lean. This pass lands those markers, having learned the lesson the lean-four review taught: tier by where each earns its keep, don't blanket-seed at standard.

So versioning and supply-chain hygiene join standard — both matter the moment a team ships releases and pulls dependencies, which is what "standard" (a maintained team product) means. Their heavier forms — an SBOM and build attestation, formal audited WCAG conformance — are enterprise concerns: they answer a regulated or consumer-verification obligation that a team product doesn't yet carry. Accessibility appears only at enterprise: the lean-four pass explicitly found a standard-tier a11y principle to be noise for the many non-UI projects, and rather than re-open that, this pass seeds accessibility where a formal obligation is real and omits the ambiguous baseline entirely.

Everything here is a principle: advisory, per the P-8 / T-009 discipline that a principle nudges and a gate guarantees. The supply-chain enforce category already gives the hygiene/provenance principles their teeth at verified/enterprise; the versioning and accessibility principles ship as the nudge, their durable checks left to later enforce/validate phases.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • ADD two principles to the standard preset in spectastic-profiles.json — semantic versioning and supply-chain hygiene — repeated into the verified and enterprise presets (cumulative-by-repetition; the manifest has no cross-profile inheritance).
  • ADD two principles to the enterprise preset only — supply-chain provenance (SBOM + attestation) and accessibility conformance (formal WCAG) — the heavier, regulated forms that a team product doesn't yet carry.
  • Extend the existing drift-guard test so the new repeated names (semver, supply-chain hygiene) are covered, and update any per-tier principle-count assertions in the profile tests.
  • Rebuild the bundle so packages/cli/_bundled/spectastic-profiles.json matches; keep the profile/compose tests green.

Out of scope

  • A standard-tier baseline accessibility principle. Deliberately omitted, not deferred-with-intent-to-add: the lean-four pass found it noise for non-UI standard projects, and enterprise-only formal WCAG is the placement this pass commits to. Its own fresh slug (not this proposal's TBD-profile-principle-catalog-next, which this pass closes) so it isn't orphaned; reopen only if a UI-detecting signal or a user-facing profile axis makes a scoped baseline meaningful.
  • The verified-tier rows the lean-four pass also parked under TBD-profile-principle-catalog-next — idempotency / safe-retries and integration-first testing. This pass handles only the standard-tier maturity markers + the enterprise forms, so those two are re-deferred to their own slug rather than silently dropped when this TBD closes.
  • Machine-checkable teeth for the versioning and accessibility principles (a semver-tag check, an axe/WCAG gate). Principles are advisory here by design; the supply-chain principles already have their enforce category, the other two get theirs in later phases.
  • New AGENTS.md lines or any numbered FR change. FR-004 requires AGENTS to point at enforcement commands, not restate advisory prose; FR-001..FR-009 govern the mechanism, unchanged.

3 · Approach

Pure data, exactly as the lean-four pass. combinedPrinciples() composes a profile's seeded principles.html as base + thisProfile.principles, deduped by name, with no standard→verified→enterprise inheritance — so "at standard, cumulative upward" is achieved by repeating the entry in the standard, verified, and enterprise arrays. Semver and supply-chain hygiene therefore appear in all three; supply-chain provenance and accessibility appear in enterprise alone. Composed lengths stay modest — standard grows by two, enterprise by four — well within the lean skeleton 041 protects.

Statements are written in the manifest's established voice — one or two imperative, agent-aware sentences, no attribution, no internal ids — so they read as a project's own constitution once seeded. The drift guard the lean-four pass added is name-based, so it automatically covers the two new repeated principles; the task extends its explicit assertions and the per-tier counts, nothing structural.

On SC-002. The two enterprise-only additions (provenance, accessibility) are new enterprise-vs-verified differentiators that trace to none of the five named axes — the same strain the lean-four pass raised for observability/SLOs. As there, read SC-002's trace to axis differences (e.g. …) as illustrative: the criterion's teeth are that pairwise diffs are non-empty, which these additions strengthen, not that every difference maps to a named axis. The durable fix is the deferred contracts/interfaces axis; until it lands, the existing differentiators (the verification principle at verified, the enterprise set) keep the pairwise diffs non-empty and SC-002 is met.

4 · Deltas

Two additive data/content deltas — one for the standard-tier additions (repeated up), one for the enterprise-only additions. Both target a manifest key, not a numbered requirement (profile data governed by FR-003), so neither carries an embedded <spec-requirement>; the post-state is the seeded statement itself, shown in the <spec-diff>.

What's added: two principles appended to the standard preset's principles[] in spectastic-profiles.json, repeated into verified and enterprise so the ladder stays cumulative.

Semantic versioning — Release versions are MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH, a compatibility contract: a breaking change bumps MAJOR, a backward-compatible feature MINOR, a fix PATCH. Dependents and agents then reason mechanically about whether an upgrade is safe. Supply-chain hygiene — Pin, audit, and prune dependencies; adding one is a reviewed decision, not a reflex. Agents pull transitive forests, so bound the blast radius at the manifest.
Rationale — why standard

Both matter the moment a team ships releases others depend on and pulls dependencies an agent will extend — which is what the standard tier (a maintained team product) already assumes. Semver lets a dependent or an agent reason mechanically about upgrade safety; hygiene bounds the transitive blast radius an autonomous agent widens by reflex. Repeated into verified + enterprise for the cumulative ladder.

What's added: two principles appended to the enterprise preset's principles[] only — the heavier, regulated forms.

Supply-chain provenance — Publish an SBOM and a build attestation for every release, so a consumer can verify what went into an artifact and where it came from. Accessibility conformance — A user-facing product meets formal, audited WCAG 2.x AA — for such a product a regulated obligation, not a finishing touch. A service or CLI with no user-facing surface satisfies this trivially.
Rationale — why enterprise only

Provenance (SBOM + attestation) matches the supply-chain enforce category enterprise already carries — a principle to name the obligation the gate enforces. Accessibility lands only here because the lean-four review found a standard-tier baseline to be noise for the many non-UI projects; enterprise is where a formal, audited WCAG obligation is real and worth stating. Neither is repeated downward — they are genuine enterprise-tier differentiators.

5 · Risk register

From the opt-in adversarial pass — this change seeds the constitution of every downstream project, so it earned a skeptic. All three findings were mitigated in review; each response records what changed in the proposal.

The accessibility principle reproduces at enterprise the very non-UI-noise the proposal avoids at standard: its second sentence was an unscoped blanket ("a regulated obligation for released software"), constitutional noise for the many non-UI enterprise projects (backend services, CLIs, data platforms) — enterprise is not synonymous with user-facing. The placement also deviates from the source doc (D-001 recommends a standard baseline) and rests on the survey's own lowest-confidence row (§7: the a11y→tier mapping is "synthesis … lower confidence than the security rungs").

<spec-delta op="added" target="enterprise/provenance-and-accessibility-principles"> — the former phrase Accessibility is a regulated obligation for released software, not a finishing touch.

Mitigated — scoped. The statement's second clause is now bounded to the user-facing case ("for such a product a regulated obligation … a service or CLI with no user-facing surface satisfies this trivially"), so a non-UI enterprise constitution seeds it as a no-op. The deviation from D-001's standard-baseline recommendation is the deliberate, interview-anchored choice (enterprise-only, sidestepping the standard-tier noise); the low-confidence caveat is why it lands as an advisory principle, not a gate.

The enterprise-only additions create new enterprise-vs-verified differentiators that trace to none of the profile's named axes (verification, enforcement, toggles, grounding, frameworks) — the identical strain the prior lean-four pass raised and mitigated for observability/SLOs — and the first draft was silent on SC-002 despite manufacturing exactly those non-axis-traceable pairwise diffs.

SC-002 (live spec 041) — pairwise diffs of the seeded principles.html … are non-empty and trace to axis differences.

Mitigated — addressed in §3. §3 now reads SC-002's "e.g." as illustrative (as the sibling did): the criterion's teeth are non-empty pairwise diffs, which these additions strengthen; the durable fix is the deferred contracts/interfaces axis. Existing differentiators keep the diffs non-empty, so SC-002 is met.

Scope was too narrow with a circular defer: the out-of-scope item re-deferred the standard-tier a11y baseline to TBD-profile-principle-catalog-next — the slug this proposal resolves — orphaning it on apply; and that same slug (per the lean-four sibling) also parked the verified-tier idempotency/safe-retries and integration-first rows, which the first draft dropped silently, neither delivering nor re-deferring them.

§2 Out of scope — the a11y-baseline item's former defer-to="TBD-profile-principle-catalog-next" (the slug this proposal itself closes), and the absent verified-tier rows.

Mitigated — repointed. The a11y baseline now defers to a fresh TBD-standard-a11y-baseline, and a new out-of-scope item re-defers the verified-tier rows (idempotency/safe-retries, integration-first) to TBD-profile-catalog-verified-tier — so closing this TBD orphans nothing.
