Specification · 071-contract-promotion

Contract promotion

When a change lands, its proposed contract becomes the effective one — written, archived, and refused rather than silently overwritten.

Status Accepted Spec ID 071-contract-promotion Owner Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Reviewers — Created 31 Jul 2026 Principlesv1.9.0 Smallest demoableLanding a change whose spec carries a proposed contract writes the effective contract at its declared path and archives the proposal — one command, no manual copy. Read time

The two-state convention (070) only holds if something moves a contract between states. Left manual, it degrades exactly the way every hand-maintained copy does: the proposal stays behind as a second live file, or the effective contract is edited directly and the proposal is forgotten. This spec adds the transition — a promotion step at the point a change lands, mirroring the fold-and-archive the apply verb already performs on a change proposal. It writes the proposed contract to its declared effective path, archives the spec-local copy so two live copies never coexist, and refuses rather than silently overwriting when the effective contract has moved since the proposal was authored — the case where two specs are in flight against one interface. The operation is atomic and idempotent: it either writes and archives or does neither, and running it twice is indistinguishable from running it once. For authors landing an interface change, and the reviewers who have to trust that what shipped is what was approved.

1 · Context

Spec 070 establishes that a contract has two states — proposed beside its spec, effective at the project's declared path — and that exactly one is authoritative at a time. That invariant is only as good as the mechanism that moves a contract between them. Without one, the convention survives on discipline: someone must remember to copy the file and delete the original, at the moment a change lands, correctly, every time.

The tool already performs this exact shape of move elsewhere. Applying a change proposal folds its content into the live spec and relocates the source into an archive directory, so the proposal cannot later be mistaken for the current requirement. A contract promotion is the same operation over a different pair of files, and should behave the same way — including the part people forget to specify, which is what happens when the destination has changed underneath you.

That last case is not hypothetical. Two specs can be in flight against one interface, each carrying a proposed contract written against a different baseline. Whichever lands second would, under a naive copy, silently discard the first. Whose problem: the author who loses work without being told, and the consumer who receives an interface nobody approved.

2 · User scenarios & testing

Each story MUST be independently testable: if you implemented only US1, you would still have a viable MVP.

US1 · Landing a change promotes its contract P1

As an author, I want my proposed contract to become the effective one when the change lands so that the interface ships without a manual copy step I can forget.

Acceptance: After landing, the declared effective path holds the proposed contract's content byte-for-byte, and the spec-local proposal is no longer at its original location.

US2 · Exactly one live copy afterwards P1

As a reviewer, I want the proposal archived rather than left in place so that nobody can mistake a landed proposal for a pending one, or edit the wrong file.

Acceptance: After promotion, no readable contract remains at the proposed location; the archived copy is retained and identifiable.

US3 · A conflicting promotion refuses P2

As an author, I want promotion to stop when the effective contract has changed since I authored my proposal so that I am told about the conflict instead of silently overwriting someone else's landed interface.

Acceptance: Given an effective contract that differs from the baseline the proposal was written against, promotion exits non-zero, writes nothing, archives nothing, and names the conflict.

Edge cases

  • Promotion run twice — the second run must be a no-op, not a failure and not a duplicate archive entry.
  • No proposed contract at all: the overwhelming majority of changes. Promotion must do nothing and say nothing.
  • A declared effective path whose parent directory does not yet exist — the first contract a project ever ships.
  • A proposal whose declared path changed after it was authored, so the baseline is a file at a different location.
  • Promotion interrupted partway — the destination must not be left holding a partial file.
  • A spec carrying several proposed contracts where one conflicts: none should be promoted.

3 · Requirements

Conformance keywords (MUST, SHOULD, MAY) follow RFC 2119.

Functional

When a change carrying a proposed contract lands, the tool MUST write that contract's content to the effective path the design declares.

Rationale

The transition is the mechanism that makes the two-state convention real rather than aspirational.

Promotion MUST archive the spec-local proposal so that no readable contract remains at the proposed location.

Rationale

Retains provenance while restoring the one-live-copy invariant, mirroring how a folded change proposal is archived rather than deleted.

Promotion MUST refuse — writing nothing and archiving nothing — when the effective contract differs from the baseline the proposal was authored against.

Rationale

Two specs in flight against one interface is the case that loses work silently. Refusing is the only outcome that neither discards a landed change nor guesses at a merge.

Promotion MUST be atomic across all of a change's contracts: either every contract is written and archived, or none is.

Rationale

A half-promoted change leaves the estate in a state no invariant describes, and leaves the author to reconstruct what happened.

Promotion MUST be idempotent: running it again after a successful promotion MUST make at most 0 further changes and MUST NOT fail.

A change carrying no proposed contract MUST be unaffected — at most 0 files written, and no output about contracts.

Rationale

Most changes touch no interface. The step must be invisible to them, or it becomes noise that trains people to ignore it.

A refusal SHOULD name the conflicting path and, where determinable, the spec whose landed change moved it.

The tool SHOULD offer a dry-run that reports what promotion would write and archive, changing at most 0 files.

Non-functional

Promotion MUST be deterministic — given the same inputs it produces the same result, with at most 0 network calls and no dependence on wall-clock time.

Promotion SHOULD add under 200 ms to the command that hosts it, for a change carrying up to 10 contracts.

Out of scope (deferred to other slices)

  • Refreshing any rendered view of the contract after promotion.
  • Telling a consuming repository that the effective contract changed.
  • Linting the promoted contract or diffing it for breaking changes before it lands.
  • Merging two proposals against one interface rather than refusing — deliberately not attempted, since a schema merge is not a text merge.

4 · Data model

No new persisted entity. Promotion moves existing files between the two locations 070 defines, and needs one piece of state it does not have today.

Entities

Promotion baseline
What the effective contract looked like when the proposal was authored — the reference FR-003 compares against to detect a conflict. Recorded when the proposal is written, not when it lands.
Archived proposal
The retained spec-local contract after promotion, kept for provenance and never readable as a live contract.

The baseline is the only genuinely new state here, and it is what makes FR-003 possible at all: without it, "has the destination changed?" can only be answered as "does it differ from what I am about to write", which is true of every legitimate promotion.

5 · Success criteria

Technology-agnostic, measurable outcomes. These are how we know it worked.

Landing a change with a proposed contract leaves exactly 1 readable contract for that interface — at the declared effective path — in 100% of fixtures.

A conflicting promotion exits non-zero and leaves the working tree byte-identical to its pre-run state — at most 0 files written, at most 0 archived.

Re-running promotion after success changes at most 0 files and exits zero.

Across the existing spec estate, where no spec carries a proposed contract, promotion writes at most 0 files and adds under 200 ms to the hosting command.

6 · Assumptions

Contracts are small text files, so writing one atomically is a write-and-rename rather than a streaming concern.

The project is under version control, so an archived proposal and a promoted contract are both recoverable if a promotion turns out to have been wrong.

Concurrent promotions on one working tree are not a case worth designing for — the conflicting-baseline refusal covers the sequential version of the same hazard, which is the one that actually occurs.

7 · Open questions

None outstanding — refuse-rather-than-merge, atomicity, and idempotence were settled when the two-state convention was chosen; the baseline is the mechanism that follows from them.
