Success criteria

A criterion names who is better off, and what would prove it was not.

Status Draft Spec ID 108-success-criteria Owner Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Reviewers Unassigned Created 19 Aug 2026 Principlesv1.9.0 Smallest demoableOne criterion, authored in the new shape, refused by the checker because its actor is an artifact. Read time

A success criterion is supposed to say who is better off and by how much. Across 102 specs and 382 criteria, 2% name a person, none says how it would be measured, and 37% assert a count of zero. Most restate the requirement they sit beside, so a passing test suite satisfies them automatically and they can never fail. The cause is structural rather than careless: no requirement anywhere owns the shape of a criterion and no check reads one — the whole contract is two sentences of command markdown, which is the one place this project already says a guarantee may not live. This gives a criterion a shape whose slots each make one failure hard to write, and gates the five that a machine can settle.

1 · Context

A success criterion is the artifact’s claim about the world: someone is better off, by a stated amount, and here is what would show they were not. Nothing in this project has ever checked that claim.

Measured across every spec in the estate — 382 criteria in 102 specs:

Property a criterion should haveCriteria that have it
Names a person or organisation as its subject11 of 382 — 2%
Says how it would be measured0 of 382 — none, ever
Expresses a single outcome (no conjunction)156 of 382 — 41%
Avoids asserting a bare 0 or 100%237 of 382 — 63%

The subjects are An image, the number captured, the verb. A criterion whose subject is the tool restates the requirement beside it, so the test closing the requirement closes the criterion too. These are not badly written; they are unfalsifiable.

The cause is structural. No requirement owns the shape of a criterion and no validate rule reads one: the contract is two sentences of command markdown and a template placeholder. The placeholder is good — authors had a correct example and drifted anyway, because nothing ever disagreed. Command markdown is where triage T-009 and P-8 say a guarantee may not live.

2 · User scenarios & testing

Each story MUST be independently testable: if you implemented only US1, you would still have a viable MVP.

US1 · A criterion that can fail P1

As a spec author, I want to be stopped when I write a criterion the implementation satisfies by existing so that the criteria I ship describe an outcome somebody could be denied rather than a restatement of the build.

Acceptance: A criterion naming an artifact as its actor, or carrying no way to observe it, is refused at the boundary with the slot named. One naming a person, with a threshold, a baseline and a way to observe it, passes.

US2 · What would prove this wrong P2

As a reviewer, I want to read what observation would falsify a criterion, and which requirements it validates so that I can tell a claim under test from a claim nobody could lose.

Acceptance: Every criterion carries a falsifying observation and names at least one requirement it validates. A requirement reachable from no criterion is reported.

US3 · The estate does not fall over P3

As a maintainer, I want to adopt the contract without 382 criteria failing on the day it lands so that the gate is something people fix forward through rather than switch off.

Acceptance: The checks bind new and amended specs from a stated version and leave earlier specs untouched. Adopting the element changes no existing spec’s conformance index, budget gauge or verify trace.

Edge cases

  • A criterion whose outcome is genuinely mechanical — a byte comparison — and has no human population to sample.
  • A threshold that is honestly zero, because one occurrence is a defect.
  • A project with no baseline at all, because the thing has never been done before.
  • An author who satisfies every slot and still writes a criterion that restates the requirement — the case a machine cannot settle.

3 · Requirements

Conformance keywords (MUST, SHOULD, MAY) follow RFC 2119.

Functional

A success criterion MUST be a <spec-criterion> element, distinct from <spec-requirement>.

Rationale

A requirement is an obligation on the system; a criterion is a measured observation about the world. Sharing one element is what let criteria be written in the requirement’s voice and inherit its habits — including its RFC 2119 keyword, which is how “the verb exits with a stated reason” came to be filed as a criterion. Separating the element is what makes every rule below expressible without constraining requirements.

A criterion MUST declare an actor, and that actor MUST NOT name an artifact of the system.

Rationale

The highest-value slot: 98% of the estate fails it, and it is what separates an outcome from a restatement. Declared rather than inferred from the sentence, per 073 FR-003’s declaration-over-inference — a glossary-based subject parse was the first design and only 1 of 102 specs has a glossary, so the check would have been unimplementable as written.

A criterion MUST state two thresholds: the level below which the feature has failed, and the level it is aiming at.

Rationale

One number can only say “unbroken”. Two say “unbroken” and “good” — the survival/success distinction a single figure silently collapses, usually onto whichever the author had in mind.

A criterion MUST state the baseline it improves on, and MUST be able to state that no baseline is established.

Rationale

“≥ 90%” is unreadable without knowing whether today is 40% or 89%. The explicit no-baseline value matters as much as the number: it separates a measurement nobody took from one nobody could have taken, and collapsing them is how a first-of-its-kind feature acquires a fictional past.

A criterion whose observation is made by a person MUST carry a meter naming what is observed, over what window, by whom. A criterion observed mechanically MUST instead name the observation point, and MUST NOT be required to carry a sampling frame.

Rationale

A scale with no meter is a number that looks measurable and is not — the slot no criterion in the estate has ever carried. But the gate is on the observation, not the actor: most of this project’s own criteria are settled by a command, and requiring a sampling frame there would invent a review log nobody keeps. That is the fiction the observability survey refused when it declined to author 77 objectives for a tool with no traffic, and the shape-gate is 093’s D-006 applied one artifact over.

A criterion MUST state the observation that would prove the feature failed.

Rationale

The cheapest defence against a criterion that cannot lose. An author who cannot name what would falsify it has written a description, and the slot forces the discovery at authoring time rather than at the review where it is too late to matter.

A criterion MUST declare the requirements it validates, and a requirement reachable from no criterion MUST be reported.

Rationale

Traceability in the direction that finds gaps. A criterion pointing at nothing is an orphan metric; a requirement nobody agreed how to judge is the more expensive omission, because it stays invisible until delivery.

A criterion MUST express a single outcome.

Rationale

59% of the estate carries a conjunction, and a criterion joining two outcomes cannot be passed or failed — only half-met, which no gate can act on. The singular rule is ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148’s and it is the one defect here with an unambiguous fix.

A criterion MUST NOT use an RFC 2119 keyword.

Rationale

The indicative mood is the point: “reviewers confirm…”, not “the system MUST allow reviewers to confirm…”. An author reaching for MUST has written a requirement, and this is the cheapest possible detector for that mistake.

A criterion’s priority MUST mean only whether missing it blocks release.

Rationale

On a requirement, priority ranks an obligation. On a criterion it could mean a less important outcome or a softer target — and the second is already carried by FR-003’s thresholds. Narrowing it stops the attribute duplicating them.

A criterion asserting a threshold of 0 or 100%, or two equal thresholds, MUST carry a stated justification.

Rationale

37% of the estate asserts a bare zero. Some are right — one corrupted byte is a defect — and the rest are a reflex that makes a criterion unfalsifiable, since the first occurrence fails it and nobody intends to ship on that basis. The justification separates the two, and costs the honest case one sentence.

Adopting the criterion element MUST NOT change any existing spec’s conformance index, size gauge or verify trace.

Rationale

Four consumers read <spec-requirement> and would silently disagree with a criterion that is no longer one: the verify view’s trace would empty, the conformance index would drop every criterion, and the size gauge’s requirement count would fall by the criterion count in every spec at once. Silent, in all four. Naming this as a requirement is what stops the element landing before its readers are taught.

The checks MUST bind specs authored or amended from a stated version onward, and MUST NOT reopen specs authored before it.

Rationale

382 criteria at 2% conformance is not a backlog anyone fixes in one pass, and a gate that fails the whole estate on day one is a gate somebody disables. Forward-only binding is this project’s own mechanism, already written into principles, which binds from a version and explicitly does not retroactively reopen what came before.

The two judgments a machine cannot settle — whether a criterion’s vocabulary is stakeholder-facing, and whether a passing test suite would satisfy it automatically — SHOULD be judged by the existing critic panel rather than a new harness.

Rationale

Both are the interesting checks and neither is deterministic. 033 already ships a panel that runs critics from distinct lenses, arbitrates categorical findings by majority and cannot judge its own output; a second harness would duplicate it and drift. Should-tier because a graded judgment is advisory by nature — it informs an author, it does not gate a commit.

Non-functional

The checks in FR-002, FR-005, FR-007, FR-008 and FR-009 MUST reach a verdict in at most 0 model calls.

Rationale

These five gate a commit, and a gate that needs a network or a key is one that fails closed on a plane. It also keeps the boundary honest: what a machine can settle is gated, what it cannot is advised, and the split is visible in which check needs a model.

Validating a spec carrying criteria MUST add at most 0 filesystem reads beyond those its existing rules already perform.

Rationale

Every slot these rules read is an attribute or the element’s own content, so the whole contract is decidable from the document already in memory. Stating the budget as zero is what stops a later cross-file check — resolving a validated requirement id, say — being added without anyone noticing it changed the cost class.

Out of scope (deferred to other slices)

  • Migrating the 382 existing criteria. Forward-only binding is what makes this optional rather than urgent; whether it is ever worth doing is a separate judgment about 100 specs mostly already delivered.
  • A stakeholder-facing glossary. One spec in the estate has one, so making it load-bearing here would have imported a second migration into this one.
  • Rejecting an unknown spec- element. Found while checking whether this element could be authored before its rules existed — it can, because nothing validates the vocabulary, which also means a typo’d element name passes silently today.
  • Rendering the meter and the falsifying observation in verify.html. FR-012 requires the trace not to break; showing the new slots is a further step.

4 · Data model

One entity, and the relation that makes it traceable.

Entities

Criterion
A claim that a named actor is measurably better off. Carries an actor, a scale with a failure level and a target level, a baseline, a way to observe it, and the observation that would falsify it. Distinct from a requirement, which is an obligation on the system rather than a claim about the world.
Validation
The relation from a criterion to the requirements it judges. Each criterion validates at least 1 requirement; each requirement should be validated by at least 1 criterion, and one validated by 0 is a reportable gap.

Two invariants: a criterion’s actor is never an artifact, and every criterion carries exactly 1 outcome.

5 · Success criteria

Authored in the shape this spec defines. Two consequences are visible now and are the point rather than an accident: these do not appear in the conformance index below, and the size gauge excludes them — the breakage FR-012 exists to prevent, seen from inside.

A spec author is stopped before commit when a criterion they wrote could not fail — share of criteria reaching main with an artifact actor, or with no way to observe them, ≤ 5% (target 0%), from 98% across the 382 criteria measured today.

Observed at

spectastic validate in the pre-commit gate, counted per criterion added or amended in a spec-touching commit. Mechanical, so no sampling frame: every such commit is seen, not a sample of them.

Falsified by

Criteria naming the verb, an image or a bare count continuing to reach main after the gate is on — which would mean the slots are satisfiable without changing what an author actually writes.

A reviewer can tell which requirements nobody agreed how to judge — requirements reachable from no criterion, in a spec authored under this contract, reported rather than silent: share reported 100% (target 100%), from 0% today, where the relation does not exist.

Observed at

The validate run over a spec, comparing declared validates targets against the spec’s own requirement ids. Both thresholds are 100% because the check is a total scan rather than a sample — the justification FR-011 asks for.

Falsified by

A requirement with no validating criterion passing a validate run unremarked.

A maintainer adopts the contract without the estate failing — existing specs whose conformance index, size gauge or verify trace changes on the day it lands: 0 (target 0), from 102 specs at risk.

Observed at

A full-corpus spectastic validate and a byte comparison of every regenerated verify.html, before and after. A zero floor is justified because any non-zero value is a silent regression in a document nobody would reread.

Falsified by

Any pre-existing spec reporting a new finding, or a verify trace losing rows, after the element lands.

A spec author learns that a criterion restates its requirement while still drafting it — share of such criteria surfaced before review ≥ 60% (target 85%), from no established baseline.

Meter

Criteria flagged by the critic panel, compared against those a human reviewer later objects to on the same spec; counted over the first 15 specs authored under this contract, classified by the spec’s own reviewer at review close. A person judges the judgement here, which is why this one carries a sampling frame and the three above do not.

Falsified by

Reviewers routinely raising restated criteria the panel passed, which would mean the graded check reads as coverage while providing none.

6 · Assumptions

Structure changes what authors write. A correct example did not stop the drift to 2%, so the bet is that a slot which must be filled and can be checked will. Weak evidence, which is why SC-001 measures what reaches main rather than what authors intend.

A tool’s own specs are a hard case for this format, not the case it is designed for. Most criteria here are mechanically observed, so most carry an observation point rather than a meter; the richer shape earns its keep downstream, where there are users. Stating this prevents judging the format by how it reads here.

7 · Open questions

None outstanding. The one that arose during the interview — how an actor is checked when only 1 of 102 specs carries a glossary the original design assumed — is answered by declaring the actor rather than parsing for it (FR-002), and the glossary convention itself is deferred rather than made a prerequisite.
