Change proposal · 093-design-visual-section

A screen has a name

Give the quantifier a referent, and give the requirement a check. It has had neither, in either wording.

Status Applied Change ID 2026-08-14-a-screen-has-a-name Spec 093-design-visual-section Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 14 Aug 2026 Targets 2 requirements — 1 modified, 1 added Read time

FR-014 obliges a design to "introduce each screen it declares that surface addresses". A design declares a path; screens live in the sidecar; so the quantifier ranges over nothing. Two rounds of proposals argued over that sentence's wording and neither noticed the larger fact: FR-014 has never been implemented — no rule, no scan, nothing in the history. This gives the quantifier a referent by letting a declaration name the screens it addresses, obliges the list to be complete rather than non-empty, and makes the obligation checkable against the material the design points at.

A screen has a name

Applied

1 · Intent

FR-014 requires a design to introduce each screen its surface addresses. The declaration it must read is <spec-visual>, which carries attributes and free prose — a token-set path, a variants path, a screens path, contexts, a source. It names no screens. Screens are declared in the sidecar the path points at. So the set the requirement quantifies over is empty, always, and the obligation is satisfied by any design at all.

The exemplar shows what that costs. Its design declares screens="specs/001-currency-conversion/visual" and names not one screen in authored prose — the ids pairs and convert-unmapped appear only inside the generated view, which is the tool's own output. Its authored text says "One screen, ten states"; the sidecar holds three screens and the view projects three. A reader is told something false about the feature and nothing reports it.

Two rounds of proposals have argued about this sentence — one enumerating from the directory, one turning the obligation around to read the declaration — and the second was justified partly by a budget it did not need to respect. Neither noticed the fact that makes both arguments moot: FR-014 has no implementation and never has. No rule under packages/schema/src/rules/, no scan in validate.ts, and git log -S"introduce each screen" returns nothing. Validating the exemplar fires three visual rules and none of them is this one. The correction did not repeal an effect; there was no effect.

So this change does the two things the previous rounds skipped. It gives the quantifier something real to range over — a declaration may name the screens it addresses — and it obliges the check to exist.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • Letting <spec-visual> name the screens a feature addresses, so "each screen it declares" has a referent inside the artifact rather than in a directory or in the tool's own output.
  • Reading the declared material to verify the named set is complete and resolvable — not to derive it. The walk is already paid for: materialiseVisualViews runs on every document carrying a declaration, in the same pass.
  • Restating FR-014 against that referent, so the obligation is determinate — a named screen must resolve, and a design that names none while declaring a surface is the case the requirement exists to catch.
  • Obliging the check to exist, and building it. A must-tier requirement that nothing enforces is the defect this change is really about.

Out of scope

  • Treating the directory as the authority — deriving the addressed set from whatever is on disk, which is what the first round did and what 094 §4 forbids. The declaration says which screens are claimed; reading the material is how that claim is checked. Those are different acts, and only the first is refused. defer-to=never.
  • Deriving the referent from the generated view. It is the tool's output, and treating it as evidence of what an author declared makes the tool grade its own homework. defer-to=never.
  • Requiring prose about each screen — a description, a story, a rationale. FR-014's original reach. Naming is checkable; whether a sentence is a real introduction is not, and a check that cannot tell would either pass everything or become a word count. defer-to=TBD-screen-introduction-quality.
  • Whether a screen's meaning belongs in the design or beside the screen in the sidecar. A real question, and answering it is not needed to give this requirement a referent. defer-to=TBD-screen-meaning-location.

A reference is not a copy. The declaration names which screens a feature addresses; the sidecar remains the sole place a screen is defined — its states, annotations, renders. That is the same split 070 already uses for contracts, where a design names a contract and the contract file holds it.

4 · Deltas

What changes: new requirement. The declaration gains the ability to name the screens it addresses — the referent FR-014 has always lacked.

A visual declaration MUST be able to name the screens the feature addresses, by the identifier each screen declares for itself, and naming one MUST NOT require restating anything else that screen's own declaration holds.

Rationale

Without this there is nothing for FR-014 to quantify over. The declaration carries a path; the screens live behind it; and the only per-screen structure that has ever appeared inside a design is the generated view, which is the tool's output and cannot serve as evidence of what an author declared.

By the screen's own identifier, so the design and the sidecar agree by construction rather than by an author keeping two spellings in step. The second clause is the guard against this becoming duplication, and says else deliberately — the identifier is itself something the screen declares, so a blanket prohibition on restating what the screen holds would forbid the very reference this requirement creates. Everything beyond the name — states, annotations, renders, contexts — stays in the sidecar, which is where FR-005 points and what 094 establishes as the material's home.

Able to name, not obliged to — the obligation belongs in FR-014, where the condition that triggers it already lives. Splitting them keeps this requirement about the vocabulary and that one about when it must be used.

What changes: the obligation is restated against the referent FR-015 supplies, and the check is required to exist rather than left as prose nothing enforces.

A design whose visual declaration carries shape="screens" MUST name every screen the declared material holds, and every named screen MUST resolve to a screen in that material. A screen present in the material and not named, and a name that resolves to nothing, MUST each be reported as an error, naming the screen and the design. The named set is the authority for what the feature claims; the material is read only to check that claim, never to derive it.

Rationale

A declared path tells a reader that screens exist. Which ones this feature addresses is what a reader came for, and the adjacent contract declaration already requires authored reasoning beside its path rather than a bare pointer.

Four things changed and each was a defect. The obligation ranges over named screens (FR-015) rather than "each screen it declares", which had no referent and was therefore satisfiable by naming nothing — the previous wording's only observable effect. It obliges the list to be complete rather than merely non-empty: an earlier draft of this change said "at least 1", which would have let the exemplar name convert alone and leave the one-versus-three discrepancy that started all three rounds still unreported. The antecedent is stated as shape="screens" rather than left as "declaring a visual surface", because an explicit shape="none" is itself a design declaring its visual surface — FR-007 requires that record — and a completeness floor read loosely would turn an honest no-surface declaration into an error. T-001 flagged that ambiguity a round ago and nobody chose; this chooses. And the check is required explicitly, following FR-002's precedent of naming the enforcement surface in the requirement, because this obligation has run through two proposal rounds with no implementation at all, so the wording was never what was wrong with it.

The last sentence is the one that keeps 094 §4 intact while letting the check work. Deriving the addressed set from whatever is on disk is what the first round did and what the convention forbids: an undeclared file would become part of the feature by existing. Reading that material to test a claim the author already made is a different act, and the read costs nothing new — materialiseVisualViews already walks the declared path on every document carrying a declaration, in the same pass.

5 · Risk register

Findings from the adversarial pass. Statuses stay identified until the author dispositions them.

Run before apply. This is the third attempt at FR-014 and the critic was briefed with that, and with the six cards or author claims already found overstated in this recovery. It confirmed the central premise — FR-014 has no implementation — and found four defects, three of which changed the artifact before you read it. It also overturned a doubt the author raised: "MUST be able to name" is established house style here (FR-005, FR-012) and is checkable as incoherence rather than absence, so it is not a finding.

"At least 1" would upgrade an unchecked prose claim into an unchecked structured one, and still miss the defect all three rounds were written for

The draft obliged naming at least 1 screen. The motivating defect is that the exemplar's prose says "One screen, ten states" while its sidecar declares three — under that wording an author who names convert alone passes, and the one-versus-three discrepancy stays unreported. Worse, FR-015 puts a hand-maintained list of ids inside the very element the materialiser injects a generated list of the same ids into: two inventories three lines apart, one drift-checked, the other compared to nothing. Today the false claim is prose a reader discounts; it would have become a structured attribute that looks checked.

Draft response — the requirement was rewritten. It now obliges the named set to be complete against the declared material, so the exemplar fails until all three screens are named. That is what makes the second inventory checked rather than decorative, and it is the finding that forced the scope change below. Status left identified — worth confirming the completeness wording actually closes the two-inventories objection rather than restating it.

The antecedent was undefined, and a numeric floor turns an explicit shape="none" from a vacuous pass into a mandatory error

FR-007 requires a feature with no visual surface to record that explicitly, and <spec-visual shape="none"> is that record — a first-class, tested case. Such an element is a design declaring its visual surface, and it names zero screens. The old wording made that harmless; a floor inverts it, and whether an honest explicit-none hard-fails the pre-commit gate would have depended entirely on how a rule author read the antecedent. T-001 recorded this exact ambiguity against an earlier round: "Nobody chose, and whoever writes the rule will."

Draft response — chosen, in the requirement. The antecedent is now shape="screens" explicitly, so an explicit no-surface declaration is outside the obligation by the requirement's own words rather than by a reading. This is the third round of this requirement and the second time the ambiguity has been raised; leaving it to the implementer twice was the actual defect.

The scope forbade the mechanism the delta requires, and defer-to=never foreclosed the completeness check permanently

§Scope refused "Reading the screens directory to enumerate them … defer-to=never" while FR-014 required every named screen to resolve "in the material the design points at" — and the only real declaration in the estate points at a directory, which FR-005 expressly permits. The refusal and the obligation could not both be honoured. The cost justification was also hollow: validate.ts already invokes materialiseVisualViews on every document carrying a declaration, which does that walk unconditionally in the same pass. Its real effect was to write defer-to=never against the only check that could ever compare the named set to the declared set.

Draft response — the refusal was wrong and is narrowed. What 094 §4 forbids is the directory being the authority: an undeclared file becoming part of the feature by existing. Reading that material to test a claim the author already made is a different act, and it is now in scope and stated in FR-014's own text. The two are distinguished in the requirement rather than left to the reader, since conflating them is what the first round got wrong.

A must-tier obligation now rests on an assumed fact whose escape hatch it removes, with no accepted risk recording who accepted the gap

KB-0001@2026-07-25 commits that "a must-tier decision may not rest on an assumed or unresolved-spike fact without an explicit <spec-risk status='accepted'> recording who accepted the gap and why." The live spec's §6 records exactly such a fact: "Authors know, at design time, which screens a feature touches. Where they do not, that is a genuine open question for that design rather than something this spec can resolve." The old FR-014 left that escape intact; a completeness floor at error severity removes it — an open question does not satisfy it.

Draft response — narrowed by the antecedent, and the remainder needs your acceptance. The shape="screens" gate absorbs most of it: an author who does not yet know the feature's screens should not be declaring that shape with a path, and shape="none" or an absent declaration both stay legal. What survives is the author who has declared the shape and genuinely cannot enumerate yet. That is a real if narrow gap, and the corpus discipline says it must be accepted by a named human rather than argued away — so this one is left for you to disposition as accepted rather than mitigated, unless you would rather the requirement carry an explicit provisional state.

7 · Open questions

None outstanding. Two judgments are recorded rather than left open: the referent is a name and not prose, because naming is checkable and "is this a real introduction" is not; and resolution reads the declared material rather than the directory, keeping the declaration authoritative even though the materialiser walks that directory for its own purposes under its own budget.
