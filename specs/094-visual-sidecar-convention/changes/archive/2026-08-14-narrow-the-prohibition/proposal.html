Narrow the prohibition

Applied

1 · Intent

An independent adversarial pass on the change that added FR-009 found that it contradicts FR-005 directly, and that both are must-tier.

FR-005: "…no check may depend on one being present, and its absence MUST NOT be reported." FR-009: "an absent render MUST be shown as not recorded rather than omitted." The spec asserts a prohibition and its negation, and an implementer must pick one.

The originating risk register raised FR-005 and dispositioned it mitigated because the prohibition was "restated inside the new requirement". What was restated is FR-005's first clause. The absence-reporting half is the one FR-009 crosses and was never addressed — and that change's scope declared "FR-005 left exactly as it is", which stated the error as a feature. A self-run pass graded its own mitigation without reading the sentence to the end.

FR-005's intent is right and survives untouched: a render is evidence, never a baseline, and no gate may demand one. Its reach was wider than that reason — it forbade all reporting, including a view telling a reader plainly that nothing has been captured, which costs a reader information and protects nothing.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • Narrowing FR-005's prohibition to what it protects: no finding, no gate.
  • Removing FR-009 from this spec, which is not where a requirement about the generated view belongs.

Out of scope

  • Re-authoring the render slot. It lands in 099, which owns the view and is still draft — so it takes an in-place edit rather than a second ceremony. defer-to=099-visual-embedded-view.
  • The inverted MUST construction, which appears in three places across two specs and is a house idiom rather than this requirement's defect. defer-to=TBD-negated-must-rule.

3 · Approach

Two moves, and the order is the argument. Narrow FR-005 first so the prohibition says what it was protecting — a validate finding, a gate, anything that makes a build fail or a project look non-conformant for lacking a picture. A view that tells a reader "not recorded" does none of that; it is the artifact being honest about its own contents, which is the opposite of the failure FR-005 exists to prevent.

Then remove FR-009 rather than repair it. Three things are wrong with it and only one is fixable here: it contradicts FR-005 (fixed by the narrowing), it inverts its own safety clause into the machine-readable channel, and it legislates a surface 099 owns — while this spec's own §4 says it "adds two locations and a policy about what occupies them" and defers what the files contain elsewhere. Repairing it in place would leave a view requirement filed under the directory-convention spec, which is where the next person will not look.

099 is draft. Under P-6 it takes an in-place edit with no change-management ceremony, so an accepted spec was re-opened through the heavy path to add a requirement that could have been authored there for free. Recording that, because the mistake is one of routing rather than of content.

4 · Deltas

What changes: the prohibition names what it protects. Reported becomes reported as a finding, so a gate still cannot demand a render and a view can still say plainly that none was captured.

A rendered image of a screen MUST be treated as evidence for a reader and MUST NOT be load-bearing: no check may depend on one being present, and its absence MUST NOT be reported as a finding or made to fail any gate.

Rationale

A design system whose validation demands screenshots turns every visual change into a re-capture chore, and the picture is not the authority — fidelity is measured against declared tokens, never by comparing images.

The original wording forbade reporting an absence at all, which is wider than that reason. It also forbade a generated view from telling a reader that nothing has been captured — which costs the reader information and protects nothing, since a rendered sentence cannot fail a build. Narrowed to the mechanisms that can: a finding, and a gate.

Found when a change added a requirement that negated this one and both were must-tier. The reach was wider than the reason, and nothing had needed the difference until something did.

Remove FR-009 from this spec.

Reason. Three defects, and the one that matters is placement. This spec adds two locations and a policy about what occupies them; the generated view belongs to 099, which already owns every neighbouring requirement and explicitly permits a later slice to contribute a section without amending it. Filing a view requirement here is where the next reader will not look. Alongside that: it contradicted FR-005 until the delta above, and its safety clause — "No check MUST depend on a render being present" — puts the negation outside the rule tag, so the machine-readable channel asserts the inverse of the sentence. It also said "for each state" while its own task said "once per screen", and only the requirement binds, so as applied it mandated exactly the per-state rendering its risk register was written to avoid.
Migration. Nothing implemented it — every folded task is unchecked, so no behaviour changes and no project is affected. The capability it was reaching for is real and is re-authored in 099, which is draft and takes the edit in place. The two deferrals that lived only inside its archived proposal, TBD-render-capture and TBD-import-renders, are folded into this spec's out-of-scope block by the tasks below so they stop being invisible to every backlog signal.

5 · Risk register

The findings this proposal corrects came from an independent critic. No second independent pass was run on the correction itself — context, not judgment — so the entries below are self-authored and carry the weaker confidence that implies. Said plainly, since mislabelling a self-run pass is one of the failures being corrected here.

Narrowing a prohibition is how a prohibition erodes

FR-005 said no reporting. It now says no finding and no gate. Every future proposal that wants to surface an absent render has a smaller step to argue for than the one just taken, and the requirement no longer refuses on its own terms — it refuses two named mechanisms and is silent about the next one.

Accepted, deliberately not called mitigated. There is no mechanism here that prevents the erosion; the requirement names two channels and a third could be invented. What is recorded instead is the reason — a render is evidence and fidelity is measured against tokens — so a future reader has the argument rather than only the rule. Naming this accepted rather than mitigated is the correction's own lesson applied to itself.

Removing it leaves the original gap open until 099 is edited

The finding that started this — nothing anywhere asks for a render, so no project has one — is not addressed by a removal. Between this apply and the 099 edit, the estate is back to where it was, with a card that reads closed.

Accepted. The window is short and the edit is in scope of the tasks below rather than deferred. The alternative — leaving a contradictory must-tier requirement in place because removing it briefly reopens a gap — trades a real defect for a bookkeeping comfort.

The 099 edit escapes review by being a draft edit

Moving the requirement to a draft spec means it lands with no proposal, no risk pass and no changelog ceremony — which is exactly the leniency P-6 grants a draft, and also how a requirement that just failed review lands unreviewed somewhere else.

Accepted, with the reason recorded. P-6's leniency is deliberate and 099 is genuinely draft. The mitigation is that the content is now known-bad in three specific ways, all written down above, so the re-authoring has a checklist rather than a memory. If 099 were accepted this would be a proposal instead.
