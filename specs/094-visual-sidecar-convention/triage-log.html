FR-009 contradicts FR-005 outright, and both are must-tier

Spec upstream

Debugging what a rule author should build, root cause was that the change landed a requirement whose central clause is the negation of a requirement already in the same document; fixed by narrowing FR-005's prohibition to what it was actually protecting; regeneration fails, because the spec now says both things and an implementer must pick one.

Expected
One position on whether a reader is told a render is absent.
Actual
Two, verbatim.

FR-005: "…no check may depend on one being present, and its absence MUST NOT be reported."

FR-009: "an absent render MUST be shown as not recorded rather than omitted."

Both must-tier. 099 restates the strong form independently, so the estate now asserts the prohibition twice and its negation once.

Diagnosis
The proposal raised a risk against FR-005 and dispositioned it mitigated on the ground that "the prohibition is restated inside the new requirement". What was restated is FR-005's first clause — the no-check-depends half. The absence-reporting half is the one FR-009 crosses and it is not addressed anywhere. The proposal's scope explicitly said "FR-005 left exactly as it is", which was the error stated as a feature: the change needed a MODIFY on FR-005 narrowing "reported" to mean a validate finding, and instead declared the requirement untouched. A self-run pass graded its own mitigation and did not read the sentence to the end.
Fix
specs/094-visual-sidecar-convention/spec.html — a propose. MODIFY FR-005 so the prohibition names what it protects (no validate finding, no gate) rather than all reporting, and MODIFY FR-009 to sit inside it. The intent behind FR-005 is right and survives; only its reach was wider than its reason.
Fixed
Applied 2026-08-14-narrow-the-prohibition. FR-005's prohibition now names what it protects — no finding, no gate — so the reach matches the reason and a view may still tell a reader nothing was captured. FR-009 was removed rather than repaired, because its real defect was placement.
Deep dive — cross-spec

099 FR-009 carries the strong form too, so the narrowing has to reach it or the contradiction just moves. Sequence the MODIFY on 094 first, since 099 is where the view actually lives and would otherwise be amended twice.

A prohibition written as a MUST, in the channel that exists to be machine-read

Spec upstream

Debugging why the safety clause reads oddly, root cause was that "No check MUST depend on a render being present" tags an absolute requirement onto the proposition it means to forbid; fixed by using the prohibition keyword; regeneration fails, because the construction is already in three places and reads as house style.

Expected
A prohibition uses MUST NOT. KB-0001@2026-07-25 commits the vocabulary, and the <spec-rule> wrapper exists precisely so a tool can find every must-tier requirement without parsing prose.
Actual
The negation sits in the subject, outside the tag. A reader parses it correctly; a machine reading <spec-rule> tags sees an absolute requirement asserted over "a check depends on a render being present" — the exact inverse. FR-005 gets the same constraint right one requirement earlier with "MUST NOT be load-bearing". One spec, one constraint, two conformance levels.
Diagnosis
Not a typo — 099 FR-006 and 099 FR-009 carry the identical construction. A spreading idiom, which is the argument for correcting it while it is in three places rather than thirty.
Fix
Fold into T-001's propose. Worth considering a check: a <spec-rule>MUST</spec-rule> preceded by a negating word in the same clause is mechanically detectable, and this is exactly the machine-readable channel P-8 says should carry the guarantee.
Fixed
The inverted construction is gone from the requirement that carried it, and its replacement in 099 FR-009 is written MUST NOT. The idiom survives elsewhere and is deferred as TBD-negated-must-rule — a <spec-rule>MUST</spec-rule> preceded by a negating word is mechanically detectable, which is the check worth having.
