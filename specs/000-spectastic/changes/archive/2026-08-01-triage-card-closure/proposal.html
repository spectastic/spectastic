Change proposal · 000-spectastic

Give a diagnostic triage card a closure state

A log where a shipped fix reads exactly like an untouched defect is not a record. It is a pile.

Status Applied Change ID 2026-08-01-triage-card-closure Spec 000-spectastic Source Triage T-021 — asked while reading 020's log: "are we correctly closing triaged issues?" The answer was no, and the reason was the format. Author Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Created 01 Aug 2026 Targets 1 added (REQ-CHANGE-009) Read time

TRIAGE-001…TRIAGE-006 mandate five fields — headline, Expected, Actual, Diagnosis, layer+regen, Fix — and no resolution state. Fix is a proposal, never a record that the proposal happened. REQ-CHANGE-005 introduced data-status for inbox cards only, and assets/spec.css agrees, commenting its own rule "for ticked-off just-do items". So a diagnostic card reads identically whether its fix shipped years ago or was never started.

The measured cost: 78 cards across 23 logs carry 19 markers. Every one of 020's 13 cards is fixed in code; 2 say so. This ADDs a requirement generalising the two existing markers to diagnostic cards — inventing no vocabulary, needing no CSS — and states the honest ceiling: no validate rule can know whether a fix shipped, so this is a discipline the verbs carry, not a gate.

Give a diagnostic triage card a closure state

Applied

1 · Intent

The triage format was designed for a card that reads in under 30 seconds and is written at the moment of diagnosis. That design succeeded at capture and never addressed what happens afterwards. A card records what was wrong and what should be done; nothing records that it was.

The consequence is not cosmetic. Four of 020's cards (T-005, T-006, T-007, T-011) say in their own Fix fields that they need a /spectastic.propose. All four proposals landed — REQ-AUTHOR-005 exists, the scanner knows verify, artifactPanelTitle exists, FR-015 exists — and all four cards still read as outstanding work. A reader planning what to do next would re-do finished work or, more likely, stop trusting the log.

Two authors already felt the gap and improvised around it: T-009 and T-010 reached for data-status="done" despite its just-do scoping, and invented an undocumented <dt>Fixed</dt> row. Improvisation converging on the same shape twice is the signal that the format owes it.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • ADD REQ-CHANGE-009: a diagnostic <spec-triage> card records its resolution, reusing the two data-status values REQ-CHANGE-005 already defines; the evidence row T-009/T-010 improvised is codified; the no-machine-check ceiling is stated.

Out of scope

  • A validate rule enforcing closure. Validate reads artifacts; it cannot know whether a fix shipped. Any such rule would either nag every open card or trust a marker it cannot verify — recorded as an honest ceiling rather than a gap awaiting work.
  • Sweeping the 59 unmarked cards across the other 22 logs. Mechanically trivial and epistemically not: marking a card done is a claim about the world, so each needs verifying against the code. Tracked separately so this requirement is not held hostage to an audit.
  • A distinct resolved vocabulary for diagnostic cards. Considered and rejected in §3 — two words for one idea.

3 · Approach

Reuse the markers; invent nothing. The tempting move is a diagnostic-specific vocabulary — a card is resolved, not ticked off. It is rejected here for three reasons: data-status="done" and "rejected" already render correctly on <spec-triage> (the CSS selectors are element-wide, not inbox-scoped); two authors independently reached for done on diagnostic cards before any requirement permitted it; and a second vocabulary would mean every consumer learns two spellings of one state. The change is therefore a widening of scope, not new machinery — no CSS, no element, no rule.

Codify the improvised evidence row. T-009 and T-010 both added <dt>Fixed</dt> recording what actually happened, and in both cases it diverged usefully from the proposed Fix — T-010's reads "Host-side only, as it turned out — spec.js already prefers data-doc-path". That divergence is the most valuable line on the card, and the format was discarding it. It becomes a SHOULD, not a MUST: requiring it would make closing a card whose fix went exactly as proposed more ceremonious than the fix itself.

State the ceiling plainly. There is no machine check here and there cannot be. validate parses artifacts and has no way to know whether a proposal landed or a line of code shipped. Per P-8, an unenforceable discipline must say so rather than imply a guarantee — so the requirement names the verbs that carry it and records that the gate does not exist.

4 · Deltas

What's added: a closure contract for diagnostic triage cards — the two existing data-status values generalised beyond inbox just-do cards, the improvised evidence row codified as a SHOULD, the owning verbs named, and the absence of a machine check recorded.

A <spec-triage> card in a per-spec triage log records whether it is still owed work. When a card's fix has landed, the card SHOULD carry data-status="done"; when a card is closed without a fix — not a defect, superseded, or deliberately declined — it SHOULD carry data-status="rejected" and a <dt>Rejected because</dt> row. These are the same two values and the same rendering REQ-CHANGE-005 defines for inbox cards; this requirement widens their scope to diagnostic cards rather than introducing a parallel vocabulary. A card carrying no data-status MUST be read as still open.

A card marked done SHOULD carry a <dt>Fixed</dt> row inside its <dl> recording what actually shipped, especially where that differed from the proposed Fix — the divergence is usually the most informative line on the card. The card is never rewritten to match the outcome: Fix stays as proposed and Fixed records reality, so the log preserves the reasoning as well as the result.

Closure is owed at the moment the fix lands. /spectastic.implement SHOULD mark the card when it drains work that resolves one, and /spectastic.apply SHOULD do so when the proposal a card called for is applied. Cards remain append-only and IDs stable (REQ-CHANGE-001): closing a card annotates it, never deletes or renumbers it.

No machine check exists, and none is proposed. validate reads artifacts and cannot know whether a proposal landed or a line of code shipped, so a rule could only nag on every open card or trust a marker it cannot verify. Per P-8 this is recorded as a review-caught discipline rather than dressed as a guarantee — the honest posture the fat-core/thin-CLI convention and the no-internal-id-in-copy ceiling already take.

Rationale

The format optimised for capture and never addressed the afterwards: the five Tier-1 fields (TRIAGE-001…TRIAGE-006) contain no resolution state, and Fix is a proposal, not a record that it happened. Measured at proposal time: 78 cards across 23 logs carried 19 markers; all 13 of 020's cards were fixed in code and 2 said so, including four whose own Fix fields called for proposals that had all long since landed. That is a log a reader cannot plan from. Reusing the two existing values rather than minting a resolved vocabulary follows the evidence — T-009 and T-010 reached for done on diagnostic cards before any requirement allowed it, and the CSS already renders it element-wide; a second spelling would cost every consumer and buy nothing. The evidence row is a SHOULD rather than a MUST so a fix that went exactly as proposed is not more ceremonious to close than to make. Priority should, matching REQ-CHANGE-005 / REQ-CHANGE-007: codifying from incident, re-evaluate once a few logs have been kept under it.

5 · Risk register

Auto-fired: an ADD delta on a lifecycle-format requirement. Self-reviewed against the draft; all three findings dispositioned inline.

A SHOULD with no machine check is precisely the advisory-prose gap P-8 and T-009 exist to warn about — 59 unmarked cards are the evidence that discipline alone does not hold, and this proposal answers with more discipline.

The spec's own §1 Context on 042: a promise that "lives in prose an agent can skip". The same critique lands here.

Mitigated as far as it honestly can be, and the residue is recorded rather than argued away. The requirement does not merely ask nicely: it names the two verbs that own the action at the moment the fix lands (implement, apply), which is where a durable implementation can later attach — the same path REQ-CHANGE-007 took when the §6 fold moved from prose into the kernel. What cannot be mitigated is stated outright in the requirement body: no validate rule can know a fix shipped. Claiming otherwise would be the overclaim P-8 actually forbids.

Widening data-status="done" from just-do cards to diagnostic cards silently changes what the marker means. On an inbox card it means "I did this small thing"; on a diagnostic card it would mean "a defect is resolved" — two different claims sharing one token, and REQ-CHANGE-005's own text ties it to just-do items.

REQ-CHANGE-005: "mirrors the existing data-status="done" convention for just-do cards"; assets/spec.css: "Done state — for ticked-off just-do items kept as history".

Mitigated by making the widening explicit in the requirement text rather than leaving it to be inferred — it states that these are REQ-CHANGE-005's values and that their scope is being widened, so a reader of either requirement finds the other. The underlying claim is the same in both cases ("this item is finished, retained as history"), which is why both surfaces render identically and why two authors reached for it unprompted. The §6 tasks include correcting the CSS comment's just-do-scoped wording so the code stops contradicting the contract.

"A card carrying no data-status MUST be read as still open" retroactively declares 59 existing cards open, most of which are in fact fixed — the requirement manufactures a false backlog the moment it lands, and the backfill that would correct it is explicitly out of scope.

§2 defers the sweep to TBD-triage-closure-backfill; the measured estate is 78 cards, 19 marked.

Accepted. The alternative — reading an unmarked card as ambiguous — is the status quo, and ambiguity is the defect. A default of "open" is the safe direction: it over-reports work rather than under-reporting it, and it makes the backlog visible enough to be worth clearing, which an ambiguous default never did. The author's decision on this pass is to clear the whole estate immediately, each card verified against the code before it is marked; the deferral exists so the contract is not blocked if that audit runs long.
