Codify the warm-tone dark-mode palette

Light mode is warm cream. Dark mode should be warm dark — never pure black, never pure grey.

Status Applied Change ID 2026-06-12-theme-pass Spec 000-spectastic v0.1 Targets 1 requirement (1 added) Author Brian Corbin · brian@example.com Created 12 Jun 2026 Read time

The light palette is intentionally warm — cream surface, warm dark text, crimson links. Dark mode currently mirrors the structure but the contract is informal. Inbox card I-003 flagged a too-sharp sidenote border as the entry point of a wider concern. Rather than fix one CSS value in isolation, this proposal codifies the principle: dark mode honors the warm-tone palette — surfaces and borders use warm dark greys, never pure black, never neutral grey. The apply lands one requirement (REQ-THEME-002) and tweaks the dark-mode --c-border token for slightly better surface-border contrast.

Codify the warm-tone dark-mode palette

Applied

1 · Intent

Inbox card I-003 was deferred to a TBD-theme-pass with the rationale "one of several dark-mode polish items; better batched into a single themed proposal." This is that proposal.

The deferred fix is small (warmer dark-grey for the sidenote border). The reason it's worth a proposal — rather than another just-do — is that the underlying principle is not yet codified. The light palette intentionally rejects pure white (cream #f6f5f1) and pure black (warm dark #353534); dark mode should follow the same discipline. Today it does, but only by author convention. One requirement makes it a contract.

2 · Scope

In scope

  • One ADD delta: REQ-THEME-002 codifying the warm-tone principle for the dark palette.
  • One CSS infrastructure edit on apply: nudge dark-mode --c-border from #38352f to #403d36 for better surface-border contrast (specifically where sidenote borders sit on the warm dark surface).
  • Close inbox card I-003.

Out of scope

  • Other dark-mode polish (focus rings, code-block contrast, accent saturation). Each gets its own card if and when it surfaces; the principle codified here governs all of them going forward.
  • Changing the light-mode palette. Light is the reference; dark conforms.
  • Adding a separate --c-border-dark token. The existing single --c-border token + the html.dark overrides remain the mechanism.

3 · Approach

One MUST-level addition that names two concrete constraints:

  1. Surfaces never pure black. Dark-mode background and surface tokens stay in the warm dark-grey range (informally: #1c–#28 brightness; warm hue temperature).
  2. Borders contrast against surface without resorting to pure grey. The border token sits 4–8 luminance steps above the surface token; never desaturated.

The apply ships an immediate honoring of (2): bump --c-border from #38352f (Δ20 above surface #24221f) to #403d36 (Δ28 above), giving the sidenote left-border a stronger visual edge without going cold.

Why MUST and not SHOULD: the principle should reject "let's just darken the border to pure grey" patches the next time someone reaches for one. SHOULD is too easy to wave away in code review; MUST is a brake.

4 · Deltas

Add a new requirement codifying the warm-tone palette principle for dark mode.

The dark-mode palette MUST honor the warm-tone principle established by the light palette. Concretely: surface and background tokens MUST use warm dark greys (warm hue, brightness in the #1c–#28 range) and never pure black; border tokens MUST sit 4–8 luminance steps above their adjacent surface token and never be desaturated neutrals.

Rationale

The light palette intentionally rejects pure white and pure black for the same reason: warmth reads as considered, neutral tones read as undesigned. Dark mode should follow the same discipline, not stumble into cold defaults when a fresh contributor tweaks a token.

6 · Open questions

  1. Range tolerance. The requirement names a #1c–#28 brightness range and a 4–8 step border delta. Those are picked from current values, not from systematic study. If a future contrast-audit surfaces edge cases, the range may need loosening or tightening — fine to leave the numbers as guidance and let the apply experience inform a follow-up.
