Design · 102-motion-choreography

Motion is a sequence

How we will build it.

Status Accepted Spec 102-motion-choreography Owner Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Branch spec/visual-slice-095-098 Created 13 Aug 2026 Read time

A choreography is an ordered set of steps sharing one origin, each naming an element and its offset. Offsets are measured from that origin and never from the previous step, so inserting a step cannot silently move everything after it. The reduced-motion record is the only required record in this family, and the exception is deliberate: elsewhere silence means nobody asked, which is tolerable for a coverage claim and not for an accessibility preference the platform already exposes.

6 · Approach

A choreography element holding ordered steps, each naming an element and an offset from the choreography's declared origin, optionally with a duration and easing that may be a token reference or a raw value. The reader is pure and per-file; the rule checks that an origin is declared, that a reduced-motion record exists, and that a step names an element the screen declares.

Two decisions carry the design. Offsets are relative to the origin rather than to the preceding step — relative-to-previous reads more naturally and makes every step depend on the one before it, so inserting a step silently moves everything after it, which is exactly the late edit nobody re-reviews. And the reduced-motion record is required, which is the only place this family compels an answer; it is justified rather than assumed, because a rule that looks like an inconsistency invites someone to "fix" it.

7 · Alternatives considered

What an offset is measured from. Scored 1–5 on whether an edit can silently change other steps, how it reads, and how easily a reviewer can spot two elements colliding.

OptionEdit-safeReads wellCollisions visibleTotal
Relative to the previous step1528
Explicit start and end per step42511
Relative to a shared origin — chosen54514

Relative-to-previous wins on readability alone and loses everywhere it matters. Inserting a step shifts every later one without any of them changing, and two elements that collide are only discoverable by adding the offsets up in your head.

8 · Decisions

D-001 · Offsets are measured from a shared origin, never from the previous step

Status
Accepted
Context
An entrance is several elements moving in a declared order. Both schemes express that; they differ in what an edit does. Relative-to-previous makes every step depend on its predecessor.
Decision
Every step's offset is from the choreography's declared origin. There is no relative form.
Consequences
+ Inserting a step changes nothing else, so the most common late edit is safe. + Two elements colliding is visible by reading two numbers rather than adding a column. − Shifting a whole sequence means editing every offset, which is the trade and is the rarer operation.

D-002 · The reduced-motion record is required — the family's only one

Status
Accepted
Context
Everywhere else in this family an absent record means not recorded — 093 FR-012 makes that explicit for coverage, and a sibling was corrected mid-proposal for reading absence as a positive claim. This breaks that pattern deliberately.
Decision
A choreography with no reduced-motion record is reported. Written down as an exception rather than left to look like an inconsistency somebody should tidy up.
Consequences
+ A sequence a user cannot escape is a defect for people who get motion sick, and the platform already exposes the preference — so silence here is not neutral in the way silence about coverage is. − It is the one place an author is compelled, so it is the record most likely to be satisfied with a token answer. − The check sees that a record exists and never that it says anything, which is the honest ceiling.

D-003 · A timing value may be a token reference or a raw value, and neither is resolved

Status
Accepted
Context
The token format carries duration and easing types, and the exemplar already declares motion.rate-refresh. But requiring a token would make the vocabulary unusable by a project that has not built its motion scale, which is most of them.
Decision
A step may reference a token or state a raw value. Neither is resolved at check time, on the same reasoning that keeps a design source and an annotation target unresolved.
Consequences
+ Usable on day one and better on day one hundred. + No cross-file read, so the rule stays pure. − A referenced token that does not exist is not caught here, which is the standing cost of every unresolved reference in this family.

11 · Complexity tracking

One required record in a family that otherwise never compels. The inconsistency is real and is the point: coverage silence is tolerable because nobody is harmed by an unanswered question, and motion silence is not. Written down here so a later reader finds the reasoning rather than the anomaly.

12 · Open questions

None outstanding. The two decisions that could have gone either way are recorded with their reasons: offsets are absolute because relative-to-previous makes an insertion silently move everything after it, and the reduced-motion record is required because an accessibility preference the platform exposes is not the kind of silence this family otherwise tolerates.
