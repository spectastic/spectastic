Corpus licensing

Committing a document in-repo makes its redistribution terms unavoidable — so make them visible, not silent.

Status Draft Spec ID 058-corpus-licensing Owner Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Reviewers — Created 25 Jul 2026 Principlesv1.8.0 Smallest demoableA corpus document with a restrictive license surfaces a redistribution warning on validate. Read time

Licensing becomes concrete the moment documents are committed. Vendored standards and paywalled material carry redistribution terms that an in-repo corpus makes unavoidable — the clearest cost of choosing in-repo over by-reference. This spec makes that cost visible rather than silent: every corpus document must declare a license (a missing one warns), and a license that restricts redistribution surfaces a corpus-license warning — so committing paywalled or vendored material is a decision on the record, exactly as the worked example's deliberately-restrictive KB-002 demonstrates. It carries a written redistribution policy naming by-reference (an MCP bridge, or origin-url only) as the escape hatch for material that cannot be redistributed. The rule flags a declared restrictive license; it does not adjudicate license compatibility — a recorded ceiling.

1 · Context

Choosing an in-repo corpus over a by-reference one buys durability — a cited source that still exists next year — but it carries a cost the by-reference approach doesn't: the moment a third-party document is committed, its redistribution terms apply to the repo. Vendored standards, paywalled regulatory text, licensed reference manuals — all carry terms that an in-repo copy makes unavoidable. The considerations doc lists this as a ceiling; this spec turns it into a visible signal so the cost is a decision on the record, not a silent liability.

The worked example already anticipates it: KB-002 deliberately carries a restrictive license so the corpus surfaces a redistribution question rather than hiding it. This spec generalises that — every document declares a license, and a restrictive one warns — and pairs the signal with a written policy that names the escape hatch (bridge by-reference) for material that genuinely cannot be committed.

2 · User scenarios & testing

Each story MUST be independently testable.

US1 · A restrictive license is loud P1

As a maintainer, I want a restrictive license on a committed corpus document to surface a warning so that redistribution exposure is a visible decision, not something discovered later.

Acceptance: a document whose license restricts redistribution produces a corpus-license warning; a permissive one produces none.

US2 · Every document declares a license P2

As a legal reviewer, I want every corpus document to declare a license so that redistribution terms are auditable across the whole corpus.

Acceptance: a document with no license field warns; the corpus makes the licensing state of every document explicit.

US3 · A written redistribution policy with an escape hatch P3

As a project, I want a written redistribution policy that names by-reference as the escape hatch so that material that cannot be committed has a sanctioned path (bridge live via MCP, or keep only the origin-url).

Acceptance: the convention carries a policy stating what may be committed and directing non-redistributable material to a by-reference bridge.

Edge cases

  • An illustrative-excerpt document (hand-written, status: illustrative-excerpt) paraphrasing public facts — a permissive/own license; no redistribution concern.
  • A license the rule doesn't recognise — treated conservatively (warn to declare intent), never silently passed as permissive.
  • Two documents in one pack with different licenses — each judged on its own declared license (FR-001).

3 · Requirements

Conformance keywords (MUST, SHOULD, MAY) follow RFC 2119.

Functional

Every corpus document MUST declare a license in its provenance frontmatter; a missing license MUST warn.

A corpus document whose license restricts redistribution MUST surface a corpus-license validate finding at warning severity, so committing paywalled or vendored material is a visible decision, not a silent one.

Rationale

A warning, not an error: whether to commit restricted material is a project's call to make with the fact in front of it. The rule's job is to make the fact unavoidable, not to block the decision.

The convention SHOULD carry a written redistribution policy stating what may be committed in-repo and what must be held by reference.

The policy SHOULD name by-reference — an MCP bridge, or keeping only the origin-url — as the sanctioned escape hatch for material that cannot be redistributed in-repo.

Rationale

The bridge tier trades durability for legality: the cited source lives outside the repo (so it can't be frozen or gated), but it stays usable for freshness-critical or non-redistributable material.

Non-functional

The corpus-license rule MUST flag a declared restrictive license only; it MUST NOT claim to adjudicate license compatibility or legal effect. This ceiling MUST be recorded, not hidden.

Out of scope (deferred to other slices)

  • The license provenance field this rule reads.
  • The provenance/staleness gates — sibling validate rules with their own spec.
  • Legal adjudication of license compatibility — outside the tool's competence and deliberately not attempted.

4 · Data model

The entities this feature owns or substantially changes.

Entities

License declaration
The license field of a document's provenance (051). Classified for this rule as permissive, restrictive, or undeclared.
Redistribution policy
A written convention document: what may be committed in-repo, what must be by-reference, and the sanctioned bridge for the latter.
License finding
A corpus-license validate result. Fields: the document, the declared license (or its absence), severity warning.

Invariant: the rule reads only the declared license and reports; it never infers a license from document content, and never asserts legal compatibility. Declaration is the user's responsibility; visibility is the tool's.

5 · Success criteria

Technology-agnostic, measurable outcomes.

A corpus document declaring a restrictive license produces a corpus-license warning; a document declaring a permissive license produces none.

A corpus document with no license field produces a warning.

The convention ships a written redistribution policy that a reviewer can read to decide whether a given document may be committed or must be bridged by reference.

6 · Assumptions

Licenses can be classified permissive vs restrictive by a maintained list of identifiers (SPDX-style), sufficient for a warn-to-surface signal without full legal analysis.

Projects that need non-redistributable material accept the by-reference trade (the cited source lives outside the repo and cannot be frozen or gated) as the price of legality.
