Specification · 099-visual-embedded-view

The visual view, inside the design

The declaration points at files and the sidecars hold them; nothing yet turns the two into something a person reads.

Status Accepted Spec ID 099-visual-embedded-view Owner Brian Corbin · @briancorbinxyz Reviewers — Created 13 Aug 2026 Principlesv1.9.0 Smallest demoableRunning the design verb on a feature with a declared surface leaves a table of that feature's screens and states inside design.html, and changing a screen without regenerating reports it. The visual chassis has three legs and two were built. 093 declares where the material lives, 094 gives it a home, and 095–098 say what it contains — but nothing assembles any of it into something a reader can look at. This is the third leg, built where 072 built the contract's: a view materialised into the design at generate time and checked against the files it projects, plus the one element the sidecars still lack — a render, bound to the cell of the grid it is evidence of.

1 · Context

The survey that produced this family chose one of four places a visual spec could live: a declaration in the design, a sidecar holding the real thing, and a view materialised from both and checked against them. Its suggested-next-artifacts list then named the declaration, the sidecar, the vocabulary, the grid, the components and the versioning — and never the view. Six specs drained that list faithfully, so the omission propagated: a feature's screens, states, annotations, grid and token version sit in three files a reader must open one at a time, and nothing brings them together.

The gap was found by an artifact left behind: a worked example carried a hand-written visual.html whose README argued it was the missing leg — a separate file. That inverts the decision it implements. 072 puts the contract's view inside design.html, and the survey says "an embedded view", "any rendering inside an artifact". A ninth artifact to avoid a ninth verb is not the trade that was made.

Two readers want different things: a developer wants the surface without a design-tool licence, a reviewer wants to know it is still true. The second is what makes the drift check load-bearing rather than decorative — the half a hand-written file can never have.

2 · User scenarios & testing

Each story MUST be independently testable: if you implemented only US1, you would still have a viable MVP.

US1 · A design shows the surface it declares P1

As a developer picking up a feature, I want to see its screens, their states and the contexts it addresses in the design I am already reading so that I do not have to open three sidecars and hold them in my head to know what I am building.

Acceptance: A design declaring a surface carries a view after generation and names every screen and state its sidecar contains; a design declaring none carries nothing.

US2 · A stale view says so P1

As a reviewer, I want to be told when the view no longer matches the files it projects so that I never read a copy as though it were current.

Acceptance: Adding a state without regenerating reports one finding naming it; regenerating clears it; an unchanged sidecar reports nothing.

US3 · A render is evidence a person can look at P2

As a designer or a reviewer, I want to see a captured image of a screen beside the cell of the grid it is evidence of so that the claim that a state was designed is checkable by eye as well as by name.

Acceptance: A declared render appears in the view labelled with what it is evidence of; deleting every render produces zero findings.

Edge cases

  • A declared sidecar that is absent, unreadable, or not the vocabulary — the view is omitted rather than rendered empty, and the resolve check that already exists reports the path.
  • A design authored by copying the template and interviewing, which is how designs are actually written and the path the contract view never reached.
  • A sidecar large enough that projecting it whole would dominate the artifact.
  • A render referenced from a project that has never committed one — the reference is inert, not an error.
  • A design regenerated twice with nothing changed, which must produce identical bytes.

3 · Requirements

Conformance keywords (MUST, SHOULD, MAY) follow RFC 2119.

Functional

The design artifact MUST be able to render the visual material a feature declares as a view materialised at generate time, inside the design itself rather than as a separate artifact.

Rationale

The content policy forbids loading it at view time, so materialisation is the only mechanism available. Inside the design because that is the option the survey chose and 072 built — a separate file reinstates by the back door the ninth artifact "no ninth verb" refused.

The view MUST be a derived projection of the declared files — their screens, states, annotations, contexts and token-set version — and MUST NOT copy them verbatim.

Rationale

The one place this must not clone the contract view. A contract file is opaque text, so an escaped excerpt is all that is available; the visual sidecars are already this tool's vocabulary, and a table serves a reader better than three files' markup pasted into a fourth — which would roughly double a typical design.

Materialisation MUST be reachable from every path by which a design is authored, not only the programmatic one.

Rationale

Paid for by 072's T-001: that view is wired into the kernel verb alone, while the canonical path copies a template and interviews — so a design declaring a contract has never once received one, and no check fired because an absent view is legal there.

A view that no longer matches the files it projects MUST be reported, compared against those files as they are rather than a stored digest.

Rationale

A materialised copy is a drift problem by construction, which is what makes the check necessary rather than optional. Live comparison follows verify-view-stale: a stored fingerprint is a third thing to keep true, and it goes stale silently.

Where a design declares a surface, an absent view MUST be reported rather than permitted.

Rationale

The deliberate divergence from 072 FR-008, whose MAY is why nobody noticed for months that the contract view never rendered. A capability whose absence is legal and unreported is indistinguishable from one that does not work. Conditional on a declaration, so a project with no surface reports nothing.

The view MUST NOT be a source of truth: no check MUST read it as the declaration, and editing it MUST NOT change what it projects.

Regenerating a view over unchanged inputs MUST replace any existing one rather than adding a second, and MUST produce identical output.

Rationale

If regeneration could differ from itself, a reported difference would not distinguish a stale view from a noisy generator.

A rendered image MUST be declarable as evidence of one combination — a screen, its state, and the contexts it was captured in — and MUST name a file rather than embed one.

Rationale

Renders are permitted in the sidecar and nothing binds one to what it is evidence of, which makes a captured image an unlabelled picture. Named rather than embedded because a same-origin reference has always been permitted while an inline data URI is an error — so an image needs no copy, has no drift surface, and costs no size.

A check MUST NOT depend on a render being present, and a render's absence MUST NOT be reported as a finding or made to fail any gate.

Rationale

A render is evidence for a reader, never a baseline — fidelity is measured against declared tokens, not by comparing pictures — so a validation that demands one turns every visual change into a re-capture chore.

Rewritten twice over. The prohibition was previously written as "No check MUST depend…", which puts the negation in the subject and outside the rule tag, so a tool reading those tags saw an absolute requirement asserted over the proposition it meant to forbid. And "absence MUST NOT be reported" was wider than its reason: it forbade this view from telling a reader plainly that nothing has been captured, which costs information and prevents nothing, since a rendered sentence cannot fail a build. Narrowed here to match 094 FR-005, so the two specs say one thing.

The view MUST show, for each screen it projects, whether any render of that screen has been recorded, and MUST NOT report that absence per state.

Rationale

The capability exists and no project has ever used it: a first real downstream project reached eleven declared states with no render, because nothing anywhere said the word. Never required had been built as never mentioned, and an unfilled slot is visible where an absent concept is not.

Per screen, and explicitly not per state, which is the whole of the second clause. This estate has paid twice for the other choice: the verify view's required toggle field reads as the literal string none in 50 of 61 views, and the observables trace produced 77 gap rows across 37 artifacts at a 100% gap rate — diagnosed as the question being wrong for the population rather than as a backlog. Eleven "not recorded" cells on one screen would reproduce both. A screen says it once.

Authored here rather than in the sidecar convention, which owns two directories and a policy about what occupies them. A requirement about this view belongs to the spec that owns this view — the first attempt filed it next door, where the next reader would not look.

The view SHOULD degrade honestly: omitted rather than empty where a declared file cannot be read, and bounded-and-labelled-as-such where the material exceeds its capacity.

Rationale

Both halves are the same rule — a view says what it can show and never implies it showed everything. An empty box reads as "there is nothing there", which is a different and false claim.

A later slice MAY contribute a further section to the view without amending this spec, and that section MAY carry its own drift check.

Rationale

The observables trace joined the verify view without reopening the spec that built it, and five siblings are queued to write into this one. Naming the seam now is cheaper than five amendments.

Non-functional

The view MUST render with scripting disabled: 100% of the projected content readable with JavaScript off.

Rationale

P-4 — progressive enhancement, never dependence. A screen inventory behind a script is worse than no view.

The view MUST NOT introduce executable content or a runtime fetch: at most 0 scripts, event-handler attributes, inline frames or external requests.

Rationale

P-11 — artifacts are data, not instructions. A same-origin reference is permitted; an inline frame and a data URI are not, so an embedded live surface cannot be built here at all.

Materialising a view SHOULD add under 100 ms to a design generation, for a feature declaring up to 20 screens.

Out of scope (deferred to other slices)

  • Reading any of this to check a build against it. The view shows what was declared and measures nothing.
  • A render as a ratified baseline, compared against a current capture. This spec says what a render is evidence of and stops.
  • Where a screen sits relative to other screens.
  • A component's own states, as distinct from a screen's.
  • Motion as a sequence rather than a token.
  • Copy budgets, and the strings a product refuses to ship.
  • What a tracking annotation should contain.
  • Landing any of this from a design tool.

4 · Data model

No persisted entity. The view is derived, and the one genuinely new thing is a render's binding to what it is evidence of.

Entities

Materialised view
A projection of declared visual material into the design that declares it. Derived, never authored, never read as a source. At most one per declaration, and regenerating replaces rather than appends. It has no identity of its own — a view is only ever of something.
Render
A committed image, named rather than embedded, bound to the screen, state and contexts it is evidence of. Any number per screen, including none, and its absence is never a gap.

The invariant worth stating: a view and the files it projects can only agree or disagree. There is no third state, because a view that cannot be built is not written rather than written empty — which is what lets the check be a straight comparison and an absent view mean exactly one thing.

5 · Success criteria

Technology-agnostic, measurable outcomes. These are how we know it worked.

For every design declaring a visual surface, a reader can name that feature's screens and states from the design alone, opening 0 other files.

Changing a declared sidecar without regenerating produces at least 1 finding naming what changed; regenerating produces exactly 0. Designs declaring no surface produce 0 in both cases.

Deleting every render from a project produces 0 findings about their absence, and the design still generates.

Across both authoring paths, designs that declare a surface and carry no view number 0.

Generating twice over unchanged inputs produces byte-identical designs, over at least 2 runs.

6 · Assumptions

A reader would rather see a derived table than the sidecars' own markup projected verbatim. Assumed, not tested — nobody has read a visual view of either kind. If it is wrong, FR-002 moves.

The two authoring paths stay two. If a third appears, FR-003 holds only if materialisation lives somewhere all three reach — an argument for a single entry point rather than a step added per path.

Projects will commit renders once something displays them. They have not so far, which is unsurprising given nothing did. If they still do not, nothing breaks — FR-009 makes a render evidence rather than a dependency.

7 · Open questions

None outstanding. The two decisions that could have been left open were settled by the sibling's triage card rather than deferred: an absent view is reported here (FR-005) where the contract view permits one, and materialisation must reach every authoring path (FR-003) rather than only the programmatic one. Both are exactly where 072's T-001 shows a clone would have gone wrong, so inheriting them silently was the one outcome worth avoiding.
